Level 10 · Research Systems, MLOps and Scaling

Phase: Scaled Systems, Trustworthy AI & Alignment
Suggested duration: 30 sessions (~7 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 9
Scope: Move from notebooks to reliable research systems that can run, scale, observe, and be reproduced by a team.

M10.1 · Packaging, Testing and Data Pipelines — (sessions: 5)
- Package a research project with locked dependencies, configuration, unit tests, data checks, and continuous integration.
- Separate exploration, reusable library code, training, evaluation, and reporting.
- Make pipelines resumable, cacheable, observable, and safe to rerun.
- Automate experiment configuration, artifact lineage, checkpointing, monitoring, and recovery across local and cluster runs.
- Report compute budget, hardware utilization, wall-clock time, energy or cost assumptions, and scaling bottlenecks.
Mental model: Research software is an instrument whose failure modes must be tested like a laboratory device.
Try: Convert a notebook experiment into a tested command-line pipeline with one-command reproduction.
Outcome: You can turn exploratory code into maintainable research software.

M10.2 · Experiment Operations and Model Serving — (sessions: 5)
- Track datasets, configurations, runs, artifacts, checkpoints, and lineage in an experiment registry.
- Measure latency, throughput, memory, cost, and reliability for batch and online inference.
- Add data validation, model monitoring, rollback, and incident documentation.
- Automate experiment configuration, artifact lineage, checkpointing, monitoring, and recovery across local and cluster runs.
- Report compute budget, hardware utilization, wall-clock time, energy or cost assumptions, and scaling bottlenecks.
Mental model: A model is only useful when its behavior remains observable after it leaves the notebook.
Try: Serve a small model behind an API and measure performance, failure responses, and resource use.
Outcome: You can operate a model experiment and report its engineering as well as scientific performance.

M10.3 · GPUs, Distributed Training and Scaling Laws — (sessions: 5)
- Profile input pipelines, memory, communication, utilization, and numerical precision.
- Explain data parallelism, gradient accumulation, checkpointing, and distributed failure recovery.
- Design scaling experiments that vary data, parameters, compute, and context without confounding them.
- Automate experiment configuration, artifact lineage, checkpointing, monitoring, and recovery across local and cluster runs.
- Report compute budget, hardware utilization, wall-clock time, energy or cost assumptions, and scaling bottlenecks.
Mental model: Scaling is a measured systems experiment, not simply adding more hardware.
Try: Run a controlled single-GPU and multi-GPU comparison and publish throughput, cost, and quality curves.
Outcome: You can scale an experiment while preserving evidence about what changed and why.

M10.4 · Cluster Scheduling and Distributed Experiment Design — (sessions: 3)
- Use queues, resource requests, checkpointing, retries, and artifact stores on shared compute.
- Separate data, model, and parallelism decisions and measure communication and utilization overhead.
Mental model: Scale increases the cost of ambiguity, so operational design is part of experimental design.
Try: Run a small parameter sweep with resumable jobs and a summary of failed and completed trials.
Outcome: You can run experiments efficiently without losing provenance.

M10.5 · Compute, Energy and Research Operations — (sessions: 3)
- Report hardware, wall-clock time, memory, energy or cost assumptions, and carbon or access constraints.
- Build dashboards for run status, data lineage, metrics, alerts, and reproducibility checks.
Mental model: A research system should make expensive evidence easier to inspect, not merely faster to produce.
Try: Create an experiment operations review that identifies one reliability and one efficiency improvement.
Outcome: You can operate a research platform with transparent resource trade-offs.

M10.6 · Capstone: Reproducible Research Platform — (sessions: 9)
- Build a template that provisions an environment, validates data, launches a tracked run, evaluates it, and exports a report.
- Add failure recovery, resource limits, tests, and documentation for a new collaborator.
- Demonstrate reproduction on a second machine or clean environment.
Final result: A dependable research platform that supports collaboration, scaling, and artifact evaluation.
