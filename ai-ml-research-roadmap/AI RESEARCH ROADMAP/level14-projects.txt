Level 14 · Comprehensive Research Capstones

Phase: Frontier Robustness & Thesis Projects
Suggested duration: 25 sessions (~6 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 4 (Datasets & Domain Exploration), Level 5 (Model Architectures), Level 6 (Sequence Models & GNNs), Level 7 (Generative Models), Level 8 (Benchmarking), Level 9 (HPC Systems), Level 11 (Interpretability), Level 12 (Robustness & Alignment)
Scope: Execute seven end-to-end, thesis-grade research capstones embodying the complete lifecycle of Bachelor's honors theses, Master's theses, and foundational PhD dissertation chapters across high-impact Computer Science research domains: medical healthcare AI, cybersecurity graph intelligence, decontaminated domain benchmarks, mechanistic circuit analysis, privacy-preserving generative diffusion, adversarial malware defense, and publication-ready conference submission artifacts.

P14.1 · Flagship Project 1: Medical Image Segmentation & Volumetric Biomarker Discovery — (sessions: 3)
- Select a competitive 3D volumetric medical imaging benchmark from the Medical Segmentation Decathlon or BraTS (Brain Tumor Segmentation).
- Implement a modern volumetric deep architecture: 3D U-Net or Swin-UNETR with hierarchical shifted-window attention from scratch in PyTorch.
- Formulate specialized volumetric loss functions: Generalized Soft Dice Loss combined with Focal Tversky Loss to handle severe foreground-background voxel imbalance.
- Execute full 5-fold cross-validation partitioned strictly by patient ID, calculating Dice Similarity Coefficients (DSC) and 95th percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95).
- Conduct an exhaustive ablation study evaluating the quantitative impact of deep supervision, data augmentations, and skip-connection gating.
- Author an empirical research report featuring multi-planar orthogonal slice visualizations (axial, coronal, sagittal) and statistical significance testing against baselines.
Outcome: A scientifically verified 3D medical image segmentation research codebase and clinical biomarker evaluation report meeting peer-reviewed medical imaging standards.

P14.2 · Flagship Project 2: Cybersecurity Threat Graph Intrusion Detection & Botnet Investigation — (sessions: 4)
- Transform raw network flow captures (CIC-IDS-2017) and enterprise host provenance logs into large-scale, directed heterogeneous property graphs using PyTorch Geometric.
- Architect an inductive Graph Neural Network (GAT with edge features or Temporal Graph Network) capable of modeling dynamic enterprise network topologies.
- Design spatial message-passing layers to detect multi-stage attack campaigns: port scanning, brute-force authentication, privilege escalation, and lateral movement.
- Evaluate detection performance under extreme class imbalance (< 0.01% attack edges), computing AUROC, Average Precision (AUPRC), and False Alarm Rate (FAR).
- Benchmark against classical tree ensembles (XGBoost) and recurrent sequence models, proving that graph structural context resolves ambiguous individual flows.
- Profile runtime computational efficiency on an HPC GPU cluster: measuring memory scaling, node sampling throughput, and real-time inference latency.
Outcome: A novel graph-based cybersecurity threat detection framework that identifies coordinated attack campaigns across complex enterprise network topologies.

P14.3 · Flagship Project 3: Decontaminated Domain-Specific Foundation Model Benchmark — (sessions: 4)
- Curate a novel, multi-task domain-specific evaluation benchmark dataset comprising 500+ challenging multi-step reasoning problems in clinical medicine or cybersecurity.
- Author a formal annotation codebook, conduct multi-annotator expert review with clinicians or security analysts, and verify inter-rater reliability (Krippendorff’s alpha > 0.80).
- Build an automated 13-gram contamination detection scanner using suffix arrays to verify zero overlap with standard open-source pre-training text corpora.
- Evaluate a comprehensive suite of open-weight (Llama, Mistral, Qwen) and proprietary foundation models under standardized zero-shot and few-shot Chain-of-Thought conditions.
- Implement an automated LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline, calibrating automated evaluation scores against human expert consensus via Kendall’s tau and Spearman rank correlation.
- Format all dataset artifacts with formal Datasheets for Datasets documentation and release clean evaluation harnesses on Hugging Face Datasets.
Outcome: A pristine, decontaminated domain evaluation benchmark established as an open-source standard for assessing domain-specific foundation model reasoning.

P14.4 · Flagship Project 4: Mechanistic Circuit Discovery in Clinical Decision Networks — (sessions: 3)
- Select a critical clinical reasoning capability in an open-weight foundation model (e.g., differential diagnosis, contraindication detection, or dosage calculation).
- Apply causal activation patching across all layers and token positions to map the exact minimal computational circuit mediating the target clinical capability.
- Deconstruct mediating attention heads into mathematically formulated QK addressing circuits and OV information-routing circuits.
- Train an overcomplete Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) with TopK/L1 sparsity penalties on the critical intermediate layer, extracting monosemantic medical symptom features.
- Extract contrastive activation steering vectors and demonstrate targeted behavioral steering during generation without increasing general language perplexity.
- Author an interactive research report featuring causal circuit wiring diagrams, activation heatmaps, and mechanistic ablation proofs.
Outcome: An end-to-end mechanistic interpretability discovery identifying the internal computational circuit and demonstrating precise latent steering in clinical AI.

P14.5 · Flagship Project 5: Privacy-Preserving Synthetic Clinical Cohort Generation — (sessions: 4)
- Formulate and implement a continuous-time Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) generative model on longitudinal tabular clinical records (MIMIC-IV).
- Train an adaptive velocity backbone with classifier-free guidance, synthesizing diverse patient cohorts conditioned on diagnosis codes and mortality outcomes.
- Construct straight-line probability paths between standard Gaussian priors and complex empirical clinical feature manifolds.
- Implement accelerated numerical ODE solvers (midpoint/Euler) achieving high-fidelity generation in 15 to 25 deterministic steps.
- Evaluate downstream empirical utility via Train on Synthetic, Test on Real (TSTR) protocols, demonstrating competitive predictive utility compared to real cohorts.
- Execute an empirical membership inference attack (MIA) audit, proving differential privacy guarantees and zero training patient data regurgitation.
Outcome: A state-of-the-art continuous flow matching framework generating privacy-preserving synthetic patient cohorts with proven clinical utility and privacy guarantees.

P14.6 · Flagship Project 6: Adversarial Malware Evasion & Certified Defense — (sessions: 3)
- Construct problem-space adversarial attacks on Windows Portable Executable (PE) malware binaries from the Ember and SOREL-20M benchmarks.
- Implement functionality-preserving structural manipulations: injecting adversarial bytes into slack spaces, section header manipulation, and benign import injection.
- Evade multiple state-of-the-art malware detectors (gradient boosted trees and deep byte-level CNNs), reducing detection rates from 98% to under 15%.
- Formulate a robust adversarial training defense: retraining models on dynamically generated problem-space adversarial variants.
- Evaluate the trade-off frontier: plotting clean accuracy on benign executables against robust accuracy under aggressive adversarial evasion attempts.
- Compute certified robustness bounds via randomized smoothing, demonstrating mathematical limits of adversary evasion capabilities.
Outcome: A rigorous problem-space adversarial attack and defense framework published with binary manipulation algorithms and empirical robustness proofs.

P14.7 · Flagship Project 7: End-to-End Conference Submission: Paper, Codebase & Data Release — (sessions: 4)
- Synthesize empirical discoveries from one of the preceding flagship research projects into a full 9-page conference paper manuscript in LaTeX (NeurIPS / ICML / USENIX Security format).
- Draft all standard academic sections: Abstract, Introduction, Related Work Taxonomy, Mathematical Methodology, Experimental Setup, Results, Ablations, and Limitations.
- Design publication-quality vector figures (using TikZ, Matplotlib/Seaborn science themes, and Illustrator) depicting architecture, scaling curves, and interpretability circuits.
- Package the complete research codebase into an open-source GitHub repository with Dockerized environment manifests, pre-trained weights, and automated unit tests.
- Draft an exhaustive reproducibility checklist detailing compute resources, random seeds, hyperparameter search grids, and negative findings.
- Assemble a complete conference submission package ready for submission to OpenReview with anonymized review-ready artifacts.
Outcome: A complete, publication-grade academic conference submission package ready for submission to a premier peer-reviewed AI venue or inclusion as a thesis chapter.
