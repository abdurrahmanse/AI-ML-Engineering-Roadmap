Level 4 · Dataset Discovery, Curation & Multi-Domain Adaptation

Phase: Datasets & Domain Exploration
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 3 (Research Mathematics & Applied Statistical Testing)
Scope: Master the discovery, ingestion, ethical curation, and preprocessing of real-world datasets across premier Computer Science research domains: medical/healthcare (MIMIC-IV EHR, CheXpert chest X-rays, TCGA genomic profiles), cybersecurity (CIC-IDS-2017 network traffic, Ember PE malware binaries, NSL-KDD), and scientific data; handling severe class imbalance, high missingness, patient/user privacy compliance (HIPAA, GDPR, de-identification), temporal data leakage prevention, and authoring formal "Datasheets for Datasets".

M4.1 · Medical & Healthcare Datasets: MIMIC-IV, CheXpert & TCGA — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Clinical Records & Patient Registries (MIMIC-IV & PhysioNet)
- Complete human subjects research ethics training (CITI Program Data or Specimens Only Research) to secure credentialed access to PhysioNet and MIMIC-IV.
- Ingest and normalize relational electronic health records (EHR): ICD-9/10 diagnosis codes, clinical lab events, prescription tables, and ICU vital signs.
- Convert longitudinal patient admissions into standardized temporal event streams using medical ontology mappings (SNOMED-CT, RxNorm, LOINC).
Session 2 · Volumetric Imaging & Genomic Data (CheXpert & TCGA)
- Process standard medical imaging formats (DICOM, NIfTI) for 2D chest radiographs (CheXpert, NIH ChestX-ray14) and 3D volumetric CT/MRI scans.
- Handle multi-label clinical radiology report uncertainty annotations (positive, negative, uncertain, unmentioned) via CheXpert labelers.
- Ingest The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) multimodal cohorts: aligning bulk RNA-seq gene expression profiles with clinical survival endpoints.
Mental model: Medical data represents human lives; strict privacy protocols and ontology alignment are essential before applying any machine learning model.
Try: Download a de-identified sample of MIMIC-IV clinical events, map ICD-10 diagnostic codes to high-level clinical categories, and construct patient admission timelines.
Outcome: Acquire credentialed access to leading medical research databases and preprocess heterogeneous clinical, radiological, and genomic data.

M4.2 · Cybersecurity & Threat Intelligence Datasets: CIC-IDS, Ember & PCAPs — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Network Intrusion & Packet Telemetry (CIC-IDS & NSL-KDD)
- Parse raw network packet captures (PCAP) and bidirectional NetFlow / IPFIX flow records using Wireshark, Zeek, and Argus.
- Ingest modern intrusion detection datasets (CIC-IDS-2017, UNSW-NB15), analyzing simulated multi-stage attack scenarios (DDoS, PortScan, Infiltration, Brute Force).
- Extract statistical flow features: inter-arrival packet times, flow duration, byte volume ratios, and TCP flag anomalies.
Session 2 · Malware Binaries & Threat Intelligence (Ember & SOREL-20M)
- Ingest the Ember (Elastic Malware Benchmark for Empowering Researchers) and SOREL-20M benchmark datasets for Windows Portable Executable (PE) files.
- Extract static malware features: section table characteristics, imported DLL function calls, byte entropy histograms, and string metadata.
- Account for rapid concept drift in cybersecurity caused by evolving malware polymorphism and zero-day exploit campaigns over time.
Mental model: In cybersecurity research, adversaries actively adapt to evade detection; static benchmark distributions decay rapidly across temporal horizons.
Try: Parse a raw PCAP file using Python Scapy, extract bidirectional flow statistics for benign and malicious traffic, and format feature vectors for ML ingestion.
Outcome: Process raw network packet captures and binary malware datasets, extracting domain-specific features for intrusion detection research.

M4.3 · Handling Severe Class Imbalance & Real-World Missingness — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Addressing Extreme Class Imbalance
- Understand the extreme rarity problem in medical pathology (rare diseases < 0.1%) and cybersecurity intrusion logs (attacks < 0.01% of total traffic).
- Compare sampling strategies: random minority over-sampling, Tomek link under-sampling, and synthetic oversampling via SMOTE and Borderline-SMOTE.
- Implement cost-sensitive learning and loss-function re-weighting: class-balanced focal loss, weighted cross-entropy, and LDAM (Label-Distribution-Aware Margin).
Session 2 · Clinical & Telemetry Missingness Mechanisms
- Formalize missingness taxonomies: Missing Completely at Random (MCAR), Missing at Random (MAR), and Missing Not At Random (MNAR).
- Differentiate between informative clinical missingness (the absence of a lab test implies normal health) and sensor dropouts.
- Apply advanced imputation: Multivariate Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), and temporal forward-filling with missingness indicator masks.
Mental model: The fact that a clinical test or network sensor log was NOT recorded is itself a powerful predictive signal that must not be discarded.
Try: Take an EHR dataset with 30% missing lab values, introduce missingness indicator masks, and compare MICE imputation against forward-fill baselines.
Outcome: Implement robust strategies to overcome extreme class imbalance and missing data without introducing distribution bias.

M4.4 · Preventing Subtle Data Leakage & Temporal Contamination — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Patient-Level & Entity-Level Leakage Prevention
- Identify catastrophic entity leakage: splitting multiple chest X-rays or ECG strips from the same patient across both training and test sets.
- Implement strict GroupKFold cross-validation partitioning data strictly at the patient ID or enterprise host level.
- Detect feature engineering leakage: fitting scalers, imputers, or dimensionality reduction transformations on the combined dataset prior to splitting.
Session 2 · Temporal Contamination in Sequential & Security Data
- Identify temporal lookahead bias: predicting cyber attacks or patient sepsis using features calculated from future timestamps.
- Implement rolling-origin temporal splits and PurgedGroupTimeSeriesSplit ensuring all test instances occur strictly after all training records.
- Audit data preprocessing pipelines for target encoding leakage and subtle duplicate record contamination between train and test splits.
Mental model: If your test split contains a single record from a patient or machine present in training, your published accuracy is an illusion.
Try: Take a clinical dataset with multiple visits per patient, demonstrate the inflated performance of random splitting, and correct it using patient-level GroupKFold.
Outcome: Audit research pipelines to eliminate patient-level, feature engineering, and temporal data leakage that invalidates published research findings.

M4.5 · Research Ethics, Data Governance & Datasheets for Datasets — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Privacy Regulations & De-Identification Standards
- Master data privacy legal frameworks: HIPAA Safe Harbor and Expert Determination methods in the United States, and GDPR anonymization standards in the EU.
- Understand de-identification techniques: removing 18 HIPAA Direct Identifiers, date shifting by random patient offsets, and text scrubbing of unstructured clinical notes.
- Evaluate differential privacy (DP) guarantees: epsilon-delta budgets and noise addition mechanisms to protect against membership inference attacks.
Session 2 · Datasheets for Datasets & Research Documentation
- Adopt the "Datasheets for Datasets" framework (Gebru et al.) to document motivation, composition, collection process, preprocessing, uses, and distribution.
- Identify demographic, institutional, and geographical biases embedded in public medical (e.g., predominantly single-center hospital data) and cyber datasets.
- Author an ethical impact and broader impacts statement adhering to NeurIPS, ICML, and IEEE publication standards.
Mental model: A dataset without comprehensive provenance documentation is a black box that risks propagating harmful demographic bias and regulatory violations.
Try: Author a formal 2-page Datasheet for Datasets document for an open-source medical or cybersecurity dataset, detailing collection protocols and known limitations.
Outcome: Comply with international privacy regulations, implement rigorous de-identification, and author academic Datasheets for Datasets.

M4.6 · Capstone Project: Multi-Domain Research Dataset Curation & Benchmarking Pipeline — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Multi-Domain Raw Ingestion & Entity-Safe Splitting
- Build an automated data engineering and curation pipeline targeting either a clinical cohort (MIMIC-IV / CheXpert) or a cybersecurity benchmark (CIC-IDS / Ember).
- Ingest raw heterogeneous data (relational tables, images, or packet flows), clean corrupted records, and implement entity-safe patient/host partitioning.
- Log missingness statistics, compute class distribution ratios, and verify that no feature leakage or temporal contamination occurs between splits.
Session 2 · Imputation, Imbalance Mitigation & Standardized Benchmarking
- Implement domain-specific preprocessing: medical ontology normalization or network flow feature extraction with missingness indicator masks.
- Apply focal loss weighting or synthetic sampling to resolve severe class imbalance, maintaining a pristine, untouched test set.
- Package the curated benchmark into standardized PyTorch Dataset and DataLoader formats with comprehensive unit tests and automated integrity checks.
Mental model: High-impact AI/ML research begins with pristine, leak-free data curation; a flawless benchmark pipeline provides the foundation for valid scientific discoveries.
Try: Run the full curation pipeline end-to-end on raw data, verifying zero patient overlap and exporting a complete metadata manifest.
Outcome: Deliver a standardized, ethically compliant, leak-free multi-domain research dataset and benchmark pipeline ready for publication experiments.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Multi-Domain Research Dataset Curation & Benchmarking Pipeline
BRIEF: Design and implement an end-to-end, publication-grade dataset curation and preprocessing pipeline for either clinical healthcare data (MIMIC-IV / CheXpert) or cybersecurity threat telemetry (CIC-IDS-2017 / Ember), enforcing strict entity-safe splitting, temporal leakage prevention, missingness modeling, and complete Datasheet documentation.
Build a data curation pipeline that:
- Ingests raw multi-modal or tabular data files, verifying checksum integrity and handling malformed records.
- Implements strict entity-level splitting (Patient ID or Network Host IP) to completely eliminate cross-set identity leakage.
- Enforces temporal train-validation-test ordering for sequential logs, preventing lookahead bias.
- Analyzes and documents missingness mechanisms, generating binary missingness indicator masks alongside robust imputations.
- Formulates class imbalance mitigation strategies (cost-sensitive weighting, focal loss parameters) calibrated on training splits only.
- Exports standardized PyTorch Dataset wrappers with deterministic caching and memory-mapped loading for fast GPU throughput.
- Generates an automated Data Quality & Hygiene Report detailing class distributions, correlation matrices, and leakage audit proofs.
Deliverables:
- Modular Python codebase (data_curation_pipeline/) with CLI interface and automated unit tests.
- Reproducible preprocessing scripts converting raw downloads into pristine, partitioned research artifacts.
- A comprehensive "Datasheet for Datasets" markdown document detailing dataset provenance, ethical considerations, and known biases.
- Interactive visualization dashboard displaying feature distributions, missingness patterns, and class imbalance ratios across splits.
Outcome: You can ingest, sanitize, partition, and document complex domain-specific research datasets with zero data leakage, ready for peer-reviewed publication.
