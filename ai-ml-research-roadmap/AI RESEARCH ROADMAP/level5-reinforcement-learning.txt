Level 5 · Classical Machine Learning & Deep Learning Paradigms

Phase: Model Architectures & Paradigms
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 4 (Dataset Discovery, Curation & Multi-Domain Adaptation)
Scope: Systematically explore foundational and deep learning modeling paradigms across domains: comparing tree ensembles (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) against deep tabular models (TabNet) on clinical and cybersecurity tabular records, spatial representation learning via 2D/3D Convolutional Neural Networks (3D U-Net, ResNet, Med3D) for volumetric medical imaging (CT/MRI), 1D CNNs and recurrent networks for temporal intrusion logs, transfer learning, and domain adaptation.

M5.1 · Tabular Modeling Paradigms: Gradient Boosted Trees vs Deep Tabular — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost, LightGBM & CatBoost)
- Master the mathematical foundations of gradient tree boosting: second-order Taylor expansion of the loss function, split finding algorithms, and leaf weight shrinkage.
- Apply XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost to tabular clinical records (MIMIC-IV patient mortality prediction) and cybersecurity logs (CIC-IDS attack classification).
- Handle categorical features natively using target encoding, frequency encoding, and CatBoost's ordered target statistics to prevent target leakage.
Session 2 · Deep Tabular Architectures (TabNet & FT-Transformer)
- Implement deep architectures tailored for tabular data: TabNet (sequential sparse attention masks) and Feature Tokenizer Transformer (FT-Transformer).
- Compare inductive biases: understand why GBDTs consistently outperform standard multi-layer perceptrons on unrotated heterogeneous tabular data.
- Benchmark training compute, inference throughput, and hyperparameter sensitivity between tree ensembles and deep tabular models.
Mental model: GBDTs partition feature space along orthogonal hyperplanes, making them the gold-standard baseline for non-spatial, unrotated tabular data.
Try: Train an XGBoost baseline and a TabNet model on a clinical tabular dataset, comparing AUROC, parameter count, and training wall-clock time.
Outcome: Select, tune, and evaluate state-of-the-art tree ensembles and deep tabular models across medical and cybersecurity benchmarks.

M5.2 · Spatial Deep Learning & Medical Imaging: 2D/3D CNNs & U-Net — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Convolutional Backbones & Spatial Invariance (ResNet to ConvNeXt)
- Analyze inductive biases of convolutional operators: translation equivariance, weight sharing, and localized receptive field hierarchies.
- Implement residual learning (ResNet), grouped convolutions (ResNeXt), and modernized architectures (ConvNeXt) for 2D radiological images (CheXpert).
- Evaluate receptive field expansion, multi-scale feature pyramids (FPN), and global average pooling for multi-label pathological classification.
Session 2 · Volumetric 3D CNNs & Semantic Segmentation (3D U-Net)
- Transition from 2D pixel grids to 3D voxel grids for volumetric medical imaging (CT and MRI scans from the Medical Segmentation Decathlon).
- Architect the 3D U-Net: encoder feature compression, skip connections preserving high-resolution spatial boundaries, and decoder upsampling.
- Formulate specialized segmentation loss functions: Soft Dice Loss, Generalized Dice Loss, and Focal Tversky Loss to handle severe foreground-background voxel imbalance.
Mental model: Skip connections in U-Net act as spatial bridges, allowing fine-grained anatomical edge details to bypass the bottleneck directly to the decoder.
Try: Implement a 3D U-Net in PyTorch, apply Soft Dice Loss on a volumetric brain tumor segmentation dataset, and compute the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC).
Outcome: Design and train 2D and 3D convolutional neural networks for medical radiological classification and volumetric anatomical segmentation.

M5.3 · Temporal & Sequential Paradigms: 1D CNNs, BiLSTMs & TCNs — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · 1D Convolutions & Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)
- Apply 1D temporal convolutions to sequential telemetry data: continuous ICU physiological waveforms (ECG, arterial blood pressure) and network packet bursts.
- Architect Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN): causal convolutions preventing future leakage and dilated convolutions providing exponentially expanding receptive fields.
- Analyze computational advantages of TCNs over recurrent networks: massively parallel training execution and stable gradient propagation across long time horizons.
Session 2 · Recurrent Neural Networks & Bidirectional LSTMs
- Derive the mathematical gating equations for Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), analyzing how additive cell state updates mitigate vanishing gradients.
- Train Bidirectional LSTMs (BiLSTM) with temporal attention mechanisms on cybersecurity log sequences and sequential clinical lab event histories.
- Compare sequential modeling performance: evaluate memory footprint, sequential inference latency, and temporal context retention across 1D CNNs and LSTMs.
Mental model: Dilated causal convolutions expand receptive field exponentially without increasing parameter count, offering a parallelizable alternative to recurrence.
Try: Build a Temporal Convolutional Network with dilated causal residual blocks in PyTorch, training it on raw network packet sequence data to detect intrusion anomalies.
Outcome: Model time-series physiological waveforms and sequential cybersecurity telemetry using 1D convolutional and recurrent deep learning architectures.

M5.4 · Self-Supervised Representation Learning across Domains — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Contrastive Self-Supervised Learning (SimCLR & MoCo)
- Formulate contrastive representation learning from first principles: the InfoNCE loss function and positive/negative pair formulation.
- Implement SimCLR and Momentum Contrast (MoCo) frameworks, analyzing the role of memory banks, momentum encoders, and temperature hyperparameters.
- Design domain-specific data augmentations: random cropping, Gaussian blur, and affine transformations for medical scans; random jitter and masking for cyber telemetry.
Session 2 · Non-Contrastive & Masked Representation Learning (Barlow Twins & MAE)
- Analyze non-contrastive self-supervised architectures (Barlow Twins, BYOL) that eliminate negative pairs by minimizing cross-correlation redundancy.
- Implement Masked Autoencoders (MAE): masking 75% of input visual or spatial patches and training lightweight decoders to reconstruct pixel intensities.
- Evaluate learned representations using linear probing (freezing the backbone and training a linear classifier) and k-nearest-neighbors evaluation.
Mental model: Self-supervised learning exploits massive unlabeled datasets to build rich geometric representation spaces before a single task-specific label is seen.
Try: Pre-train a convolutional encoder using SimCLR on unlabelled chest X-rays, freeze the backbone, and evaluate linear probe classification accuracy against supervised training.
Outcome: Pre-train domain-specific visual and sequential encoders using self-supervised contrastive and reconstruction objectives without manual labels.

M5.5 · Transfer Learning, Domain Shift & Out-of-Distribution Generalization — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Fine-Tuning Strategies & Inductive Transfer
- Master transfer learning mechanics: layer freezing, discriminative learning rates, gradual unfreezing, and feature extraction vs. full fine-tuning.
- Investigate negative transfer: when pre-training on natural images (ImageNet) provides suboptimal representations for specialized domains (microscopy or packet flows).
- Apply domain adaptation techniques: Domain Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN) using gradient reversal layers (GRL) to align source and target latent spaces.
Session 2 · Covariate Shift & Out-of-Distribution Evaluation
- Formalize distribution shift: covariate shift (P(X) shifts while P(Y|X) is constant), concept drift (P(Y|X) shifts), and label shift (P(Y) shifts).
- Evaluate multi-center domain generalization in healthcare: training a diagnostic model on Hospital A and testing zero-shot generalization on Hospital B and C.
- Stress-test cybersecurity classifiers against temporal concept drift: evaluating malware classifiers on binaries compiled months or years after the training window.
Mental model: High within-distribution test accuracy is easy; true scientific robustness requires generalization across different hospitals, scanner protocols, and enterprise networks.
Try: Train a classifier on data from one institution, test on a secondary institution to measure out-of-distribution performance drop, and mitigate the drop using DANN.
Outcome: Detect and mitigate domain shift, evaluate out-of-distribution generalization, and apply transfer learning across distinct scientific environments.

M5.6 · Capstone Project: Cross-Paradigm Clinical & Cybersecurity Modeling Benchmark — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Experimental Setup & Multi-Paradigm Architecture Harness
- Build an end-to-end comparative benchmarking framework implementing classical tree ensembles (LightGBM/XGBoost), deep tabular models (TabNet), and deep neural networks.
- Evaluate on both a clinical risk prediction task (MIMIC-IV mortality/sepsis) and a cybersecurity threat detection task (CIC-IDS-2017 intrusion classification).
- Standardize evaluation protocols: compute-matched hyperparameter tuning, identical cross-validation folds, and unified evaluation metric logging.
Session 2 · Comprehensive Empirical Evaluation & Scientific Reporting
- Compute multi-dimensional metrics: AUROC, AUPRC (Average Precision), Expected Calibration Error (ECE), parameter count, and inference latency per sample.
- Execute paired statistical hypothesis tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank) to determine whether deep learning outperforms tree ensembles with statistical significance.
- Generate publication-ready comparative tables and ROC/PR curve plots formatted for conference submission.
Mental model: Rigorous research avoids paradigm dogmatism; systematically comparing classical and deep models on equal footing reveals genuine empirical trade-offs.
Try: Run the cross-paradigm benchmark, execute statistical significance tests, and produce an empirical comparison table ready for paper insertion.
Outcome: Deliver an exhaustive empirical benchmark comparing classical and deep learning paradigms across medical and cybersecurity domains.
