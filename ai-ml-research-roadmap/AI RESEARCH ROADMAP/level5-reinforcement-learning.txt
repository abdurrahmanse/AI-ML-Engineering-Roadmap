Level 5 · Core Modeling Paradigms: Architectures & Representations

Phase: Paradigms & Generative Models
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 4 (Deep Learning Research Prototyping in PyTorch & JAX)
Scope: Master foundational modeling paradigms and representation learning from first principles: the Transformer architecture, attention variants, rotary embeddings (RoPE), State Space Models (Mamba), self-supervised representation objectives, and geometric deep learning.

M5.1 · The Transformer Blueprint: Attention Mechanics, KV Cache & Scaled Dot Products — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Multi-Head Attention Derivation & Mechanics
- Derive scaled dot-product attention from first principles: query-key compatibility, square-root dimension scaling, and Softmax normalization.
- Implement multi-head self-attention and cross-attention from scratch, managing tensor projections, head splitting, and output transformations.
- Analyze the quadratic time and memory complexity O(N^2) of standard attention with respect to sequence length N.
Session 2 · Autoregressive Decoding & The KV Cache
- Understand autoregressive sequence generation dynamics and the computational redundancy of re-computing past key and value vectors.
- Implement a key-value (KV) cache for fast autoregressive decoding, managing rolling tensor buffers across generation timesteps.
- Implement modern attention memory optimizations: Multi-Query Attention (MQA) and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) to reduce KV cache memory footprints.
Mental model: Multi-head attention is a soft, content-addressable memory retrieval mechanism; the KV cache prevents redundant historical recomputation.
Try: Implement a self-attention module from scratch in PyTorch with Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) and verify output tensor shapes and parity against torch.nn.MultiheadAttention.
Outcome: Build, modify, and optimize multi-head attention mechanisms from mathematical equations, including low-overhead KV caching.

M5.2 · Advanced Context & Positional Inductive Biases: RoPE, ALiBi & Linear Attention — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) & Relative Encodings
- Understand why absolute sinusoidal position embeddings fail to generalize to sequence lengths beyond the training horizon.
- Derive Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) mathematically as complex number rotations of 2D coordinate pairs in query and key feature spaces.
- Implement RoPE from scratch in PyTorch, applying 2D complex rotations and inspecting the decay of inner products with increasing relative distance.
Session 2 · Long-Context Extensions & Attention Variants
- Implement Attention with Linear Biases (ALiBi), penalizing query-key attention scores with static linear distance biases for zero-shot length extrapolation.
- Analyze linear attention approximations (Kernelized Attention, Performer, Linear Transformer) and their computational trade-offs.
- Understand the hardware-aware design of FlashAttention: GPU SRAM tiling, online Softmax computation, and avoiding HBM read/write roundtrips.
Mental model: RoPE injects relative positional information purely through geometric rotations of the query and key vectors without altering their magnitudes.
Try: Implement RoPE vector rotations on a toy sequence, and compute the attention matrix to visualize the natural decay of attention weights over distance.
Outcome: Implement modern positional encoding schemes (RoPE, ALiBi) and analyze long-context extrapolation mechanisms in foundation models.

M5.3 · Sub-Quadratic State Space Models: S4, Mamba & Hybrid Sequence Architectures — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Continuous-Time State Space Systems & Discretization
- Formulate linear time-invariant (LTI) continuous state space models dx/dt = Ax + Bu, y = Cx + Du from classical control theory.
- Discretize continuous state space equations into discrete recurrence relations using the bilinear (Tustin) or zero-order hold (ZOH) transformation.
- Master the Structured State Space (S4) model: HiPPO matrix initialization for long-range memory and dual computation (recurrent inference, convolutional training).
Session 2 · Selective State Spaces & The Mamba Architecture
- Understand the limitation of LTI state space models: static, input-independent transition matrices that cannot perform selective in-context filtering.
- Implement the selective scan mechanism of Mamba where B, C, and delta matrices become dynamic, input-dependent functions of token embeddings.
- Analyze the linear O(N) sequence length complexity of Mamba and evaluate hybrid Transformer-SSM architectural designs.
Mental model: Transformers are associative memories that re-attend to all history; State Space Models compress history into a continuous latent state vector.
Try: Implement a minimal selective state space scan function in PyTorch, compare its linear scaling against quadratic attention on 16k sequence lengths.
Outcome: Master modern sub-quadratic sequence models (S4, Mamba), understand state space discretization, and evaluate hybrid architectures.

M5.4 · Self-Supervised Learning: Contrastive InfoNCE, SimCLR & Masked Autoencoding — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Contrastive Representation Learning & InfoNCE
- Understand the manifold hypothesis: high-dimensional real-world data concentrates along lower-dimensional continuous geometric sub-manifolds.
- Derive the InfoNCE contrastive loss function from lower bounds on mutual information between augmented views.
- Implement contrastive learning architectures (SimCLR, MoCo) with positive pair generation, negative queue caching, and temperature scaling.
Session 2 · Non-Contrastive SSL & Masked Autoencoding
- Analyze collapse modes in representation learning (dimensional collapse, complete representation collapse) and how stop-gradient methods prevent them (BYOL, SimSiam).
- Implement redundancy reduction methods (Barlow Twins) by driving the cross-correlation matrix of dual representations toward the identity matrix.
- Master Masked Autoencoders (MAE): high-ratio token masking, asymmetric encoder-decoder architectures, and pixel-level reconstruction objectives.
Mental model: Contrastive learning pulls augmented views of the same instance together while pushing distinct instances apart across a hyperspherical latent space.
Try: Train a small representation encoder using InfoNCE loss on unlabelled image patches, and evaluate representation quality via linear probe accuracy.
Outcome: Formulate self-supervised learning objectives, prevent representation collapse, and train rich latent encoders without human labels.

M5.5 · Geometric Deep Learning: Graph Neural Networks & Equivariant Representations — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Graph Representation Learning & Message Passing
- Represent relational scientific data (molecules, knowledge graphs, citation networks) as graph structures with node, edge, and global feature tensors.
- Derive the general Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN) paradigm: message computation, neighborhood aggregation, and node state updates.
- Implement Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and Graph Attention Networks (GAT) with dynamic edge attention coefficients.
Session 2 · Invariance, Equivariance & Symmetry Groups
- Formalize permutation invariance and permutation equivariance in deep learning architectures over graph and point-cloud structures.
- Understand geometric symmetry groups: Euclidean group E(3), special Euclidean group SE(3), and rotation group SO(3) in scientific modeling.
- Master E(n)-equivariant neural networks (EGNN) and their applications in modeling molecular conformations and 3D molecular structures.
Mental model: Deep learning succeeds by encoding the physical symmetries of the problem domain as structural inductive biases in the network architecture.
Try: Implement an MPNN message passing layer from scratch in PyTorch, verifying that node permutations produce identical permuted output representations.
Outcome: Build Graph Neural Networks from first principles, understand geometric equivariance, and apply symmetry constraints to scientific data.

M5.6 · Capstone Project: Comparative Architectural Benchmark & Latent Space Analysis — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Model Implementation & Training Infrastructure
- Implement three contrasting sequence architectures from scratch: standard Transformer (MQA/RoPE), Selective State Space Model (Mamba), and a GNN.
- Train all three models on a standardized long-range sequence reasoning task under strictly matched compute and parameter budgets.
- Log intermediate layer representations and compute layer-wise activation statistics throughout the training trajectory.
Session 2 · Latent Geometry Probing & Empirical Comparison
- Probe the geometry of learned representation spaces using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) and singular value spectrum analysis.
- Measure empirical throughput, peak memory allocation, and sequence length scaling curves from 512 to 16,384 tokens across architectures.
- Author an empirical research report comparing expressivity, memory scaling, and inductive bias trade-offs with publication-quality plots.
Mental model: Rigorous empirical comparison of distinct architectural paradigms reveals their fundamental trade-offs between memory complexity and expressivity.
Try: Run the comparative benchmark, generate a CKA similarity heatmap comparing Transformer and Mamba layer representations, and analyze the architectural differences.
Outcome: Master the implementation and empirical analysis of modern foundational architectures and probe their latent representation geometries.
