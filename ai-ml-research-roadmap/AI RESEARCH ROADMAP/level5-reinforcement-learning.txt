Level 5 · Reinforcement Learning & Sequential Decision Theory

Scope: explore the mathematical foundations of sequential decision making under uncertainty: Markov Decision Processes, Bellman equations, dynamic programming, policy gradients, actor-critic algorithms, and model-based reinforcement learning.

Prerequisites: Level 4

M5.1 · Markov Decision Processes & Bellman Optimality — (sessions: 3)

Formulate the Reinforcement Learning problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP): states S, actions A, transition probability function P(s'|s, a), reward function R(s, a), and discount factor gamma.
Define trajectories, discounted return G_t, policy pi(a|s), state-value function V^pi(s), and action-value function Q^pi(s, a).
Derive the Bellman Expectation Equations for V^pi(s) and Q^pi(s, a) using the law of total expectation.
Define optimal policy pi^*, optimal state-value V^*(s), and optimal action-value Q^*(s, a).
Derive the Bellman Optimality Equations: non-linear system of equations incorporating max operators over actions.
Prove the Bellman operator is a contraction mapping in the supremum norm via Banach Fixed-Point Theorem, ensuring convergence to a unique fixed point.
Implement Dynamic Programming algorithms for known MDPs: Policy Iteration (policy evaluation + policy improvement theorem) and Value Iteration.
Outcome: prove the contraction mapping property of the Bellman optimality operator and implement exact Value Iteration for discrete MDP environments.

M5.2 · Model-Free Value-Based Methods & Q-Learning — (sessions: 4)

Understand the model-free setting where transition dynamics P(s'|s, a) and reward functions R(s, a) are unknown.
Analyze Monte Carlo (MC) methods: first-visit vs every-visit MC, high variance, zero bias, and requiring complete episodes.
Master Temporal Difference (TD) learning: TD(0) update rule, TD error delta_t, bootstrapping, and bias-variance trade-offs against Monte Carlo.
Contrast on-policy learning (SARSA) and off-policy learning (Q-learning) and analyze their exploration-exploitation convergence guarantees.
Study function approximation in Q-learning: linear function approximation, feature divergence, and the "deadly triad" (bootstrapping, off-policy learning, function approximation).
Master Deep Q-Networks (DQN, Mnih et al.): using neural networks to approximate Q(s, a), experience replay buffers to break temporal correlation, and target networks to stabilize regression targets.
Analyze improvements to DQN: Double DQN (mitigating maximization bias), Prioritized Experience Replay (PER), and Dueling DQN (decoupling state-value and advantage functions).
Outcome: derive Double DQN mathematically to prove how decoupled action selection and evaluation eliminates overestimation bias, and train a DQN agent on a classic control environment.

M5.3 · Policy Gradient Theorems & Actor-Critic Architectures — (sessions: 4)

Understand parameterized policies pi_theta(a|s) and formulate the expected trajectory return objective J(theta).
Derive the Policy Gradient Theorem analytically using the log-derivative trick (likelihood ratio): grad_theta J(theta) = E[grad_theta log pi_theta(a|s) * Q(s, a)].
Analyze the REINFORCE algorithm (Williams): Monte Carlo policy gradient, empirical sample averages, and high variance limitations.
Introduce baselines to policy gradients: proving mathematically that state-dependent baselines b(s) reduce variance without introducing any bias to the gradient estimate.
Define the Advantage Function: A(s, a) = Q(s, a) - V(s), and formulate Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C/A3C) architectures.
Analyze Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE, Schulman et al.): compounding n-step TD errors via exponential decay parameter lambda to tune bias-variance trade-offs.
Understand policy parameterization for continuous action spaces: Gaussian policies with learnable mean and state-independent or state-dependent log variance.
Outcome: derive the Policy Gradient Theorem on paper and implement Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) with analytical variance analysis.

M5.4 · Advanced Policy Optimization: TRPO, PPO & Soft Actor-Critic — (sessions: 4)

Analyze the step-size problem in policy gradient methods: large parameter updates causing catastrophic policy collapse in non-stationary RL landscapes.
Study Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO, Schulman et al.): surrogate objective, monotonic improvement guarantee, and enforcing a hard KL divergence constraint D_KL(pi_old || pi_new) <= delta.
Understand natural policy gradients and Fisher Information Matrix approximations using conjugate gradient methods to compute trust-region steps.
Master Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO, Schulman et al.): PPO-Clip surrogate objective with clipped probability ratios r_t(theta) to constrain updates without second-order Hessians.
Analyze the entropy bonus in policy objectives to encourage exploration and prevent premature policy convergence to deterministic sub-optimal actions.
Master Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC, Haarnoja et al.), entropy-augmented reward, soft Bellman equation, and continuous off-policy stability.
Compare on-policy (PPO) versus off-policy (SAC) sample efficiency, wall-clock compute requirements, and optimization stability.
Outcome: derive the PPO clipped surrogate objective and implement a complete PPO actor-critic training pipeline in PyTorch.

M5.5 · Model-Based Reinforcement Learning & Search — (sessions: 3)

Understand Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL): learning transition dynamics models f_theta(s, a) = s' and planning via simulated rollouts.
Analyze model bias and compounding error: why small inaccuracies in learned environment dynamics amplify exponentially over multi-step planning horizons.
Master Model Predictive Control (MPC): shooting methods, cross-entropy method (CEM), and online replanning at every environmental time step.
Study Dyna-Q architecture: interleaving real-world experience, model learning, and simulated experience replay for sample-efficient policy improvement.
Analyze Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS): selection (PUCT algorithm), expansion, simulation / rollout, and backpropagation phases.
Study AlphaZero and MuZero principles (Silver et al., Schrittwieser et al.): unifying deep neural networks with MCTS planning without explicit environment rules.
Outcome: implement a learned transition model with Model Predictive Control (MPC) and evaluate compounding prediction error over varying rollout depths.

M5.6 · Capstone: Benchmark PPO vs Soft Actor-Critic on Continuous Control with Ablation Analysis — (sessions: 2)

Design an empirical research benchmark comparing on-policy Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) against off-policy Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) on continuous control environments (e.g. Gymnasium HalfCheetah / Ant / InvertedPendulum).
Implement PPO with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), orthogonal weight initialization, gradient clipping, and value function clipping.
Implement Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with twin Q-networks (clipped double-Q trick), target smoothing (Polyak averaging tau), and automatic entropy temperature tuning (alpha).
Conduct controlled experiments across at least 5 distinct random seeds to compute statistically sound learning curves with 95% confidence intervals.
Perform systematic ablation studies on PPO: (1) effect of clipping epsilon (0.1, 0.2, 0.3), (2) GAE lambda trade-off between bias and variance, and (3) presence vs absence of value function clipping.
Perform systematic ablation studies on SAC: (1) fixed entropy temperature vs automatic entropy adjustment, and (2) target update rate tau.
Compare sample efficiency (environment steps to reach reward threshold) and wall-clock training time between PPO and SAC.
Draft a comprehensive academic research paper summarizing findings, plotting reward trajectories with variance bands, and analyzing stability failure modes.
Final result: a reproducible deep RL benchmark codebase and research paper evaluating on-policy vs off-policy policy optimization algorithms.

Level 5 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Formulate continuous and discrete decision problems as Markov Decision Processes.
Derive the Bellman Expectation and Optimality equations from first principles.
Prove that the Bellman optimality operator is a gamma-contraction mapping using the Banach Fixed-Point Theorem.
Implement Policy Iteration and Value Iteration for discrete state-action spaces.
Explain the deadly triad in reinforcement learning: bootstrapping, function approximation, and off-policy learning.
Implement Deep Q-Networks (DQN) with experience replay and target networks.
Derive Double DQN and explain how it resolves the maximization bias of standard Q-learning.
Derive the Policy Gradient Theorem using the likelihood ratio trick.
Prove that subtracting a state-dependent baseline from policy gradients introduces zero bias while reducing variance.
Implement Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and explain the role of the lambda parameter.
Explain the mathematical motivation for Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and the KL constraint.
Derive the clipped surrogate objective of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
Formulate the Maximum Entropy RL framework and explain how Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) balances exploration and exploitation.
Explain compounding prediction error in model-based RL and how Model Predictive Control mitigates drift.
Implement the four phases of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with PUCT action selection.
Conduct reproducible RL experiments with multiple random seeds, reporting mean returns and confidence intervals.
Progression: MDPs & Bellman Contractions → Model-Free Q-Learning & DQN → Policy Gradients & Advantage Actor-Critic → PPO & Soft Actor-Critic → Model-Based RL & MCTS → Deep RL Benchmark Capstone.
