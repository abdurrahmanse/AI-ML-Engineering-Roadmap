Level 1 · Mathematical Foundations

Phase: Research Foundations
Suggested duration: 33 sessions (~8 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 0
Scope: Master the linear algebra, multivariable vector calculus, matrix differentials, convex optimization, first-order algorithms, and the mathematics of modern neural architectures essential for deriving and analyzing AI/ML models from scratch.

M1.1 · Vector Spaces, Linear Independence, Projections & Decompositions — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Vector spaces, subspaces & orthogonality
- Prerequisites & Foundations: Define vector spaces, subspaces, linear independence, basis, span, dimension, inner products, and orthogonality in Euclidean space.
- Geometry & Projections: Compute vector projections onto subspaces, orthogonal complements, and execute Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization with geometric visualization.
Session 2 · Matrix operations, rank & fundamental subspaces
- Matrix Algebra: Connect matrix multiplication to linear transformations, change of basis, kernel/nullspace, and the Rank-Nullity Theorem.
- Matrix Decompositions: Formulate Eigendecomposition of symmetric matrices, Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), spectral norm, and low-rank matrix approximations.
Mental model: High-dimensional data points are vectors in geometric spaces; matrix transformations rotate, scale, and project this geometry into lower dimensions.
Try: Implement SVD from scratch using the power iteration method for top singular vectors and verify against NumPy linalg svd on an image compression task.
Outcome: You can manipulate high-dimensional geometric spaces, compute matrix decompositions, and derive low-rank approximations analytically.

M1.2 · Multivariable Vector Calculus & Matrix Differential Calculus — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Gradients, Jacobians, Hessians & Taylor approximations
- Multivariable Differentiation: Compute partial derivatives, directional derivatives, gradient vectors, Jacobian matrices, and Hessian curvature matrices.
- Multivariate Taylor Expansions: Construct first-order and second-order Taylor approximations to analyze local curvature and function approximations near stationary points.
Session 2 · Matrix calculus conventions & computational graphs
- Matrix Calculus Rules: Derive gradients of scalar loss functions with respect to vectors and matrices using numerator layout conventions and trace tricks.
- Computation Graphs & Autograd: Trace forward-mode and reverse-mode automatic differentiation on directed graphs, calculating vector-Jacobian products (VJPs).
Mental model: Matrix calculus allows us to calculate sensitivity across thousands of interacting parameters simultaneously along the tangent spaces of error surfaces.
Try: Derive the analytical matrix gradient of a two-layer linear autoencoder loss function and verify the computation graph gradient with two-sided numerical finite differences.
Outcome: You can calculate complex matrix derivatives, trace computational graphs, and verify analytical gradients numerically.

M1.3 · Convex Optimization Theory, Duality & Constraints — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Convex sets, convex functions & subgradients
- Convexity Analysis: Identify convex sets, convex functions, affine hulls, epigraphs, and apply Jensen's inequality to establish mathematical bounds.
- Non-Smooth Optimization: Formulate subgradients and subdifferentials for non-differentiable points such as ReLU activations and L1-norm penalties.
Session 2 · Constrained optimization, Lagrangian duality & KKT conditions
- Constrained Formulations: Formulate primal optimization problems with inequality and equality constraints using Lagrange multipliers.
- Duality & Optimality: Derive the Lagrange dual function, prove weak and strong duality under Slater's condition, and solve Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions.
Mental model: Convexity guarantees that any local minimum is a global minimum, providing rigorous theoretical guarantees for machine learning convergence.
Try: Derive the Wolfe dual formulation and KKT conditions for a soft-margin Support Vector Machine by hand and solve the quadratic program using an analytical toy example.
Outcome: You can determine function convexity, formulate Lagrangian duals, and apply KKT optimality conditions to constrained ML problems.

M1.4 · First-Order & Adaptive Optimization Algorithms — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Gradient descent variants & momentum mechanics
- Gradient Descent Dynamics: Analyze convergence rates of Batch Gradient Descent, Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), and mini-batch SGD on convex and non-convex surfaces.
- Accelerated Methods: Implement classical momentum and Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG) to dampen oscillations along high-curvature ravines.
Session 2 · Adaptive learning rate optimizers & weight decay
- Adaptive Optimizers: Derive and compare AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam, and AdamW (decoupled weight decay) to handle sparse gradients and ill-conditioned curvatures.
- Numerical Stability: Implement numerical guards including the log-sum-exp trick for stable softmax calculations, gradient clipping, and condition number audits.
Mental model: Optimization is navigation across a high-dimensional loss terrain; adaptive algorithms adjust step sizes along each coordinate based on past gradient variance.
Try: Build a simulation notebook comparing SGD, Momentum, RMSProp, and AdamW on ill-conditioned quadratic surfaces and the Rosenbrock function, tracking convergence steps.
Outcome: You can select, implement, and tune first-order and adaptive optimizers while diagnosing numerical instability and ill-conditioning.

M1.5 · Paper Reading & Theoretical Verification in Optimization — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Landmark optimization paper analysis
- Seminal Paper Reading: Study Boyd & Vandenberghe (Convex Optimization), Kingma & Ba (Adam, 2014), and Loshchilov & Hutter (Decoupled Weight Decay / AdamW, 2017).
- Research Questions: Investigate why L2 regularization fails in Adam without explicit decoupling, and how learning rate warmups stabilize early training dynamics.
Session 2 · Numerical verification & ablation experiments
- Analytical Verification: Write automated finite-difference gradient checkers to validate analytical derivations against numerical approximations (|f'(x) - f_num(x)| < 1e-6).
- Optimization Ablations: Design an ablation comparing Adam with L2 regularization versus AdamW on generalization performance and parameter weight distributions.
Mental model: Reading optimization papers requires checking their mathematical proofs against numerical simulations to verify where assumptions hold or break.
Try: Replicate the core empirical finding of the AdamW paper on a small neural network, demonstrating that AdamW yields superior weight decay dynamics than standard Adam with L2 penalty.
Outcome: You can critically read optimization literature, reproduce theoretical claims, and perform rigorous numerical verification.

M1.6 · Capstone: Numerical Optimization Engine & Convex Solver from Scratch — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Architecture of a standalone numerical optimization solver
- Build a standalone, object-oriented numerical optimization library in pure NumPy containing SGD with Nesterov momentum, AdamW, and L-BFGS.
- Implement an automated computation graph engine supporting scalar and tensor automatic differentiation with reverse-mode vector-Jacobian products.
Session 2 · Benchmarking, verification & technical report
- Benchmark the solver across convex and non-convex test surfaces (Beale, Rosenbrock, Rastrigin), recording convergence rates, trajectory plots, and condition numbers.
- Write a formal 4-page mathematical technical report documenting the derivation, convergence proofs, numerical verification checks, and experimental results.
Final result: A fully functional, mathematically verified numerical optimization engine written from scratch, accompanied by a rigorous technical report and benchmark suite.

M1.7 · The Mathematics of Modern AI: Connecting Theory to Transformers — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · From linear algebra to attention & embeddings
- Attention as Matrix Multiplication: Derive the Scaled Dot-Product Attention formula (softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)) * V) from first principles using your knowledge of matrix multiplication, inner products, and the softmax function; explain why dividing by sqrt(d_k) prevents gradient vanishing in deep networks.
- Embedding Geometry: Explain why learned token embeddings form vector spaces where arithmetic relationships (king - man + woman = queen) emerge from SVD-like low-rank structure in training data co-occurrence matrices.
- Softmax & Cross-Entropy: Connect your knowledge of probability distributions, the log-sum-exp numerical trick, and KL divergence to the cross-entropy next-token prediction loss used in all autoregressive language model training.
Session 2 · Scaling laws, loss landscapes & why large models generalize
- Neural Scaling Laws: Interpret Kaplan et al. (2020) scaling law power equations (L ~ C^(-0.054)) using your knowledge of optimization convergence rates and function approximation theory.
- Loss Landscape Geometry: Connect your knowledge of Hessian curvature, saddle points, and flat minima to empirical observations about why large language models find wide, flat minima that generalize better than sharp narrow ones.
- Why Gradient Descent Works on Non-Convex LLM Objectives: Reconcile convex optimization theory (which guarantees global minima) with the non-convex reality of neural network training, explaining why SGD with warmup reliably finds good solutions despite theoretical non-convexity.
Mental model: Every component of a modern transformer — attention, positional encoding, layer normalization, weight initialization — is a direct application of the linear algebra, calculus, and optimization you have just mastered.
Try: Implement Scaled Dot-Product Attention in pure NumPy from the formula, run a gradient check to verify the backward pass, and visualize the attention weight matrix for a 10-token input sentence to confirm it produces interpretable patterns.
Outcome: You can derive and explain every mathematical operation inside a transformer block using your foundational knowledge of linear algebra, calculus, and convex optimization.
