Level 5 · Classical Machine Learning

Phase: Data & Modeling
Suggested duration: 35 sessions (~9 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 4
Scope: Master linear models, probabilistic classifiers, instance-based learning, support vector machines, decision trees, gradient boosted ensembles, clustering, and anomaly detection to establish rock-solid scientific baselines.

M5.1 · Linear Regression, Polynomial Models & Regularization — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) derivation & geometry
- Normal Equations Derivation: Derive the analytical OLS solution (beta = (X^T X)^(-1) X^T y) from MSE minimization, proving the Gauss-Markov Theorem.
- Assumptions & Diagnostics: Inspect linear model assumptions (linearity, homoscedasticity, normality of residuals, independence) and diagnose Cook's distance outliers.
Session 2 · Polynomial regression, Ridge (L2), Lasso (L1) & ElasticNet
- Non-Linear Basis Expansion: Formulate polynomial feature expansions and analyze the resulting runaway variance and multicollinearity.
- Regularization Mechanics: Derive Ridge shrinkage analytically, derive Lasso coordinate descent updates, explain L1 sparsity geometry, and tune ElasticNet mixing ratios.
Mental model: Regularization imposes a geometric prior on parameter magnitudes, trading a small amount of bias for a dramatic reduction in variance.
Try: Implement Ridge and Lasso coordinate descent from scratch in NumPy, plot the regularization path coefficients as lambda increases, and observe exact zero-weight sparsity in Lasso.
Outcome: You can derive OLS, implement regularized linear models from scratch, and evaluate residual diagnostic plots.

M5.2 · Logistic Regression, Probabilistic Classifiers & Calibration — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Logistic regression & iteratively reweighted least squares
- Sigmoid Function & Odds Ratios: Model log-odds, formulate the cross-entropy negative log-likelihood loss, and calculate analytical gradient and Hessian matrices.
- Newton-Raphson Optimization: Implement Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) to optimize logistic regression parameters and derive Platt scaling for calibration.
Session 2 · Naive Bayes classification & generative assumptions
- Generative Classification: Formulate the Bayes optimal classifier and the conditional independence assumption underlying Naive Bayes.
- Distribution Variants: Implement Gaussian Naive Bayes for continuous features, Multinomial Naive Bayes with Laplace smoothing for text, and Bernoulli Naive Bayes.
Mental model: Logistic regression is a discriminative model learning decision boundaries; Naive Bayes is a generative model learning how each class produces features.
Try: Build a spam detection system comparing Multinomial Naive Bayes against L1-regularized Logistic Regression, computing calibration curves and Brier scores.
Outcome: You can derive, implement, and calibrate discriminative and generative linear classifiers for binary and multiclass problems.

M5.3 · Instance-Based Learning & Support Vector Machines (SVM) — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) & the curse of dimensionality
- Non-Parametric Neighborhoods: Implement KNN classification and regression, analyze distance metrics (Euclidean, Manhattan, Minkowski, Mahalanobis), and tune k via cross-validation.
- Computational Geometry: Analyze the curse of dimensionality in high dimensions and benchmark brute-force search against KD-Tree and Ball-Tree spatial partitioning.
Session 2 · Support Vector Machines & the Kernel Trick
- Maximum Margin Geometry: Formulate the primal hard-margin and soft-margin SVM with slack variables and box constraint C; derive the Wolfe dual formulation.
- The Kernel Trick: Apply Mercer's Theorem to project vectors into infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces using Radial Basis Function (RBF/Gaussian) and polynomial kernels.
Mental model: SVM finds the unique hyperplane that maximizes the geometric margin between classes, relying strictly on the subset of data points that lie on the margin boundary.
Try: Solve an SVM dual quadratic program using cvxpy, extract the active support vectors, and visualize the non-linear decision boundary generated by an RBF kernel.
Outcome: You can derive soft-margin SVMs, apply the kernel trick to non-linear classification, and evaluate instance-based models.

M5.4 · Decision Trees & Tree-Based Induction Algorithms — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Split criteria & recursive binary splitting
- Splitting Metrics: Formulate Gini Impurity, Shannon Entropy / Information Gain, and Variance Reduction for regression trees.
- CART Induction Algorithm: Implement recursive binary splitting from scratch, determining optimal feature thresholds and handling mixed numerical and categorical types.
Session 2 · Tree regularization & cost-complexity pruning
- Stopping Rules: Control tree depth, minimum samples per split, minimum samples per leaf, and maximum leaf nodes to prevent memorization of noise.
- Minimal Cost-Complexity Pruning: Implement alpha-pruning to prune subtrees that fail to improve generalized validation error.
Mental model: A decision tree recursively partitions the feature space into axis-aligned rectangular regions, assigning the majority class or mean value within each leaf.
Try: Build a complete CART decision tree classifier from scratch in Python, print the resulting tree logic in human-readable rules, and visualize its axis-aligned 2D decision boundary.
Outcome: You can code decision tree induction from scratch, compute split criteria, and prune trees to prevent overfitting.

M5.5 · Ensemble Learning: Random Forests & Gradient Boosted Decision Trees — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Bootstrap aggregating (Bagging) & Random Forests
- Bagging Theory: Prove why bootstrap averaging reduces model variance without increasing bias; calculate Out-Of-Bag (OOB) error estimates.
- Random Forests: Implement feature subsampling (mtry = sqrt(p)) at each split to decorrelate trees; compute Mean Decrease in Impurity (MDI) and permutation feature importances.
Session 2 · Boosting theory: AdaBoost & Gradient Boosting
- Adaptive Boosting: Formulate AdaBoost, exponential loss minimization, and sequential sample re-weighting based on classification errors.
- Gradient Boosting Machines: Formulate gradient boosting as gradient descent in function space, fitting successive shallow trees to the negative gradient (pseudo-residuals).
Session 3 · Modern GBDT frameworks: XGBoost, LightGBM & CatBoost
- XGBoost Innovations: Derive second-order Taylor expansion of the objective function, exact greedy vs weighted quantile sketch splits, and sparsity-aware split finding.
- LightGBM & CatBoost: Understand Gradient-based One-Side Sampling (GOSS), Exclusive Feature Bundling (EFB), and CatBoost's ordered target encoding for categorical features.
Mental model: Random Forests combine many independent, overfitted trees in parallel to cancel variance; Gradient Boosting builds a sequence of shallow trees that iteratively correct residuals.
Try: Benchmark Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost on a complex tabular dataset with mixed categorical variables, measuring training throughput and AUROC.
Outcome: You can explain ensemble theory, tune modern GBDT libraries, and evaluate feature importances reliably.

M5.6 · Unsupervised Learning: Clustering, Dimensionality Reduction & Anomalies — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Partitioning & density-based clustering
- K-Means Clustering: Implement Lloyd's algorithm, K-Means++ probabilistic initialization, evaluate the elbow curve, and calculate silhouette coefficients.
- Density-Based Clustering: Implement DBSCAN, defining core points, border points, epsilon-neighborhoods, and MinPts; contrast against hierarchical agglomerative clustering.
Session 2 · Dimensionality reduction: PCA, t-SNE & UMAP
- Spectral & Manifold Learning: Derive Principal Component Analysis (PCA) from the covariance matrix; compare against t-SNE and UMAP for non-linear manifold projection.
- Anomaly Detection: Detect multivariate tabular outliers using Isolation Forests (measuring average path length in random trees), Local Outlier Factor, and One-Class SVM.
Mental model: Unsupervised algorithms discover latent structures, clusters, and low-dimensional manifolds in data without teacher guidance, generating hypotheses for scientific validation.
Try: Run DBSCAN and Isolation Forest on an unlabelled network intrusion dataset to isolate anomalous cyber-attack clusters and visualize them in a 2D UMAP projection.
Outcome: You can apply partitioning and density clustering, project high-dimensional manifolds, and detect anomalies in unlabelled data.

M5.7 · Capstone: Publication-Quality Empirical Benchmark: Linear Models vs SVM vs GBDT — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Benchmark protocol & compute-parity tuning
- Formulate an empirical research question: Under what tabular conditions (sample size, sparsity, non-linearity, categorical cardinality) do GBDTs significantly outperform tuned SVMs and regularized linear models?
- Execute nested cross-validation with Bayesian hyperparameter optimization (Optuna) under matched compute budgets across 5 diverse tabular datasets.
Session 2 · Statistical analysis, ablation & baseline paper report
- Compute AUROC, AUPRC, calibration error (ECE), and Brier score; perform paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across 10 random seeds with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction.
- Write a formal 6-page research paper detailing experimental setup, baseline configurations, ablation of tree depth and shrinkage, and discussion of failure modes.
Final result: A publication-quality empirical benchmark paper and code repository establishing rigorous classical ML baselines with statistical significance tests.
