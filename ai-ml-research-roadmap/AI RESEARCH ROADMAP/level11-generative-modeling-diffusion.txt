Level 11 · Generative Modeling: VAEs, GANs & Diffusion Theory

Scope: explore the theoretical and mathematical foundations of deep generative modeling: variational inference, generative adversarial networks, score-based models, stochastic differential equations (SDEs), and continuous-time flow matching.

Prerequisites: Level 10

M11.1 · Variational Autoencoders & The Evidence Lower Bound — (sessions: 3)

Formulate generative modeling as estimating the true data distribution p_data(x) and sampling novel instances x ~ p_theta(x).
Analyze the intractability of marginal likelihood p_theta(x) = \int p_theta(x, z) dz in high-dimensional latent variable models.
Derive the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) mathematically using Jensen's inequality: log p_theta(x) >= E_q[log p_theta(x|z)] - D_KL(q_phi(z|x) || p(z)).
Decompose ELBO into reconstruction loss and latent KL divergence regularization against a standard isotropic Gaussian prior N(0, I).
Master the Reparameterization Trick (Kingma & Welling): z = mu_phi(x) + sigma_phi(x) \odot epsilon, where epsilon ~ N(0, I), enabling backpropagation through stochastic nodes.
Analyze posterior collapse: when the decoder ignores latent variables z and acts as an unconditional autoregressive model.
Explore Hierarchical and Vector-Quantized VAEs (VQ-VAE, Van den Oord et al.): discrete latent representations and codebook learning without posterior collapse.
Outcome: derive the analytical KL divergence between two multivariate Gaussian distributions and implement a VAE with the reparameterization trick in PyTorch.

M11.2 · Generative Adversarial Networks & Minimax Game Theory — (sessions: 3)

Formulate GANs as a two-player zero-sum minimax game: min_G max_D V(D, G) = E_{x~p_data}[log D(x)] + E_{z~p_z}[log(1 - D(G(z)))].
Prove mathematically that the optimal discriminator for any fixed generator G is D^*(x) = p_data(x) / (p_data(x) + p_g(x)).
Prove that substituting D^*(x) into the minimax objective reduces the generator loss to the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD): 2 * JSD(p_data || p_g) - 2 * log(2).
Analyze GAN Training Pathologies: vanishing gradients with saturated discriminators, non-convergence in non-convex game dynamics, and Mode Collapse.
Study Wasserstein GANs (WGAN, Arjovsky et al.): using Earth Mover's Distance (Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality), 1-Lipschitz continuity constraints, and Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP).
Outcome: prove that the global minimum of the minimax GAN objective occurs if and only if p_g = p_data, and implement WGAN with gradient penalty.

M11.3 · Score-Based Generative Modeling & Denoising Diffusion (DDPM) — (sessions: 4)

Understand Score Matching (Hyvarinen): modeling the score function of data distribution: s_theta(x) = \nabla_x log p(x) without computing intractable partition functions Z.
Analyze Langevin Dynamics: sampling from a distribution using its score function via gradient ascent with Gaussian noise perturbations.
Master Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM, Sohl-Dickstein et al., Ho et al.): forward noising process q(x_t|x_{t-1}) and reverse denoising process p_theta(x_{t-1}|x_t).
Derive the closed-form forward marginal q(x_t|x_0) = N(x_t; sqrt(alpha_bar_t) * x_0, (1 - alpha_bar_t) * I) using Gaussian reproductive properties.
Derive the simplified DDPM training objective: predicting the injected Gaussian noise epsilon_theta(x_t, t) using L2 mean squared error loss ||epsilon - epsilon_theta(x_t, t)||^2.
Analyze Noise Schedules: linear, cosine (Nichol & Dhariwal), and continuous schedules, and their impact on sample quality and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
Outcome: derive the closed-form forward sampling equation q(x_t|x_0) and implement an end-to-end DDPM training and sampling loop in PyTorch.

M11.4 · Continuous SDEs & Probability Flow ODEs — (sessions: 4)

Formulate the continuous-time generalization of diffusion models using Stochastic Differential Equations (Score-based SDEs, Song et al. 2020).
Analyze Forward Itô SDEs: dx = f(x, t) dt + g(t) dw, transforming complex data distributions into isotropic Gaussian noise.
Master Anderson's Reverse-Time SDE Theorem: dx = [f(x, t) - g(t)^2 * \nabla_x log p_t(x)] dt + g(t) dw_bar, proving reversal is an SDE guided by the score function.
Derive the Probability Flow ODE: deterministic ordinary differential equation sharing the exact same marginal probability trajectories p_t(x) as the reverse SDE.
Understand the benefits of the Probability Flow ODE: deterministic sampling, fast numerical ODE solvers (RK4, Euler), exact likelihood computation via instantaneous change of variables, and latent space inversion.
Analyze Accelerated Samplers: DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models, Song et al.), DPM-Solver (Lu et al.), reducing sampling steps from 1000 down to 15-20 steps.
Outcome: formulate the probability flow ODE for a variance-preserving (VP) SDE and implement a DDIM deterministic sampler in PyTorch.

M11.5 · Flow Matching & Optimal Transport in Latent Spaces — (sessions: 4)

Understand Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs): learning time-dependent vector fields v_t(x) that transport a simple base distribution (e.g. standard Gaussian) to a target data distribution.
Analyze the intractability of standard CNF maximum likelihood: requiring expensive ODE integration during every training step.
Master Flow Matching (Lipman et al., Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden): training neural vector fields directly on conditional probability paths via simple regression without ODE simulation.
Analyze Optimal Transport Flow Matching (OT-FM): straight-line displacement paths between noise x_0 and data x_1: x_t = (1 - t) * x_0 + t * x_1 with constant target velocity u_t(x|x_1, x_0) = x_1 - x_0.
Prove that straight-line probability trajectories minimize kinetic energy, leading to significantly simpler ODE trajectories that can be solved in as few as 4-8 integration steps.
Examine Latent Diffusion Models (LDM / Stable Diffusion, Rombach et al.): compressing high-dimensional images into lower-dimensional latent spaces using a pre-trained VAE before applying diffusion / flow matching.
Study Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG, Ho & Salimans): interpolating between unconditional and conditional vector fields to dramatically improve generation fidelity and prompt adherence.
Outcome: implement Optimal Transport Flow Matching from scratch and train a flow model on 2D synthetic distributions and images with straight-line trajectories.

M11.6 · Capstone: Implement a Continuous Flow Matching Model from Mathematical First Principles — (sessions: 2)

Build and train a continuous-time Flow Matching generative model from mathematical scratch on a dataset of images or complex scientific multi-modal trajectories.
Implement the Optimal Transport interpolation path: x_t = (1 - (1 - sigma_min) * t) * x_0 + t * x_1, and derive the ground-truth target velocity field u_t(x_t|x_0, x_1).
Implement a U-Net or Diffusion Transformer (DiT, Peebles & Xie) architecture to parameterize the time-dependent velocity field v_theta(x, t).
Integrate adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) conditioning on timestep t and class / text embeddings.
Train the model by regressing v_theta(x_t, t) onto target velocity u_t using simple mean squared error loss with zero ODE simulation during training.
Implement numerical ODE integration samplers (Euler method, Midpoint method, and Runge-Kutta 4th order RK4) to generate samples at inference time.
Implement Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) within the vector field sampling loop with guidance scale parameter w.
Evaluate generation quality quantitatively: compute Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and measure integration step requirements (evaluating performance at 4, 10, 25, and 50 steps).
Draft an academic research paper comparing Flow Matching against DDPM in terms of training stability, trajectory curvature, and sample generation speed.
Final result: a verified Flow Matching generative model codebase, sampling notebook, and academic paper dissecting optimal transport trajectories.

Level 11 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Derive the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) mathematically using Jensen's inequality.
Explain why the reparameterization trick enables gradient backpropagation through stochastic latent nodes.
Prove that the global optimum of the original GAN objective is achieved when the generator distribution equals the data distribution.
Explain mode collapse in GANs and how Wasserstein distance (WGAN-GP) improves training stability.
Derive the closed-form forward sampling formula q(x_t|x_0) for Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM).
Explain the difference between score matching and likelihood-based generative modeling.
Formulate diffusion models as continuous Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) and reverse-time SDEs.
Derive the Probability Flow ODE and explain how it enables deterministic sampling and exact likelihood computation.
Explain the mathematical formulation of Flow Matching and why it avoids ODE simulation during training.
Prove that Optimal Transport Flow Matching produces straight-line paths with minimal kinetic energy.
Implement Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and explain its effect on sample fidelity and diversity.
Build a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture with adaptive layer normalization conditioning.
Evaluate generative models using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS).
Progression: VAEs & ELBO Foundations → GANs & Wasserstein Minimax → DDPM & Score Matching → Continuous SDEs & Probability Flow ODEs → Flow Matching & Optimal Transport → Continuous Flow Matching Capstone.
