Level 11 · Explainability, Interpretability & Trustworthy AI

Phase: HPC Clusters, Scaling & Explainability
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 10 (Foundation Models, Fine-Tuning & Domain Specialization)
Scope: Unpack the internal mechanisms of black-box models to satisfy scientific peer review and regulatory standards (FDA clinical AI clearance, EU AI Act): feature attribution (SHAP, Integrated Gradients, Grad-CAM for medical scans), mechanistic interpretability (linear representation probing, activation patching, attention head visualization), sparse autoencoders (SAEs) for monosemantic feature discovery, and counterfactual explanation generation for clinical diagnostic and cybersecurity triage decisions.

M11.1 · Feature Attribution: SHAP, Integrated Gradients & Saliency — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Cooperative Game Theory & Shapley Values (SHAP)
- Master the axiomatic foundations of Shapley values: efficiency, symmetry, dummy player, and additivity in cooperative game theory.
- Implement KernelSHAP for black-box models and TreeSHAP (Lundberg et al.) for high-speed exact attribution in gradient boosted trees.
- Apply SHAP to clinical tabular models (identifying patient risk factors for sepsis) and cybersecurity intrusion classifiers (pinpointing anomalous packet flags).
Session 2 · Path Attribution & Gradient-Based Saliency
- Analyze the failure of vanilla gradient saliency maps: gradient saturation, threshold sensitivity, and lack of implementation invariance.
- Implement Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al.): accumulating path integrals from a neutral baseline to the input to satisfy Completeness and Implementation Invariance.
- Apply Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++ to convolutional and vision Transformer architectures, visualizing spatial attention maps on clinical radiographs.
Mental model: Integrated Gradients traces a straight-line interpolation between a neutral baseline and the input, measuring how every feature contributes to the final logit.
Try: Compute Integrated Gradients on a medical image classifier in PyTorch, verify that attributions sum to the difference between output and baseline logits, and plot the heatmap.
Outcome: Compute theoretically grounded feature attributions (SHAP, Integrated Gradients) across tabular and imaging models to satisfy clinical and security standards.

M11.2 · Linear Representation Probing & Latent Geometry — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Linear Probes & Concept Emergence
- Understand representation probing: training simple linear classifiers on intermediate hidden activations to test whether specific concepts are linearly represented.
- Probe deep neural networks for medical concepts (e.g., patient age, smoker status) or cybersecurity concepts (e.g., protocol types, attack family) across sequential layers.
- Map the trajectory of concept emergence: demonstrating how low-level superficial features peak in early layers while high-level semantic concepts form in late layers.
Session 2 · Probe Sanity Checks & Control Tasks
- Avoid the "probing trap": distinguishing whether a probe is discovering a representation learned by the model or merely memorizing the task itself.
- Implement Control Tasks (Hewitt & Liang): evaluating linear probes against randomized control labels to measure probe selectivity.
- Analyze representation geometry: computing representation similarity matrices (Centered Kernel Alignment CKA) to compare latent spaces across model checkpoints.
Mental model: A linear probe is a scientific stethoscope listening to hidden activations; if a concept cannot be extracted linearly, the model has not explicitly disentangled it.
Try: Extract hidden states across all layers of a pre-trained Transformer on clinical notes, train linear probes for mortality prediction, and plot probe accuracy across depth.
Outcome: Execute rigorous linear probing experiments, evaluate concept emergence across layer depth, and validate probe selectivity using control tasks.

M11.3 · Mechanistic Interpretability: Activation Patching & Circuits — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Causal Tracing & Activation Patching
- Master the paradigm of mechanistic interpretability: reverse-engineering neural networks into understandable computational algorithms and functional circuits.
- Implement activation patching (causal mediation analysis): swapping intermediate activations between clean and corrupted inputs to isolate causal components.
- Locate factual knowledge and reasoning circuits in Transformers: identifying specific MLP layers or attention heads responsible for specific predictions.
Session 2 · Circuit Discovery & Attention Head Specialization
- Analyze attention head specialization: identifying Induction Heads (responsible for in-context pattern copying), Duplicate Token Heads, and Inhibition Heads.
- Map end-to-end computational subgraphs (circuits) within models performing specific domain logic (e.g., matching clinical symptoms to prescription decisions).
- Test circuit completeness: verifying that pruning all neurons and attention heads outside the discovered circuit preserves task performance.
Mental model: Mechanistic interpretability treats neural networks like biological brains, using targeted activation lesions to identify causal cognitive circuits.
Try: Perform activation patching on a small Transformer model solving an algorithmic task, isolating the exact attention heads that perform in-context copying.
Outcome: Trace causal computational circuits in neural networks using activation patching and identify specialized attention head mechanisms.

M11.4 · Dictionary Learning & Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Polysemanticity Crisis in Neural Representations
- Understand polysemanticity: individual neurons activate for multiple completely unrelated concepts due to superposition (compressing more concepts than dimensions).
- Formalize the superposition hypothesis: neural networks utilize non-orthogonal almost-independent direction vectors in high-dimensional vector spaces.
- Contrast polysemantic biological neurons with the goal of isolating pure, monosemantic research features.
Session 2 · Training Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)
- Architect an overcomplete Sparse Autoencoder (SAE): expanding intermediate hidden states into a higher-dimensional dictionary (e.g., 8x to 32x hidden dimension).
- Enforce strict sparsity: training SAEs with L1 regularization penalties or modern TopK activation functions to ensure only a handful of dictionary features fire per token.
- Uncover interpretable monosemantic features: discovering individual dictionary latent directions corresponding to clinical disease concepts or malicious shellcode strings.
Mental model: Sparse autoencoders untangle the entangled web of polysemantic neurons into an expanded dictionary of clean, interpretable monosemantic concepts.
Try: Train a small Sparse Autoencoder on the intermediate residual stream of a Transformer, enforce TopK sparsity, and inspect the top-activating dataset tokens for learned latents.
Outcome: Train overcomplete Sparse Autoencoders, resolve polysemantic superposition, and discover monosemantic interpretable features in foundation models.

M11.5 · Counterfactual Explanations & Algorithmic Recourse — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Formulating Counterfactual Optimization
- Formulate counterfactual explanations from first principles: finding the smallest perturbation delta applied to input X such that the model prediction flips from class Y to Y*.
- Balance competing objectives: minimizing input distance (L1/L2 norm), maximizing target class probability, and enforcing domain plausibility constraints.
- Prevent invalid recourse: ensuring immutable features (e.g., patient biological age, system hardware UUIDs) cannot be modified in counterfactual recommendations.
Session 2 · Domain Plausibility in Healthcare & Cybersecurity
- Enforce manifold constraints: ensuring counterfactual clinical patient states remain on the valid physiological manifold (e.g., blood pressure cannot be zero while alive).
- Generate counterfactual cyber incident explanations: demonstrating the minimum packet modifications required to transform an intrusion alert into benign traffic.
- Present recourse recommendations: providing actionable, human-interpretable guidance for physicians evaluating automated diagnostic alerts or security analysts triaging alarms.
Mental model: A counterfactual answer does not just explain why a model made a decision; it shows what must change in the real world to alter the outcome.
Try: Implement a counterfactual optimization algorithm using gradient descent on a clinical tabular classifier, finding the minimal actionable lifestyle change to reduce cardiac risk.
Outcome: Formulate and optimize plausible counterfactual explanations with domain-specific feasibility constraints for healthcare and cybersecurity applications.

Session 2 · Circuit Dissection, Counterfactuals & Peer-Review Documentation

Mental model: In high-stakes domains, interpretability is not an optional post-hoc visualization; it is the scientific prerequisite for real-world clinical and security deployment.
Try: Run the full interpretability audit on a trained model, extracting feature attributions, probing representations, and generating a 4-page scientific interpretability report.
Outcome: Deliver an exhaustive, peer-reviewed explainability audit combining feature attribution, linear probing, and mechanistic circuit analysis.
