Level 11 · Mechanistic Interpretability & Model Analysis

Phase: HPC, Scaling & Interpretability
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 10 (Pre-Training Dynamics, Scaling Laws & Empirical Science)
Scope: Master the scientific discipline of mechanistic interpretability: reverse-engineering neural network representations, probing internal latent spaces, circuit discovery (induction heads), disentangling superposition with Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), causal activation patching, and representation steering.

M11.1 · The Linear Representation Hypothesis & Latent Space Probing — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Linear Representation Hypothesis
- Formulate the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., Elhage et al.): high-level human concepts are encoded as linear directions in activation spaces.
- Implement linear diagnostic probes: train linear classifiers on intermediate hidden activations to predict semantic, syntactic, and factual properties.
- Control for probe memorization: compare linear probe accuracy against non-linear probes (MLPs) and randomized baseline controls (Hewitt & Liang selectivity).
Session 2 · Geometry & Representation Similarity (CKA)
- Analyze the geometric alignment of hidden representations across distinct layers, models, and training checkpoints.
- Implement Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) using linear and radial basis function (RBF) kernels to quantify representational similarity invariance.
- Use Singular Value Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA) to isolate the intrinsic dimensionality of learned representations.
Mental model: The activation space of a neural network is a high-dimensional concept space; linear vectors act as geometric coordinates for abstract ideas.
Try: Train a linear probe on the residual stream activations of a language model to extract grammatical tense, verifying high probe selectivity against random controls.
Outcome: Probe internal representations rigorously, test the linear representation hypothesis, and measure representational similarity using CKA.

M11.2 · Circuit Discovery: Residual Stream Decomposition & Induction Head Identification — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Mathematical Framework of Transformer Circuits
- Understand the Transformer as a residual stream communication channel where attention heads and MLPs write additive linear updates.
- Deconstruct the multi-head attention operation into independent QK (query-key addressing) and OV (output-value transformation) circuits (Elhage et al.).
- Trace information flow mathematically: compute direct, indirect, and mediated path contributions to the final vocabulary projection logits.
Session 2 · Induction Heads & In-Context Learning Circuits
- Analyze the mechanistic discovery of induction heads (Olsson et al., 2022): two-head circuits that implement pattern completion [A][B] ... [A] -> [B].
- Write automated induction head detectors that measure attention score spikes on repeated random token sequences across all layer-head pairs.
- Trace the emergence of induction heads during pre-training: correlate their formation with the phase shift in in-context learning loss curves.
Mental model: Induction heads are the fundamental algorithmic microprocessors of in-context learning, copying historical patterns into future predictions.
Try: Implement an automated induction head detection script in PyTorch/TransformerLens, scan a model, and identify the exact layer and head indices responsible.
Outcome: Decompose the Transformer residual stream into mathematical circuits and mechanically identify induction heads responsible for in-context learning.

M11.3 · Disentangling Superposition: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) & Dictionary Learning — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Problem of Polysemanticity & Superposition
- Formulate the superposition hypothesis: neural networks represent more features than they have dimensions by packing non-orthogonal directions into activations.
- Understand polysemanticity: individual neurons activate for multiple unrelated semantic concepts, preventing clean single-neuron interpretation.
- Analyze the geometry of almost-orthogonal vectors in high dimensions bounded by the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma.
Session 2 · Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) & Monosemantic Dictionary Learning
- Implement Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) with overcomplete hidden layers (e.g., 8x to 32x expansion) and L1 sparsity regularization penalties.
- Train an SAE on intermediate residual stream activations to disentangle superposed representations into thousands of sparse, monosemantic latent features.
- Evaluate SAE quality: compute the L0 sparsity norm (average active latents per token) and reconstruction loss percentage on downstream evaluation benchmarks.
Mental model: Superposition packs a crowded city into a small suitcase; an SAE unpacks the suitcase, giving every item its own dedicated hanger.
Try: Train an SAE on hidden residual activations, evaluate L0 sparsity and reconstruction fidelity, and extract monosemantic feature interpretations.
Outcome: Disentangle polysemantic representations using Sparse Autoencoders, extract monosemantic feature dictionaries, and measure L0 sparsity.

M11.4 · Causal Tracing & Activation Patching: Localizing Factual Recall — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Causal Tracing & Mediation Analysis
- Differentiate between passive observational correlation (probing) versus active causal intervention in neural network representations.
- Implement causal mediation analysis and activation patching (Meng et al., ROME): corrupting input embeddings with noise and restoring clean activations.
- Localize factual knowledge storage: identify specific early MLP layers and late attention heads that causally mediate factual subject-relation recall.
Session 2 · Direct Knowledge Editing & Mechanistic Limits
- Formulate Rank-One Model Editing (ROME) and MEMIT: computing optimal closed-form updates to MLP weight matrices to update specific factual associations.
- Measure the generalization and specificity of mechanistic model edits: verify that editing "Eiffel Tower -> Rome" does not corrupt general knowledge about Paris.
- Analyze the limitations of direct weight editing: unintended collateral damage, distortion of adjacent concepts, and catastrophic forgetting.
Mental model: Activation patching performs surgical neurosurgery on running neural networks to pinpoint the exact circuit that stores a specific memory.
Try: Implement activation patching on a factual query ("The capital of France is [Paris]"), localizing the exact MLP layer that restores the clean output from noise.
Outcome: Execute causal activation patching to localize model capabilities and analyze direct weight editing mechanisms and limitations.

M11.5 · Representation Engineering (RepE): Contrastive Activation Vectors & Steering — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Reading Latent Cognitive States
- Formalize Representation Engineering (RepE, Zou et al.): directly inspecting and manipulating the top-down cognitive states of models during generation.
- Extract concept direction vectors by computing contrastive activation differences across paired prompts (e.g., honest vs. dishonest, sycophantic vs. factual).
- Apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to contrastive activation sets to identify the principal axis of the target concept in the residual stream.
Session 2 · Activation Steering & Behavioral Control
- Implement runtime activation steering: add or subtract the scaled concept vector v directly to the residual stream: h_l' = h_l + alpha * v.
- Compare activation steering with prompt engineering: evaluate robustness against adversarial prompt jailbreaks, token overhead, and stability over long contexts.
- Analyze the alignment tax of excessive steering: monitor the degradation of grammatical fluency and reasoning ability under high steering coefficients alpha.
Mental model: Prompting talks to the model through its ears; activation steering reaches directly into its brain to tilt its internal thought compass.
Try: Extract a "truthfulness" concept vector from contrastive pairs, inject it during inference with varying alpha coefficients, and measure the reduction in hallucination.
Outcome: Extract contrastive concept vectors, steer model generation behavior directly at the latent representation level, and quantify steering trade-offs.

M11.6 · Capstone Project: Mechanistic Circuit Discovery & Activation Steering Probe — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Circuit Isolation & Causal Mediation Pipeline
- Construct an automated mechanistic analysis harness in PyTorch (leveraging TransformerLens or native hooks) targeting a specific reasoning behavior.
- Execute causal activation patching across all layers and token positions to map the exact computational circuit responsible for the target task.
- Train an overcomplete Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) on the critical bottleneck layer, extracting monosemantic feature directions with verified high sparsity.
Session 2 · Activation Steering & Empirical Verification
- Formulate contrastive concept vectors along the identified circuit pathways and implement real-time activation steering hooks during generation.
- Evaluate behavioral control: demonstrate statistically significant suppression of undesirable outputs (e.g., sycophancy or bias) without degrading perplexity.
- Author an academic mechanistic interpretability paper in LaTeX complete with interactive circuit diagrams, activation heatmaps, and ablation tables.
Mental model: Rigorous mechanistic interpretability transforms the black box into a transparent, mathematically verifiable computing circuit.
Try: Discover the complete indirect object identification circuit in a model, extract steering vectors, and author a publication-ready empirical report.
Outcome: Execute publication-grade mechanistic interpretability investigations, localize causal circuits, and deploy representation steering controls.
