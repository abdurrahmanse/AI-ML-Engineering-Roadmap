Level 6 · Deep Learning & Reproduction

Phase: Data & Modeling
Suggested duration: 36 sessions (~9 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 5
Scope: Master neural network architectures from first-principles backpropagation in NumPy to PyTorch autograd, optimization dynamics, convolutional backbones, vision transformers, rigorous paper reproduction, and the specific methodology of reproducing LLM fine-tuning and PEFT research.

M6.1 · Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) & Analytical Backpropagation — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Multi-Layer Perceptrons & non-linear activation functions
- Architecture Anatomy: Formulate the Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) forward pass as composition of parameterized affine transformations and non-linear activation functions.
- Activation Analysis: Analyze mathematical gradients, saturation regions, and dying neuron problems across Sigmoid, Tanh, ReLU, LeakyReLU, GELU, and Swish/SiLU.
Session 2 · Analytical backpropagation derivation & pure-NumPy engine
- Matrix Gradient Derivation: Derive the analytical backpropagation equations for a deep MLP with cross-entropy loss using the chain rule and Jacobian-vector products.
- From-Scratch Implementation: Code a complete deep neural network training loop in pure NumPy without deep learning frameworks, verifying gradients via finite differences.
Mental model: Neural networks are universal function approximators whose parameters are adjusted via recursive chain-rule error signals flowing backward through the computational graph.
Try: Implement a 3-layer MLP in pure NumPy with backpropagation and train it to classify handwritten digits (MNIST) to >97% accuracy without using PyTorch.
Outcome: You can derive and implement the forward and backward passes of neural networks from scratch and analyze non-linear activation dynamics.

M6.2 · Deep Learning Training Dynamics, Normalization & Regularization — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Weight initialization & gradient preservation
- Variance Preservation: Derive Xavier/Glorot initialization for symmetric activations and He/Kaiming initialization for ReLU to prevent vanishing/exploding variance across layers.
- Initialization Diagnostics: Track activation distributions and gradient norms across layer depth during step 0 of training to diagnose initialization failures.
Session 2 · Normalization layers & internal covariate shift
- Normalization Mechanics: Formulate Batch Normalization (tracking mini-batch statistics vs running evaluation buffers), Layer Normalization, and Group Normalization.
- Training Stability: Compare LayerNorm's invariance to weight re-scaling against BatchNorm's batch-size sensitivity and communication overhead in distributed settings.
Session 3 · Regularization & learning rate schedules
- Regularization Techniques: Implement inverted Dropout, Weight Decay (L2 penalty), Stochastic Depth (DropPath), and label smoothing to prevent overfitting.
- Optimization Schedules: Implement Cosine Annealing with linear warmup, OneCycleLR, ReduceLROnPlateau, and gradient norm clipping.
Mental model: Training deep neural networks is the stabilization of a high-dimensional dynamical system; proper initialization and normalization keep signal variances within healthy bounds.
Try: Intentionally corrupt an MLP's weight initialization to demonstrate gradient explosion, then resolve it with He initialization and Layer Normalization, tracking gradient flow.
Outcome: You can initialize deep architectures correctly, apply normalization layers, and configure resilient learning rate schedules.

M6.3 · Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) & Modern Vision Backbones — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Convolutions, spatial receptive fields & pooling
- Discrete Convolutions: Formulate 2D and 3D discrete cross-correlations, kernel weights, padding schemes (valid, same), stride, and dilation.
- Effective Receptive Field (ERF): Calculate the analytical and empirical receptive field of stacked convolutional layers and evaluate spatial downsampling via pooling.
Session 2 · Architectural evolution: AlexNet to ResNet & ConvNeXt
- Landmark CNN Paradigms: Trace architectural innovations from AlexNet and VGG to Inception (factorized 1x1 convolutions) and ResNet (residual skip connections).
- Residual Learning Theory: Prove mathematically why residual connections (y = F(x) + x) eliminate the degradation problem by creating gradient highways directly to early layers.
- Modern Modernization: Analyze ConvNeXt design choices (7x7 depthwise convolutions, inverted bottlenecks, LayerNorm) modernizing CNNs for modern benchmarks.
Mental model: Convolutions encode spatial inductive bias — translational equivariance and locality — allowing models to share parameters across image locations efficiently.
Try: Build a 18-layer ResNet from scratch in PyTorch, verify that removing residual connections causes training error to plateau prematurely, and visualize learned convolutional filters.
Outcome: You can calculate receptive fields, implement modern residual architectures, and explain spatial inductive biases.

M6.4 · Dense Visual Prediction & Vision Transformers (ViT) — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Dense computer vision: Detection & segmentation
- Semantic Segmentation: Formulate pixel-level classification, U-Net encoder-decoder skip connections, transposed convolutions, and dice loss / focal loss.
- Object Detection Paradigms: Compare anchor-based detection (Faster R-CNN, YOLO grid anchors) against anchor-free bipartite matching transformers (DETR).
Session 2 · Vision Transformers (ViT) & patch self-attention
- ViT Architecture: Deconstruct Vision Transformers: non-overlapping patch tokenization, linear patch projections, learnable [CLS] tokens, and 2D spatial position embeddings.
- Inductive Bias Trade-off: Contrast ViT's global self-attention (requiring large pretraining corpora) with CNN's spatial locality; evaluate hybrid CNN-Transformer backbones.
Mental model: Vision Transformers discard spatial convolutions in favor of global self-attention across image patches, allowing dynamic information routing across distant image regions.
Try: Train a small Vision Transformer and a ResNet backbone on equal compute budgets on CIFAR-100, comparing sample efficiency and attention rollout maps.
Outcome: You can implement semantic segmentation pipelines, evaluate object detection paradigms, and construct Vision Transformers.

M6.5 · Seminal Literature Reading & Scientific Reproduction Methodology — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Landmark vision paper deep-dive
- Paper Analysis: Study He et al. (Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition, CVPR 2016), Ronneberger et al. (U-Net, MICCAI 2015), and Dosovitskiy et al. (ViT, ICLR 2021).
- Research Questions: Investigate why residual networks train faster than plain networks of equal depth, and why ViTs exhibit broader effective receptive fields from layer 1.
Session 2 · Reproduction protocols & deviation logging
- Reproduction Discipline: Translate paper equations into code, verify official repositories, audit hyperparameter discrepancies, and record deviations in a formal log.
- Seed Variance & Hardware Discrepancies: Measure performance variance across 5 random seeds and quantify impact of mixed-precision (FP16/BF16) on convergence.
Mental model: Scientific reproduction is not blindly downloading a GitHub repo; it is the independent verification of claimed empirical phenomena from reported methodologies.
Try: Replicate Table 1 of the ResNet paper on a smaller dataset, recording all hyperparameter adaptations, training curves, and deviation notes in an open-science report.
Outcome: You can dissect landmark deep learning literature and execute formal reproduction studies with transparent deviation logs.

M6.6 · Capstone: PyTorch Deep Learning Baseline Reproduction & Ablation Report — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Deep learning baseline implementation & training
- Re-implement a published vision architecture (e.g. ResNet or U-Net) in PyTorch, matching reported data augmentations, optimizer settings, and loss formulations.
- Train the model to convergence across 5 random seeds on a standard benchmark dataset, logging loss curves, validation metrics, and GPU memory utilization.
Session 2 · Component ablation & scientific manuscript report
- Conduct a rigorous ablation study isolating three core components: residual connections, normalization type (BatchNorm vs LayerNorm), and learning rate schedule.
- Author a 6-page scientific reproduction report in LaTeX detailing methodology, empirical results tables with standard deviations, ablation analysis, and failure cases.
Final result: A fully verified, reproducible PyTorch baseline implementation and a 6-page scientific reproduction paper suitable for submission to a reproducibility track.

M6.7 · Reproducing LLM Fine-Tuning & PEFT Research — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Replicating LoRA and QLoRA papers from scratch
- LoRA Reproduction Protocol: Reproduce the core empirical finding from Hu et al. (LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models, 2021) on a small open model (e.g. Phi-2 or Mistral-7B): match reported rank-r configurations (r=4, r=8, r=16), target projection matrices (Q, K, V, MLP), and alpha scaling values; evaluate on the same benchmark type (GLUE or instruction-following) and compare your reproduced accuracy to the reported number.
- Sources of Irreproducibility in PEFT Papers: Identify and measure the three most common sources of PEFT reproduction failures: (1) chat template mismatch (the fine-tuned model received training examples formatted for a different chat template than the base model was trained with), (2) random seed variance across initialization of the adapter matrices, and (3) hardware-dependent numerical precision differences between A100 and V100/T4 GPUs under BF16 vs FP16 training.
Session 2 · Honest variance reporting & failed reproductions
- Multi-Seed Variance Protocol: Execute the same LoRA fine-tuning run across 5 different random seeds, reporting mean and standard deviation of the final evaluation metric — understanding why single-seed results reported in many PEFT papers are scientifically unreliable.
- Documenting Failed Reproductions: Follow the ML Reproducibility Challenge convention of documenting reproduction failures honestly: when your reproduced numbers differ from the paper's reported numbers, investigate and document each hypothesis for the gap (different data splits, unreported preprocessing, model version differences, evaluation prompt formatting differences) without fabricating results.
- Instruction Tuning Data Quality Research: Design an experiment testing how data quality affects instruction fine-tuning outcome: compare fine-tuning on 1,000 high-quality human-written examples versus 10,000 synthetically generated examples using GPT-4, measuring downstream instruction-following quality on the AlpacaEval or MT-Bench benchmark.
Mental model: Reproducing a PEFT paper is harder than it looks because most papers omit the chat template, the exact data preprocessing, and the precise evaluation prompt format — the three details that matter most for getting the numbers right.
Try: Reproduce the central table of any LoRA paper using a Llama-3 or Phi-2 base model on a publicly available instruction-tuning dataset, document every deviation from the paper's described procedure, and report honest confidence intervals across 3 random seeds.
Outcome: You can execute disciplined LoRA and QLoRA paper reproductions, identify the most common sources of PEFT irreproducibility, and report variance-aware results that meet scientific standards.
