Level 6 · Deep Learning Foundations

Phase: Data Curation & Modeling Paradigms
Suggested duration: 30 sessions (~7 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 5
Scope: Understand neural networks from tensor operations through training, regularization, debugging, and responsible use of pretrained models.

M6.1 · Neural Networks and Automatic Differentiation — (sessions: 5)
- Implement affine layers, activations, losses, backpropagation, and minibatch training.
- Trace tensor shapes, device placement, gradients, and parameter updates in PyTorch.
- Explain initialization, normalization, dropout, weight decay, and overfitting.
- Track initialization, optimizer settings, learning curves, checkpoints, and failed runs so training behavior is inspectable.
- Reproduce a published neural result from its equations and implementation, then report deviations and remaining uncertainty.
Mental model: A neural network is a parameterized function whose behavior is learned through repeated error signals.
Try: Implement a small multilayer perceptron twice: once with NumPy gradients and once with PyTorch autograd.
Outcome: You can inspect and debug the complete training loop of a neural network.

M6.2 · Computer Vision and Convolutional Models — (sessions: 5)
- Explain convolution, receptive fields, pooling, augmentation, residual connections, and transfer learning.
- Build image classification and segmentation pipelines with correct spatial transforms and evaluation.
- Inspect saliency and failure cases without treating visual explanations as causal proof.
- Track initialization, optimizer settings, learning curves, checkpoints, and failed runs so training behavior is inspectable.
- Reproduce a published neural result from its equations and implementation, then report deviations and remaining uncertainty.
Mental model: Convolutions encode local structure, while data and labels determine what the model learns.
Try: Compare a scratch CNN and a pretrained backbone on a small image dataset with a documented error taxonomy.
Outcome: You can build and evaluate reproducible computer-vision baselines.

M6.3 · Training Engineering and Debugging — (sessions: 5)
- Use learning-rate schedules, mixed precision, gradient clipping, checkpoint recovery, and early stopping.
- Detect underfitting, overfitting, data bugs, unstable loss, and silent label or preprocessing errors.
- Profile memory and throughput so training choices are tied to measured bottlenecks.
- Track initialization, optimizer settings, learning curves, checkpoints, and failed runs so training behavior is inspectable.
- Reproduce a published neural result from its equations and implementation, then report deviations and remaining uncertainty.
Mental model: Most failed experiments are diagnosed by checking data, shapes, targets, and instrumentation before changing architecture.
Try: Intentionally introduce three training bugs, diagnose them from logs, and document the fixes.
Outcome: You can run reliable neural-network experiments and explain why a training run succeeded or failed.

M6.4 · Initialization, Normalization and Optimization Dynamics — (sessions: 3)
- Diagnose initialization, gradient flow, normalization, optimizer, batch-size, and learning-rate effects.
- Read learning curves and activation statistics to distinguish underfitting, overfitting, instability, and data bugs.
Mental model: Training curves are measurements of a dynamical system, not just progress indicators.
Try: Create controlled failures and use logged evidence to identify their causes.
Outcome: You can debug neural training from observable behavior.

M6.5 · Reproduction Engineering and Ablation Hygiene — (sessions: 3)
- Translate a paper specification into code, configs, tests, checkpoints, and a documented deviation log.
- Reproduce a result across seeds and isolate one implementation choice at a time.
Mental model: Reproduction earns trust by exposing differences, not by forcing agreement with a headline number.
Try: Reproduce one table from a paper and publish the exact commands and deviations.
Outcome: You can turn a neural paper into an inspectable reproduction study.

M6.6 · Capstone: Deep Learning Reproduction — (sessions: 9)
- Reimplement a published neural baseline from its paper and public code.
- Match its data split, preprocessing, metric, training budget, and reported uncertainty as closely as possible.
- Explain every mismatch and propose one follow-up experiment that would meaningfully test the method.
Final result: A reproducibility report demonstrating implementation skill, scientific skepticism, and transparent comparison.
