Level 1 · Research Problem Formulation, Mentorship & Lab Onboarding

Phase: Research Foundations & Lab Onboarding
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 0 (Scientific Computing, Research Tooling & Reproducibility)
Scope: Master the essential path of entering an active AI/ML research laboratory as an aspiring researcher: identifying active research groups and Principal Investigators (PIs), analyzing recent laboratory publications, formulating tailored research proposals, executing high-signal researcher outreach, passing technical Research Assistant (RA) screening, and establishing productive collaborative laboratory workflows.

M1.1 · Mapping Research Laboratories, PIs & Active Research Trajectories — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Discovering Research Labs & Active Grants
- Survey AI research institutes, national laboratories, and corporate research labs (e.g., DeepMind, Meta FAIR, Microsoft Research) in your area of interest.
- Inspect Principal Investigator (PI) profiles: evaluate recent grant funding (NSF, ERC, DARPA, NIH, industry gifts), student graduation records, and lab size.
- Analyze laboratory publication velocity: identify whether a lab publishes primarily in top-tier conferences (NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, ACL, KDD) or specialized journals.
Session 2 · Evaluating Laboratory Culture & Mentorship Styles
- Distinguish between advisory styles: hands-on junior group leaders versus hands-off senior research directors.
- Connect with current doctoral researchers and postdocs in the target lab to inquire about mentorship availability, computational resources, and project autonomy.
- Identify active multi-disciplinary collaborations (e.g., computer science labs collaborating with medical hospitals, oncology centers, or cybersecurity defense centers).
Mental model: Joining a lab is an intellectual apprenticeship; choosing a mentor who actively engages and has compute resources matters more than institutional branding.
Try: Build a research spreadsheet profiling 10 research laboratories in your target domain, noting their PI, recent top-tier papers, active grants, and student ratio.
Outcome: Systematically identify high-impact research laboratories and select scientific mentors aligned with your research ambitions.

M1.2 · Reading Laboratory Publications & Formulating Tailored Research Pitches — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Deep Dissection of Laboratory Publications
- Select the 3 most recent conference or journal papers published by the target PI and their senior doctoral researchers.
- Analyze the "Limitations and Future Work" sections of these papers to identify immediate, unresolved problems the lab is currently investigating.
- Formulate a precise understanding of the lab's technical stack, baseline benchmarks, and primary methodological paradigms.
Session 2 · Formulating a Targeted Research Pitch
- Develop a 1-page research pitch proposing a concrete extension or solution to an open problem identified in the lab’s recent work.
- Frame the pitch with clear benefits to the lab: how your technical skills (e.g., PyTorch engineering, CUDA profiling, dataset curation) can accelerate their active project.
- Connect the proposal to domain-specific datasets (e.g., applying their model to MIMIC-IV clinical records or network intrusion telemetry).
Mental model: A generic inquiry asks for an opportunity; a tailored research pitch demonstrates that you are already contributing solutions to their active problems.
Try: Read two recent papers from a target research group, identify an unaddressed limitation, and write a concise 3-paragraph research extension proposal.
Outcome: Deconstruct prospective mentors' papers and formulate high-value research pitches that demonstrate immediate intellectual compatibility.

M1.3 · High-Signal Research Outreach & Scientific Mentorship Proposals — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Anatomy of a High-Signal Cold Outreach
- Analyze why research directors ignore 95% of generic student emails (lengthy generic CV dumps, copy-pasted flattery, vague requests for "any project").
- Master the concise 4-paragraph email structure: who you are, specific citation of their recent work, proposed technical contribution, and polite call to action.
- Craft professional, high-signal subject lines that stand out in crowded inboxes (e.g., "Prospective RA: Extending [Paper Title] with [Method]").
Session 2 · Timing, Persistence & Professional Research Etiquette
- Optimize outreach timing around major conference submission deadlines (avoiding the week before NeurIPS/ICML submissions or major grant deadlines).
- Execute respectful, value-additive follow-up sequences after 7 to 10 days if no response is received, sharing a new insight or reproduction result.
- Leverage open scientific webinars, reading groups, and virtual workshops to initiate meaningful technical conversations.
Mental model: Research leads are starved for time but hungry for self-directed, technically capable researchers who take initiative without constant hand-holding.
Try: Draft a 200-word cold outreach email to a target laboratory lead referencing a specific equation or figure from their latest paper, followed by a polite follow-up template.
Outcome: Author high-signal research outreach emails that secure technical interviews and avoid common applicant blunders.

M1.4 · Developing an Academic Research CV & Demonstrating Technical Proof-of-Work — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Academic Curriculum Vitae (CV) Structure
- Format a professional academic CV in LaTeX using clean typography (avoiding colorful graphic design templates used for corporate resumes).
- Structure standard academic sections: Education, Research Interests, Publications/Preprints, Research Experience, Open-Source Contributions, and Technical Skills.
- Highlight quantitative impact in past projects: benchmark scores, parameter scales, datasets curated, and reproducibility verification.
Session 2 · Scientific Proof-of-Work & GitHub Portfolios
- Curate a research-oriented GitHub profile featuring clean reproductions of published papers, modular PyTorch implementations, and reproducible notebooks.
- Include interactive Weights & Biases experiment dashboards and Google Colab demonstrations directly linked from project README files.
- Document independent projects and exploratory studies as ArXiv-formatted technical reports rather than basic scripts.
Mental model: An academic CV establishes your credentials; a public, reproducible GitHub implementation provides indisputable proof of your research competence.
Try: Typeset your academic CV in LaTeX using a clean academic template, linking a public GitHub repository that reproduces a published ML baseline.
Outcome: Build a stellar academic CV and proof-of-work portfolio that immediately distinguishes you from hundreds of applicant candidates.

M1.5 · Passing the Research Assistant (RA) & Technical Lab Screening — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Technical Coding & PyTorch Architecture Tests
- Prepare for standard lab screening technical rounds: live implementation of neural network layers, custom loss functions, and data pipelines in PyTorch.
- Demonstrate mastery of tensor manipulation: implementing batched matrix multiplication, Einstein summation (torch.einsum), and masking without loops.
- Debug deliberately corrupted models: diagnosing exploding gradients, dimension mismatches, and silent broadcasting errors in real time.
Session 2 · Live Paper Discussion & Research Aptitude Interviews
- Master the live paper presentation format: summarizing a assigned paper’s core hypothesis, mathematical derivation, and empirical limitations in 10 minutes.
- Defend against probing technical questions: explaining why the authors chose specific loss terms, baselines, or hyperparameter schedules.
- Articulate clear intellectual curiosity and resilience: discussing how you navigate dead-end experiments, negative results, and ambiguous research directions.
Mental model: The RA interview is not a LeetCode contest; it tests whether you can read papers critically, implement custom math in PyTorch, and think like a scientist.
Try: Conduct a mock 15-minute paper walkthrough of a NeurIPS paper, explaining the primary mathematical equation and identifying two empirical weaknesses.
Outcome: Excel in technical coding evaluations and live paper defense discussions to secure coveted research positions.

M1.6 · Capstone Project: Research Proposal & Laboratory Onboarding Portfolio — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Comprehensive Research Proposal Formulation
- Select a target research laboratory and synthesize their recent research trajectory into a comprehensive 3-page research proposal in LaTeX.
- Formulate 2 to 3 formal Research Questions (RQs) proposing a novel algorithmic extension, benchmark evaluation, or domain adaptation.
- Detail the proposed experimental methodology: baseline architectures, public datasets, evaluation metrics, and expected computational requirements.
Session 2 · Application Package Assembly & Verification
- Compile the complete application package: tailored 4-paragraph outreach email, LaTeX academic CV, 3-page research proposal, and GitHub portfolio links.
- Set up a clean, open-source reproduction repository demonstrating your ability to implement and benchmark a foundational baseline model.
- Conduct a peer review of the complete application package to verify professional tone, typographic elegance, and zero grammatical errors.
Mental model: A fully assembled research portfolio proves you are not merely looking for an opportunity, but are already prepared to execute publication-quality research on day one.
Try: Assemble your complete application portfolio in LaTeX, linking a verified GitHub reproduction repository, and review against the rubric.
Outcome: Possess a complete, publication-grade research proposal and application portfolio ready to submit to top AI research laboratories.

- Identifies an active research laboratory and deconstructs their 3 most recent top-tier publications.

- Demonstrates thorough familiarity with the lab's research agenda and articulates immediate collaborative value.
Deliverables:
- Formally compiled 3-page Research Proposal PDF in LaTeX with verified BibTeX citations.
- Clean, professionally typeset 2-page Academic CV PDF in LaTeX.
- Complete Outreach Email text and tailored follow-up template.
- Public GitHub repository link with clean, documented PyTorch reproduction code.
Outcome: You can systematically approach, pitch, and secure Research Assistant and Fellow positions at premier AI/ML research laboratories.
