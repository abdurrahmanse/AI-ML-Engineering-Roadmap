Level 1 · Literature Review & Scientific Problem Formulation

Phase: Foundations & Methodology
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 0 (Scientific Computing & Reproducible Research Foundations)
Scope: Master the academic research methodology: discovering, analyzing, and synthesizing scientific literature, identifying open research gaps, formulating formal research questions (RQs), and constructing rigorous research proposals.

M1.1 · Scientific Discovery Engines: Navigating ArXiv, Google Scholar & Semantic Scholar — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Academic Search Engines & Literature Discovery
- Query academic databases (Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, DBLP, ArXiv) using boolean search syntax and field-specific filtering.
- Track citation graphs and follow seminal lineages forward and backward using citation trees and influence metrics.
- Discover emerging preprints and conference proceedings across premier AI/ML venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, ACL, KDD).
Session 2 · Automated Paper Feeds & Research Radar
- Set up automated research alerts and topic monitoring using ArXiv RSS feeds, Hugging Face Daily Papers, and Google Scholar keyword notifications.
- Leverage visual literature exploration tools (Connected Papers, Litmaps, ResearchRabbit) to discover co-citation clusters and derivative works.
- Filter signal from noise in daily preprint releases by evaluating author lab credibility, code availability, and benchmark claims.
Mental model: The scientific literature is a dynamic, interconnected knowledge graph; exploring citation trajectories reveals where a paradigm is heading.
Try: Map the citation graph of a foundational paper using Connected Papers, identifying the top three foundational prior works and top three derivative extensions.
Outcome: Systematically discover relevant academic literature, navigate citation networks, and establish an automated daily research radar.

M1.2 · Critical Paper Reading: The Three-Pass Method & Deconstructing Claims — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Efficient Multi-Pass Paper Deconstruction
- Apply Keshav’s Three-Pass Method to evaluate paper relevance, grasp central ideas, and dissect technical depth in structured increments.
- Extract the core contributions in Pass 1 by analyzing title, abstract, introduction, section headers, and conclusion within 10 minutes.
- Scrutinize figures, tables, and empirical evidence in Pass 2 to assess whether experimental data genuinely supports the stated claims.
Session 2 · Deep Technical Dissection & Critical Questioning
- Execute Pass 3: mentally re-implement the paper from scratch, identifying implicit assumptions, unstated tricks, and missing proofs.
- Distinguish between theoretical guarantees, empirical heuristic findings, and unverified narrative speculation in published papers.
- Identify common paper red flags: cherry-picked qualitative examples, weak or un-tuned baselines, moving goalposts, and missing ablation studies.
Mental model: Reading a paper is an active peer review where you interrogate every claim against the empirical evidence presented.
Try: Conduct a 3-pass review of a recent top-tier conference paper, writing a 1-page structured critique evaluating its core claims and baseline fairness.
Outcome: Read research papers efficiently, extract core insights rapidly, and critically evaluate the validity of scientific claims.

M1.3 · Citation Management & Literature Synthesis: Zotero, BibTeX & Taxonomy Building — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Systematic Reference Management
- Organize academic libraries systematically using Zotero with automatic PDF metadata extraction, tag hierarchies, and DOI lookups.
- Integrate Zotero with Overleaf and local LaTeX environments via Better BibTeX for automated, clean citation key generation.
- Annotate papers with structured research notes linking methodology variants, benchmark scores, and theoretical limitations.
Session 2 · Synthesizing Related Work & Taxonomy Building
- Construct comparative literature synthesis matrices mapping related papers across problem formulations, architectures, and assumptions.
- Develop conceptual taxonomies that categorize existing approaches into clear methodological families with contrasting trade-offs.
- Write polished "Related Work" sections that tell a compelling historical narrative rather than providing a disjointed list of summaries.
Mental model: A literature survey is not a catalog of summaries; it is a structured taxonomy that exposes the boundaries of current human knowledge.
Try: Build a 15-paper comparative synthesis table in Zotero, mapping existing methods across problem formulation, architecture, datasets, and limitations.
Outcome: Manage thousands of academic references effortlessly, maintain clean BibTeX registries, and synthesize literature into structured taxonomies.

M1.4 · Identifying Research Gaps & Formulating Research Questions (RQs) — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Systematic Gap Analysis
- Identify fertile research gaps: methodological oversights, untested domain transfers, computational bottlenecks, and contradictory published findings.
- Analyze the "Limitations and Future Work" sections of leading conference papers to extract candidate research directions.
- Distinguish between incremental "delta" tweaks (hyperparameter tuning) and substantial scientific gaps that yield meaningful contributions.
Session 2 · Formulating Formal Research Questions
- Formulate precise, falsifiable Research Questions (RQ1, RQ2, RQ3) that guide the entire trajectory of an academic investigation.
- Frame research questions spanning capability (Can method X solve Y?), mechanism (Why does X outperform Y?), and trade-off (What is the cost of X?).
- Define measurable operational definitions and evaluation metrics for each research question to establish clear success criteria.
Mental model: A well-formulated research question contains its own verification metric; if you cannot measure the answer, the question is not yet scientific.
Try: Select a sub-field of AI, identify an unaddressed limitation in recent SOTA papers, and formulate three formal, falsifiable Research Questions.
Outcome: Isolate high-impact research gaps from published literature and formalize rigorous, publication-grade research questions.

M1.5 · Hypothesis Generation, Theoretical Framing & Feasibility Analysis — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Formulating Scientific Hypotheses
- Formulate primary and alternative hypotheses (H1, H0) predicting specific empirical outcomes when modifying an algorithmic component.
- Provide theoretical justification for why the proposed approach should work, grounded in computational complexity, optimization, or statistics.
- Anticipate confounding variables and failure modes that could invalidate the hypothesis under realistic experimental conditions.
Session 2 · Research Project Feasibility & Risk De-risking
- Evaluate compute feasibility: calculate GPU hours, parameter scales, and dataset storage requirements against available lab resources.
- Assess timeline feasibility: plan phased milestones from rapid toy proof-of-concept to full-scale empirical validation.
- Design early de-risking experiments (quick failure tests) that validate critical assumptions before committing major computational budgets.
Mental model: Strong research balances theoretical ambition with pragmatic de-risking; fail fast on toy problems before scaling compute.
Try: Draft a feasibility budget for a research hypothesis, estimating required GPU hours, dataset disk footprints, and a 2-day proof-of-concept milestone.
Outcome: Formulate grounded research hypotheses, justify theoretical mechanisms, and establish resource-conscious experimental feasibility plans.

M1.6 · Capstone Project: Comprehensive Literature Survey & Research Proposal Specification — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Literature Synthesis & Gap Articulation
- Conduct an exhaustive literature survey across 25+ papers in an active AI/ML research area, constructing a multi-dimensional taxonomy matrix.
- Articulate a compelling, well-justified research gap highlighting why existing methodologies fail under specific critical conditions.
- Define 3 formal Research Questions (RQs) with explicit hypotheses and measurable empirical criteria.
Session 2 · Methodological Specification & Experimental Blueprint
- Write a formal methodological proposal specifying the proposed model architecture, loss functions, and algorithmic enhancements.
- Design an end-to-end experimental plan detailing benchmark datasets, baseline implementations, metric definitions, and ablation grids.
- Author the complete proposal document in LaTeX using a standard conference template (NeurIPS/ICLR), complete with BibTeX bibliography.
Mental model: A research proposal is an architectural blueprint for a scientific paper; when executed thoroughly, writing the final paper is simply filling in the results.
Try: Compile your complete literature survey and research proposal into a clean LaTeX PDF, verifying all cross-references, equations, and BibTeX citations.
Outcome: Produce an academic research proposal suitable for MSc/PhD thesis committees or top-tier conference paper initiation.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Comprehensive Literature Survey & Research Proposal Specification
BRIEF: Author a formal academic research proposal in LaTeX following the official NeurIPS/ICLR conference format. The proposal must synthesize current literature in a chosen AI/ML subfield, identify a significant research gap, formalize falsifiable research questions, and outline a rigorous experimental validation blueprint.
Build a research proposal that:
- Surveys at least 25 peer-reviewed papers from top-tier AI/ML venues, organizing them into a structured taxonomic framework.
- Constructs a comparative analysis table evaluating prior works across assumptions, architectural choices, compute requirements, and empirical limits.
- Identifies an unaddressed scientific research gap supported by concrete citations and empirical analysis of prior limitations.
- Formulates three formal Research Questions (RQ1, RQ2, RQ3) accompanied by precise null and alternative hypotheses.
- Specifies the mathematical formulation of the proposed approach, including loss formulations, architectural modifications, and algorithmic pseudocode.
- Details the experimental blueprint: choice of benchmark datasets, competitive baseline models to reproduce, evaluation metrics, and planned ablation studies.
- Provides a detailed compute budget estimating GPU memory requirements, training wall-clock time, and a phased risk-mitigation timeline.
- Adheres strictly to conference formatting guidelines with clean vector figures, mathematical notation, and an authenticated BibTeX bibliography.
Deliverables:
- Formally compiled 6-page Research Proposal PDF formatted in LaTeX using the official NeurIPS or ICLR template.
- Comprehensive BibTeX library (.bib) containing cleanly formatted, verified citations for all referenced works.
- Markdown summary pitch slide-deck outline for presentation to a thesis advisor or research lab director.
Outcome: You can identify high-impact scientific opportunities, review academic literature critically, and formulate publication-grade research proposals.
