Level 1 · Statistical Learning Theory & Classical ML

Scope: explore the theoretical foundations of statistical learning: PAC learning, empirical risk minimization, VC dimension, generalization bounds, convex optimization, kernel methods, and probabilistic graphical models.

Prerequisites: Level 0

M1.1 · The Learning Problem & Empirical Risk Minimization — (sessions: 3)

Formulate the statistical learning problem: input space, output space, unknown data-generating distribution P(X, Y), hypothesis class H, and loss functions.
Define true risk (expected loss) versus empirical risk (training loss) and formulate the Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) principle.
Analyze the inductive bias of hypothesis classes and explain why unconstrained learning without inductive bias is mathematically impossible.
Decompose expected generalization error into approximation error, estimation error, and irreducible optimization error.
Understand the classical bias-variance decomposition for mean squared error: bias squared, variance, and noise terms.
Analyze overfitting and underfitting from statistical learning perspectives and study the effect of sample size N on generalization error.
Examine surrogate loss functions: 0-1 loss intractability, convex surrogates (hinge loss, logistic loss, exponential loss), and classification calibration.
Outcome: formulate machine learning problems rigorously in the language of statistical decision theory and analyze risk decomposition.

M1.2 · PAC Learning & Generalization Bounds — (sessions: 4)

Master Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning framework: epsilon-accuracy, delta-confidence, sample complexity, and computational complexity.
Prove finite hypothesis class generalization bounds using Hoeffding's inequality and the union bound for realizable and agnostic settings.
Understand uniform convergence: bounding the supremum of empirical risk deviations over the entire hypothesis class.
Define Rademacher complexity and Gaussian complexity as data-dependent measures of hypothesis class richness.
Study growth functions, Shattering, and Vapnik-Chervonenkis (VC) dimension for infinite hypothesis classes.
Prove Sauer-Shelah lemma and derive VC dimension bounds for linear classifiers, hyperplanes, and polynomial classifiers.
Understand modern generalization frontiers: PAC-Bayes bounds, margin bounds, and where classical uniform convergence bounds fail in deep learning.
Outcome: compute VC dimensions for geometric hypothesis classes and derive formal sample complexity bounds for PAC learnability.

M1.3 · Convex Optimization & Duality Theory — (sessions: 3)

Understand convex sets, convex functions, epigraphs, subgradients, and subdifferentials for non-smooth convex objectives.
Master optimality conditions: Fermat's rule for unconstrained problems, Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions for constrained optimization.
Formulate Lagrangian functions, primal optimization problems, Lagrange dual functions, and dual optimization problems.
Understand weak duality, strong duality, Slater's constraint qualification condition, and zero duality gap.
Analyze first-order optimization methods: gradient descent, projected gradient descent, subgradient descent, and convergence rates for convex and strongly convex functions.
Analyze accelerated first-order algorithms: Polyak's heavy-ball method, Nesterov accelerated gradient, and optimal O(1/k^2) convergence rates.
Explore second-order methods: Newton's method, Quasi-Newton methods (BFGS, L-BFGS), and trust-region algorithms with quadratic convergence properties.
Outcome: derive dual optimization problems analytically, verify KKT conditions, and analyze algorithmic convergence rates mathematically.

M1.4 · Kernel Methods & Non-Parametric Theory — (sessions: 3)

Formulate linear models for regression and classification: ordinary least squares, ridge regression, and Lasso with L1 sparsity proofs.
Understand reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS), the Moore-Aronszajn theorem, and Mercer's theorem for positive definite kernels.
Master the kernel trick: mapping data implicitly into infinite-dimensional feature spaces via kernel evaluation k(x, x').
Analyze canonical kernels: polynomial kernels, Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel, Laplacian kernel, and neural tangent kernels (NTK) introduction.
Derive Support Vector Machines (SVM) primal and dual formulations, support vectors geometry, slack variables, and soft-margin formulations.
Formulate Kernel Ridge Regression and Gaussian Processes: covariance functions, posterior Gaussian distributions, and Bayesian non-parametrics.
Understand non-parametric estimation: k-nearest neighbors convergence rates, Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression, and the curse of dimensionality.
Outcome: prove Mercer's conditions for custom kernels, derive the dual SVM optimization problem, and implement Gaussian Process regression with analytical posteriors.

M1.5 · Probabilistic Graphical Models & Approximate Inference — (sessions: 3)

Understand Directed A Graphical Models (Bayesian Networks): factorization of joint distributions, d-separation, and conditional independence properties.
Understand Undirected Graphical Models (Markov Random Fields): cliques, potential functions, Gibbs distributions, and the Hammersley-Clifford theorem.
Formulate exact inference algorithms: variable elimination, belief propagation on trees, and the sum-product / max-product junction tree algorithm.
Analyze the computational complexity of exact inference and explain why general inference is NP-hard.
Master approximate inference through sampling: Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, and Gibbs sampling.
Master approximate inference through variational methods: Variational Inference (VI), Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), mean-field approximation, and coordinate ascent variational inference (CAVI).
Explore the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm: Jensen's inequality derivation, Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM), and convergence properties.
Outcome: derive the Evidence Lower Bound from Jensen's inequality and implement the Expectation-Maximization algorithm for latent Gaussian mixtures.

M1.6 · Capstone: Empirical and Theoretical Study of Generalization in Kernel Regression — (sessions: 2)

Formulate a rigorous research experiment comparing theoretical generalization bounds against empirical test error across varying sample sizes N and dimensions D.
Implement Kernel Ridge Regression and Support Vector Machines entirely from scratch using quadratic programming solvers (CVXPY/SciPy).
Derive the analytical generalization error bound using empirical Rademacher complexity and RKHS norm constraints.
Compute empirical Rademacher complexity via Monte Carlo sampling of random Rademacher variables over synthetically generated non-linear manifolds.
Investigate the role of regularization parameter lambda on eigenvalue decay of the kernel Gram matrix and the effective degrees of freedom.
Measure the gap between worst-case theoretical generalization bounds and actual empirical test risk across overparameterized versus underparameterized regimes.
Produce publication-quality research figures: risk curves, eigenvalue spectra, empirical vs theoretical bound gaps, and decision boundaries.
Write a formal academic report (ICML/NeurIPS formatting) analyzing where uniform convergence bounds remain tight and where they become vacuous.
Final result: a reproducible research benchmark and theoretical report validating kernel generalization bounds against empirical learning dynamics.

Level 1 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Formulate learning problems in the statistical decision theory framework.
Prove risk decomposition into bias, variance, and irreducible error.
Derive generalization error bounds for finite hypothesis classes using Hoeffding's inequality.
Define the PAC learning model and compute sample complexity for realizable hypotheses.
Calculate the VC dimension of linear classifiers and simple geometric families.
State and prove properties of convex functions, subgradients, and epigraphs.
Derive the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions for constrained optimization problems.
Formulate the Lagrangian dual problem and prove weak duality.
Explain Nesterov acceleration and prove its optimal convergence rate for smooth convex functions.
Prove that a given function is a valid Mercer kernel and construct an RKHS mapping.
Derive the dual formulation of Support Vector Machines and identify support vectors geometrically.
Implement Gaussian Process regression and compute closed-form analytical predictive posteriors.
Determine conditional independence in Bayesian networks using d-separation.
Derive the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) mathematically using Jensen's inequality.
Implement the Expectation-Maximization algorithm for latent variable models.
Compare MCMC sampling and variational inference for approximate posterior estimation.
Analyze why classical VC and Rademacher bounds can become vacuous in deep overparameterized models.
Conduct a rigorous theoretical and empirical comparison between theoretical bounds and empirical generalization.
Progression: Empirical Risk Minimization → PAC Bounds & VC Dimension → Convex Optimization & Duality → Kernel Methods & RKHS → Graphical Models & Variational Inference → Generalization Benchmark Study.
