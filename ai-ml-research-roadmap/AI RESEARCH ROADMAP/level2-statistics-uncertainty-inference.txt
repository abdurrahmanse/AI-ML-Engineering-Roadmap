Level 2 · Probability, Statistics & Uncertainty

Phase: Research Foundations & Lab Onboarding
Suggested duration: 26 sessions (~6 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 1
Scope: Learn to describe variation, estimate uncertainty, and distinguish a useful pattern from an accidental result.

M2.1 · Probability and Random Variables — (sessions: 4)
- Work with events, conditional probability, independence, Bayes' rule, and random variables.
- Interpret expectation, variance, covariance, common distributions, and sampling.
- Simulate probability experiments and compare empirical frequencies with theoretical values.
- Report uncertainty with confidence or credible intervals, effect sizes, and the assumptions behind each estimate.
- Distinguish exploratory patterns from confirmatory tests and document multiple-comparison and sampling decisions.
Mental model: Probability is a language for reasoning when observations and outcomes are uncertain.
Try: Simulate a clinical screening example and show how base rates change the meaning of a positive test.
Outcome: You can model uncertainty and explain conditional reasoning without relying on slogans.

M2.2 · Estimation, Intervals & Hypothesis Tests — (sessions: 5)
- Construct point estimates, bootstrap intervals, confidence intervals, and practical effect sizes.
- State null and alternative hypotheses, choose a test, and report assumptions and limitations.
- Separate statistical significance, practical importance, and the number of comparisons being made.
- Report uncertainty with confidence or credible intervals, effect sizes, and the assumptions behind each estimate.
- Distinguish exploratory patterns from confirmatory tests and document multiple-comparison and sampling decisions.
Mental model: An estimate without uncertainty is an incomplete scientific claim.
Try: Bootstrap the difference in two model accuracies and write a result paragraph with an interval.
Outcome: You can quantify uncertainty and report statistical comparisons responsibly.

M2.3 · Experimental Data Analysis — (sessions: 5)
- Use pandas and visualization to inspect distributions, missingness, outliers, and subgroup differences.
- Detect selection bias, confounding, leakage, survivorship bias, and Simpson's paradox.
- Choose train, validation, and test splits before looking at test outcomes.
- Report uncertainty with confidence or credible intervals, effect sizes, and the assumptions behind each estimate.
- Distinguish exploratory patterns from confirmatory tests and document multiple-comparison and sampling decisions.
Mental model: Analysis decisions are part of the experiment and must be recorded before results are known.
Try: Audit a public dataset, create a data-quality report, and list threats to validity before modeling.
Outcome: You can turn raw observations into a defensible exploratory and statistical analysis.

M2.4 · Sampling, Resampling and Power — (sessions: 3)
- Explain sampling frames, selection bias, clustered observations, dependence, and statistical power.
- Use bootstrap, permutation, and simulation to understand uncertainty when parametric assumptions are weak.
Mental model: Precision is limited by the information in the sample, not by the confidence of the analyst.
Try: Simulate a small study and show how sample size, effect size, and variance change its conclusions.
Outcome: You can design an analysis that makes uncertainty and power visible.

M2.5 · Bayesian Reasoning and Decision Analysis — (sessions: 3)
- Use priors, likelihoods, posterior uncertainty, and posterior predictive checks for research decisions.
- Compare Bayesian and frequentist interpretations without treating either as a universal substitute.
Mental model: Inference updates what is plausible; it does not turn assumptions into facts.
Try: Build a simple posterior model and explain how its conclusion changes under reasonable priors.
Outcome: You can communicate uncertainty as a decision-relevant distribution.

M2.6 · Capstone: Uncertainty and Bias Audit — (sessions: 6)
- Select a public dataset and define a population, sampling process, outcome, and estimand.
- Produce an exploratory report with uncertainty intervals, subgroup checks, and a pre-analysis plan.
- Explain which conclusions are supported, which are tentative, and which cannot be made.
Final result: A transparent statistical audit that demonstrates scientific caution, reproducible analysis, and honest limits.
