Level 2 · Probability & Statistics

Phase: Research Foundations
Suggested duration: 32 sessions (~8 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 1
Scope: Master probability theory, statistical estimation, hypothesis testing, uncertainty quantification, non-parametric statistics, multiple comparison corrections, and the statistical rigour required to evaluate modern generative AI systems.

M2.1 · Probability Spaces, Random Variables & Probability Distributions — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Foundations of probability & Bayes' Theorem
- Probability Axioms: Formulate sample spaces, events, Kolmogorov's axioms of probability, joint distributions, marginalization, and conditional probability.
- Bayesian Inference Foundations: Apply Bayes' Theorem to update prior beliefs into posterior distributions given empirical likelihood observations.
Session 2 · Parametric distributions & moment generating functions
- Discrete & Continuous Distributions: Characterize Bernoulli, Binomial, Poisson, Gaussian/Normal, Beta, Dirichlet, Gamma, and Student's t distributions.
- Moments & Expectation: Calculate expectation, variance, covariance matrices, skewness, kurtosis, and derive properties using Moment Generating Functions (MGFs).
Mental model: Probability models quantify our uncertainty about the world before data is observed, providing mathematical structures to interpret empirical phenomena.
Try: Implement a probability distribution workbench in Python that fits parametric distributions to skewed real-world data and evaluates goodness-of-fit via QQ-plots.
Outcome: You can formalize uncertain processes with appropriate probability distributions and derive analytical expectations and variances.

M2.2 · Information Theory, Entropy & Divergence Measures — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Information measures, entropy & mutual information
- Information Measures: Define self-information, Shannon entropy for discrete variables, and differential entropy for continuous distributions.
- Mutual Information: Compute joint entropy, conditional entropy, and mutual information to quantify non-linear dependencies between random variables.
Session 2 · Divergence metrics & cross-entropy
- Relative Entropy: Derive Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, prove non-negativity via Jensen's inequality, and analyze asymmetric information geometry.
- Symmetric Metrics: Compare cross-entropy loss, Jensen-Shannon divergence, and Wasserstein distance for measuring statistical distance between probability densities.
Mental model: Entropy measures the inherent surprise or information content of a distribution; divergence measures the informational cost of approximating reality.
Try: Code an interactive information theory visualizer calculating KL divergence between two multivariate Gaussians as their covariance matrices shift.
Outcome: You can apply information-theoretic metrics to measure distribution divergence, loss functions, and non-linear feature dependencies.

M2.3 · Point Estimation, Maximum Likelihood & Bayesian Estimation — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) & properties
- Likelihood Principles: Formulate likelihood and log-likelihood functions for Gaussian and Bernoulli models; derive analytical and numerical MLE parameters.
- Estimator Properties: Prove unbiasedness, asymptotic consistency, efficiency, Fisher Information, and the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound.
Session 2 · Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) & conjugate priors
- Bayesian Point Estimation: Formulate Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation, connecting Gaussian priors to L2 weight decay and Laplace priors to L1 penalties.
- Conjugate Priors: Derive analytical posterior updates using conjugate prior pairs (Beta-Binomial, Dirichlet-Multinomial, Normal-Normal).
Mental model: Point estimation searches for parameter values that make observed data most probable, balanced against prior regularizing constraints.
Try: Derive the MLE and MAP estimates for a linear regression model from first principles, code both solutions in NumPy, and verify that MAP regularizes noisy coefficients.
Outcome: You can formulate, derive, and analyze Maximum Likelihood and Bayesian Maximum A Posteriori estimators for arbitrary statistical models.

M2.4 · Hypothesis Testing, Non-Parametric Statistics & Power Analysis — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Null hypothesis testing & parametric comparisons
- Hypothesis Frameworks: Formulate Null and Alternative Hypotheses (H0, H1), define Type I error rate (alpha) and Type II error rate (beta).
- Parametric Tests: Execute Student's t-test, paired t-test, and ANOVA, verifying normality via Shapiro-Wilk and homoscedasticity via Levene's test.
Session 2 · Non-parametric tests & multiple comparison corrections
- Non-Parametric Benchmarking: Execute the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and Mann-Whitney U test on algorithm comparisons without assuming normal distributions.
- Multiple Testing Adjustments: Control Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) with Bonferroni and Holm-Bonferroni, and control False Discovery Rate (FDR) with Benjamini-Hochberg.
- Statistical Power & Effect Sizes: Conduct a priori power calculations to determine necessary sample sizes and report Cohen's d alongside p-values.
Mental model: A p-value is not a measure of effect size or importance; statistical rigor requires reporting effect magnitudes, confidence bounds, and sample power.
Try: Simulate a multi-model benchmark experiment with 10 competing algorithms across 20 datasets, apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction, and detect false discovery spikes.
Outcome: You can design statistically sound benchmark evaluations, select appropriate non-parametric tests, and apply rigorous multiplicity adjustments.

M2.5 · Resampling, Bootstrap Confidence Intervals & Conformal Prediction — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Empirical bootstrapping & permutation testing
- Non-Parametric Bootstrap: Generate empirical bootstrap sampling distributions to construct 95% BCa (bias-corrected accelerated) confidence intervals for non-linear metrics.
- Permutation Tests: Execute two-sample permutation tests to determine whether performance differences between two models could arise purely by chance.
Session 2 · Conformal prediction & distribution-free coverage
- Conformal Prediction Principles: Formulate non-conformity scores and construct prediction intervals with distribution-free, finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees (1 - alpha).
- Uncertainty Calibration: Evaluate predictive interval width versus coverage trade-offs on regression and classification benchmarks.
Mental model: Bootstrapping allows us to quantify parameter and metric uncertainty directly from data without making unrealistic Gaussian assumptions.
Try: Implement a bootstrap confidence interval calculator for F1-score and AUROC on an imbalanced classification test set and compare against naive normal approximations.
Outcome: You can compute distribution-free confidence intervals via bootstrapping and construct guaranteed prediction intervals using conformal prediction.

M2.6 · Capstone: Statistical Rigor & Hypothesis Testing Audit Package — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Engineering a comprehensive statistical evaluation toolkit
- Build a Python library providing automated hypothesis testing (paired Wilcoxon, Mann-Whitney, Friedman test with Nemenyi post-hoc).
- Integrate non-parametric bootstrap confidence interval estimation (percentile and BCa), Cohen's d effect sizes, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustments.
Session 2 · Benchmarking audit & scientific paper reporting
- Run an empirical audit on a published machine learning benchmark table, identifying uncorrected p-values, reporting true confidence intervals, and verifying statistical significance.
- Produce a publication-ready LaTeX table and statistical significance critical difference diagram (Demsar plot).
Final result: An open-source, automated statistical testing and uncertainty audit package suitable for evaluating and reporting empirical AI/ML research benchmarks.

M2.7 · Statistical Rigour in LLM Evaluation — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Benchmark validity, contamination & confidence intervals for LLMs
- Benchmark Contamination Auditing: Understand why LLM benchmark scores can be inflated by training data contamination; apply n-gram overlap tests (e.g. comparing model training data with benchmark test sets using MinHash deduplication) to audit whether a model has memorized benchmark answers.
- Confidence Intervals on Accuracy Metrics: Compute proper bootstrap confidence intervals around benchmark accuracy scores (e.g. MMLU, HellaSwag, GSM8K) rather than reporting point estimates; understand why a 1-point improvement with N=1,000 questions is not statistically significant without a confidence interval.
- Paired Significance Testing Across Models: Apply the McNemar test and paired bootstrap to compare two language models on the same question set, distinguishing genuine performance differences from random seed variance.
Session 2 · Designing statistically valid human evaluation studies for generative AI
- Human Evaluation Study Design: Design a human preference evaluation study for a generative AI system specifying annotator selection, pairwise vs rating scales, inter-annotator agreement targets (Fleiss' Kappa > 0.6), and sample size power calculations.
- Position Bias & Order Effects: Measure and correct for position bias (annotators preferring the first response), verbosity bias (preferring longer outputs), and sycophancy in LLM-as-judge evaluations using randomized counterbalancing.
- Reporting Standards: Apply the HELM (Holistic Evaluation of Language Models) and BIG-bench reporting conventions to report multi-metric evaluation results with proper uncertainty quantification and scenario breakdowns.
Mental model: An LLM benchmark score without a confidence interval and contamination audit is marketing material, not science; rigorous evaluation requires the same statistical discipline as any controlled experiment.
Try: Evaluate two open language models on 500 questions from the MMLU benchmark, compute bootstrap confidence intervals around their accuracy scores, run a McNemar significance test, and audit both models for n-gram contamination against the benchmark test set.
Outcome: You can design statistically valid LLM evaluation studies, compute contamination-adjusted confidence intervals on benchmark scores, and detect spurious performance differences between models.
