Level 14 · Multimodal & Thesis Scoping

Phase: Thesis Scoping
Suggested duration: 40 sessions (~10 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 13
Scope: Master multimodal foundation models (CLIP, LLaVA, Whisper), translate machine learning across scientific disciplines with domain experts, scope a high-impact thesis topic, conduct pilot feasibility studies, defend a formal Thesis Prospectus, and build a modern AI research portfolio.

M14.1 · Multimodal Foundation Models: CLIP, Vision-Language & Audio Architectures — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Contrastive vision-language pretraining: CLIP & SigLIP
- Dual-Encoder Architecture: Deconstruct CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining): image encoder (ViT) and text encoder (Transformer) projecting into a shared latent space.
- Symmetric InfoNCE Loss: Derive the symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss over N x N image-text pairs; implement zero-shot classification using prompt embeddings.
- SigLIP Modernization: Contrast CLIP's softmax normalization with SigLIP's pairwise sigmoid loss, analyzing memory scaling and batch-size independence.
Session 2 · Autoregressive Vision-Language Models (VLMs): LLaVA & BLIP-2
- Multimodal Projectors: Implement LLaVA (Large Language and Vision Assistant), projecting frozen ViT patch tokens through a linear or MLP adapter into an autoregressive LLM.
- Cross-Attention Fusion: Compare linear projections against BLIP-2's Q-Former (querying transformer) and Flamingo's gated cross-attention layers.
- Audio & Speech Processing: Deconstruct Whisper's encoder-decoder transformer architecture for robust multitask speech recognition and translation.
Mental model: Multimodal models bind heterogeneous sensory modalities into a unified semantic representation space, translating visual and auditory patterns into language tokens.
Try: Build a minimal LLaVA visual-question-answering pipeline in PyTorch, projecting image features from a frozen CLIP vision backbone into an open LLM prompt stream.
Outcome: You can formulate contrastive multimodal objectives, implement visual projection adapters, and configure Vision-Language Models for multimodal inference.

M14.2 · Cross-Disciplinary Research Translation & Real-World Validation — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Working with non-ML domain experts
- Interdisciplinary Co-Design: Partner with clinicians, cybersecurity analysts, structural biologists, or climate scientists to translate domain workflows into mathematical formulations.
- Ground Truth Discrepancies: Identify discrepancies between clinical/domain reality and naive machine learning labels, avoiding surrogate metric fallacies.
Session 2 · Regulatory compliance & clinical validation standards
- Clinical AI Standards: Adhere to reporting guidelines (CONSORT-AI, SPIRIT-AI) and regulatory frameworks (FDA AI/ML Software as a Medical Device / SaMD guidance).
- Prospective vs Retrospective Evidence: Differentiate retrospective historical benchmark evaluation from prospective clinical trials and shadow-deployment user studies.
Mental model: In applied domain research, domain experts define clinical or scientific validity; model accuracy on an artificial benchmark is meaningless if it optimizes the wrong clinical proxy.
Try: Co-design an evaluation protocol for an AI diagnostic tool with a domain practitioner, defining clinically meaningful failure criteria and an actionable human-in-the-loop escalation policy.
Outcome: You can translate complex domain challenges into mathematically sound AI formulations and design regulatory-compliant validation protocols.

M14.3 · Selecting a High-Impact Thesis Topic & Gap Identification — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Strategies for thesis topic selection
- The Venn Diagram of Thesis Topic Selection: Intersect personal intellectual curiosity, advisor expertise and grant funding, available compute and data resources, and community urgency.
- Incremental vs Paradigm-Shifting Research: Distinguish incremental parameter tuning ("delta engineering") from high-leverage foundational contributions that resolve key bottlenecks.
Session 2 · Systematic gap mapping & feasibility auditing
- White Space Mapping: Construct an exhaustive literature taxonomy to uncover genuine white spaces in contemporary AI research that have been overlooked or prematurely abandoned.
- Resource & Feasibility Audits: Calculate total required GPU compute hours, dataset access barriers, and institutional permissions before committing multi-year doctoral effort.
Mental model: A thesis topic is a multi-year investment of your intellectual career; choosing an overcrowded or resource-infeasible problem guarantees frustration and low impact.
Try: Conduct an exhaustive feasibility audit for three candidate thesis topics, scoring each on novelty, resource feasibility, data accessibility, and 5-year scientific relevance.
Outcome: You can systematically evaluate potential thesis topics and select a defensible, high-leverage, resource-feasible research problem.

M14.4 · Designing Thesis Pilot Studies & Risk-Reducing Feasibility Experiments — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Pilot study design & hypothesis falsification
- The Scientific Purpose of a Pilot: Design focused, small-scale pilot experiments specifically engineered to test core assumptions and attempt to falsify your primary hypothesis quickly.
- Downscaled Proof-of-Concepts: Execute downscaled experiments on synthetic data or subsampled benchmarks (e.g. 10% data on single GPU) to verify signal presence.
Session 2 · Pilot data analysis & go/no-go decision gates
- Signal-to-Noise Evaluation: Evaluate pilot results to determine whether the proposed mechanism yields sufficient empirical effect size to justify full-scale investigation.
- The Thesis Pivot Protocol: Execute a formal pivot when pilot evidence fails to confirm the hypothesis, salvaging theoretical insights and redirecting methodology.
Mental model: A failed pilot study is a tremendous scientific success if it prevents two years of wasted effort on an unworkable research direction.
Try: Execute a 1-week pilot experiment testing the core mathematical assumption of a proposed method on a toy benchmark, producing a formal go/no-go evidence report.
Outcome: You can design and execute downscaled pilot studies to test hypothesis feasibility before launching large-scale doctoral experiments.

M14.5 · Authoring the Formal Thesis Prospectus & Proposal Defense — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Structuring the academic thesis prospectus
- Dissertation Prospectus Anatomy: Author a comprehensive prospectus comprising Abstract, Problem Statement, Related Work Taxonomy, Proposed Methodology, Preliminary Pilot Results, Multi-Chapter Dissertation Outline, and Work Plan.
- Chapter Scoping: Structure three cohesive research aims corresponding to the foundational baseline, the core methodological contribution, and the real-world domain translation.
Session 2 · Committee formation & mock oral prospectus defense
- Advisory Committee Strategy: Select and recruit thesis committee members with complementary theoretical, methodological, and domain expertise.
- Prospectus Defense Presentation: Deliver a 30-minute formal prospectus presentation defending the novelty, methodology, and feasibility of the proposed dissertation before a skeptical panel.
Mental model: The thesis prospectus is a binding intellectual contract between you and your doctoral committee, establishing the exact criteria required for final graduation.
Try: Author a complete 15-page formal Thesis Prospectus, design a slide deck defending the three research aims, and participate in a mock committee oral examination.
Outcome: You can author a comprehensive doctoral thesis prospectus and defend its scientific validity and feasibility before an academic advisory committee.

M14.6 · Capstone: Multimodal Cross-Domain Research Study & Thesis Prospectus Defense — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Execution of a multimodal cross-domain empirical study
- Implement and evaluate a multimodal research system combining text, vision, and domain-specific telemetry (clinical EHR or network threat data) using cross-attention fusion.
- Conduct a controlled ablation study demonstrating the unique empirical contribution of multimodal alignment over unimodal baselines under compute parity.
Session 2 · Formal thesis prospectus dossier & oral defense
- Compile a complete, committee-ready Thesis Prospectus package incorporating preliminary pilot results, a 3-chapter dissertation roadmap, and an auditable risk register.
- Deliver an oral defense of the prospectus before a faculty committee panel, address hostile methodological critiques, and incorporate revisions into the final research protocol.
Final result: A completed multimodal cross-domain research study and a formally defended, committee-approved doctoral Thesis Prospectus establishing a clear path to publication and graduation.

M14.7 · Building a Modern AI Research Portfolio — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Constructing a research portfolio for the LLM era
- The Modern AI Research Portfolio: Understand that a competitive AI research portfolio in the LLM era requires demonstrating breadth across multiple contribution types — not only traditional model-architecture papers but also evaluation methodology contributions, dataset papers, analysis papers, and open-source tools — because hiring committees at top labs and universities evaluate researchers on their ability to identify important problems, not just their ability to improve SOTA numbers.
- GitHub as a Research Portfolio: Build a GitHub research portfolio that communicates your scientific identity: a pinned repository for each completed research project with a compelling README that explains the research question, key findings, reproducible code, and a link to the paper or preprint; understand how to write a README that speaks to both technical reviewers (who want to run your code) and non-technical decision-makers (who want to understand your contribution).
- The Research Statement as Portfolio Narrative: Draft a 2-page research statement that constructs a coherent intellectual narrative connecting your completed projects — explaining not just what you did but why the sequence of your research choices reveals a scientific perspective and research vision — following the "past work, current work, future directions" structure used in academic faculty applications and industrial research scientist interviews.
Session 2 · Open-source contributions and research community building
- Open-Sourcing Research Artifacts: Package your research contributions as community-usable open-source tools: write documentation targeting two audiences simultaneously (researchers who want to use your method in their work, and practitioners who want to apply your method to real problems); release model weights on HuggingFace Hub with a Model Card following the Hugging Face model card template; and archive code on Zenodo for a permanent DOI citation.
- Building Research Visibility: Understand the research visibility strategies that are legitimate and effective in the modern AI community: posting paper summaries on Twitter/X and LinkedIn targeting specific communities (e.g. #MachineLearning, AI alignment forums, interpretability reading groups), presenting at workshops rather than only main conference tracks when your work is at an early stage, and engaging with related papers through citing them correctly and reaching out to authors for collaboration.
- Maintaining a Living Research Website: Build a minimal academic research website (using GitHub Pages, Hugo, or Jekyll) that contains: a brief bio, a research interests paragraph, a publications list with PDF links, a projects section showcasing your GitHub portfolio, and a CV link — understanding that this is the first thing hiring committees look at before reading your application.
Mental model: A modern AI research portfolio is not a collection of impressive benchmark numbers — it is a curated argument that you have a genuine scientific perspective, the technical skills to execute research independently, and the communication skills to make your work useful to others.
Try: Package a completed research project as a full open-source release: write a compelling README explaining the research question and key findings in 500 words for a non-expert reader; write a second technical README section in 500 words for an expert who wants to reproduce your results; release model weights to HuggingFace with a complete Model Card; and write a 500-word Twitter thread summarizing your key finding that would be comprehensible to an ML-interested audience.
Outcome: You can construct a modern AI research portfolio that communicates scientific identity through GitHub projects, an academic website, and a research statement; package research artifacts for open-source release with HuggingFace Model Cards and Zenodo DOIs; and build research community visibility through legitimate professional communication strategies.
