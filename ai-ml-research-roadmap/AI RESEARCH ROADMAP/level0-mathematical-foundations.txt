Level 0 · Mathematical & Computational Foundations

Scope: master the core mathematical disciplines and numerical computing paradigms required for modern AI research: linear algebra, multivariable calculus, probability theory, information theory, and tensor automatic differentiation.

M0.1 · Linear Algebra & Vector Spaces — (sessions: 4)

Understand vector spaces, subspaces, linear independence, basis, dimension, and linear transformations from geometric and algebraic viewpoints.
Master matrix operations, rank-nullity theorem, invertibility, determinants, and matrix inverses in computational and theoretical contexts.
Analyze eigenvalues, eigenvectors, characteristic polynomials, and geometric versus algebraic multiplicity.
Understand spectral decomposition, symmetric matrices, positive semi-definite (PSD) matrices, and quadratic forms.
Master Singular Value Decomposition (SVD): compact SVD, thin SVD, truncated SVD, low-rank matrix approximation, and the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem.
Understand vector norms (L1, L2, Lp, L-infinity) and matrix norms (Frobenius, spectral norm, nuclear norm) and their roles in regularization.
Compute inner products, projections, orthogonal complements, and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.
Explore condition numbers, ill-conditioned linear systems, matrix pseudo-inverses (Moore-Penrose), and numerical stability in high dimensions.
Outcome: derive SVD decompositions, prove spectral properties of symmetric positive semi-definite matrices, and analyze high-dimensional linear mappings algebraically and geometrically.

M0.2 · Multivariable Calculus & Automatic Differentiation — (sessions: 3)

Compute partial derivatives, directional derivatives, gradients, Jacobian matrices, and Hessian matrices for scalar and vector-valued multivariable functions.
Understand multivariate Taylor series expansions, quadratic approximations, and critical points (local minima, maxima, saddle points).
Master the multivariate chain rule across nested vector-to-vector and matrix-to-matrix mappings.
Derive backpropagation mathematically as a reverse-mode automatic differentiation algorithm over computational directed acyclic graphs (DAGs).
Contrast forward-mode automatic differentiation (dual numbers) and reverse-mode automatic differentiation in terms of computational and memory complexity.
Analyze matrix and tensor calculus: computing derivatives with respect to vectors, matrices, and multi-dimensional tensors using index notation and Einstein summation.
Understand higher-order gradients: Hessian-vector products (HVPs), Fisher Information Matrix, and their applications in second-order optimization.
Outcome: implement analytical tensor backpropagation manually on paper and verify gradients against numerical finite differences.

M0.3 · Probability Theory & Stochastic Foundations — (sessions: 3)

Understand probability spaces, sigma-algebras, random variables, cumulative distribution functions, and probability density functions.
Analyze discrete distributions (Bernoulli, Binomial, Categorical, Poisson) and continuous distributions (Gaussian, Multivariate Normal, Uniform, Beta, Dirichlet).
Master joint distributions, conditional distributions, marginalization, Bayes' theorem, independence, and conditional independence.
Derive expectations, variances, covariances, correlation coefficients, covariance matrices, and moments of multidimensional distributions.
Understand change of variables formula for continuous probability density functions, Jacobians of transformations, and normalizing flows foundations.
Analyze law of large numbers (weak and strong), central limit theorem, and concentration inequalities (Markov, Chebyshev, Hoeffding, Chernoff bounds).
Understand maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation, and their exact relations to loss functions in supervised learning.
Outcome: manipulate multivariate Gaussian densities, derive MLE and MAP estimators analytically, and bound sample estimate deviations using concentration inequalities.

M0.4 · Information Theory & Entropy — (sessions: 2)

Define Shannon entropy as a measure of uncertainty and surprise for discrete and continuous (differential entropy) random variables.
Compute joint entropy, conditional entropy, and derive the chain rule of entropy.
Master Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence (relative entropy): mathematical definition, non-negativity (Gibbs' inequality), asymmetry, and directionality (forward KL vs reverse KL).
Understand cross-entropy loss, its relationship to Shannon entropy and KL divergence, and why it forms the standard surrogate loss in classification.
Analyze mutual information, channel capacity, data processing inequality, and the information bottleneck principle in deep neural representations.
Understand Wasserstein distance (Earth Mover's Distance) and optimal transport fundamentals versus f-divergences in probability distribution alignment.
Outcome: prove Gibbs' inequality, compute analytical KL divergence between multivariate Gaussians, and contrast forward and reverse KL behavior on multi-modal targets.

M0.5 · Numerical Scientific Computing & JAX/PyTorch Internals — (sessions: 3)

Understand floating-point representation (IEEE 754: FP64, FP32, FP16, BF16, FP8), precision limits, machine epsilon, underflow, and overflow.
Master numerically stable computation: log-sum-exp trick for Softmax, log-domain probabilities, and mitigating vanishing gradients.
Implement vectorized and tensorized mathematical operations using NumPy, PyTorch, and JAX with zero Python-level loops.
Understand computation graphs, tensor strides, views, contiguous memory layout, broadcasting rules, and inplace mutations.
Master JAX functional programming primitives: pure functions, jit (XLA compilation), vmap (vectorized batching), and grad (algorithmic differentiation).
Profile floating point operations (FLOPs), memory bandwidth, CPU-GPU data transfers, and tensor allocation bottlenecks.
Outcome: write high-performance vectorized numerical code in PyTorch/JAX that runs stably in low-precision floating-point regimes without numerical underflow.

M0.6 · Capstone: Build an Autograd Engine from Mathematical First Principles — (sessions: 2)

Build a complete tensor-valued reverse-mode automatic differentiation engine from scratch in pure Python and NumPy with no deep learning frameworks.
Implement a computational Tensor class supporting dynamic DAG graph construction, topological sorting, and reverse topological gradient accumulation.
Implement forward and backward passes for fundamental mathematical operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication, matrix multiplication, transpose, power, log, and exp.
Implement multi-dimensional tensor broadcasting mechanics and ensure gradient reduction over broadcasted dimensions during backward execution.
Implement activation functions with numerical stability guards: Sigmoid, ReLU, GELU, and numerically stable Softmax with LogSumExp.
Implement loss functions: Mean Squared Error, Binary Cross Entropy, and Categorical Cross Entropy with one-hot targets.
Verify gradient correctness for every single primitive operation using numerical gradient checking (finite difference approximations) with a tolerance under 1e-5.
Construct and train a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) on a non-linear dataset using purely your custom autograd engine and SGD optimizer.
Document the exact mathematical Jacobian-vector products for each operator and analyze computational complexity of forward versus backward graph passes.
Final result: a verified, documented tensor autograd engine written from first principles that executes arbitrary mathematical computation graphs and trains neural networks.

Level 0 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Solve multivariable linear algebra systems and derive singular value decompositions analytically.
Analyze symmetric positive semi-definite matrices and compute spectral projections.
Derive the multivariate chain rule across tensor mappings using index notation.
Explain forward-mode versus reverse-mode automatic differentiation computational complexity.
Calculate gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians of multivariable scalar and vector functions.
Formulate loss functions as maximum likelihood or maximum a posteriori estimation problems.
Calculate Shannon entropy, joint entropy, conditional entropy, and mutual information.
Prove the non-negativity of Kullback-Leibler divergence and analyze mode-covering versus mode-seeking behavior.
Explain the mathematical difference between KL divergence and Wasserstein distance.
Handle floating-point precision issues and implement numerically stable operations like LogSumExp.
Write vectorized and memory-contiguous tensor transformations in PyTorch and JAX.
Use JAX functional primitives including pure functions, grad, vmap, and jit compilation.
Construct a dynamic computational directed acyclic graph and perform topological sorting.
Accumulate multivariable gradients backward through complex computational trees.
Implement tensor broadcasting gradient reduction correctly in custom autograd passes.
Derive analytical gradients for matrix multiplication and multi-layer neural network parameters.
Verify empirical gradients against mathematical derivatives using finite differences.
Read theoretical machine learning research papers containing rigorous linear algebra and probability proofs.
Progression: Linear Algebra → Multivariable Calculus → Probability Theory → Information Theory → Numerical Computing → Custom Autograd Engine.
