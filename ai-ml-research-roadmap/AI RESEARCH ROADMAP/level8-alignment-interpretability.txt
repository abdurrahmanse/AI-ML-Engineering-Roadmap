Level 8 · Alignment Theory, Robustness & Interpretability

Scope: investigate the mathematical foundations of AI alignment, adversarial robustness, and mechanistic interpretability: preference optimization theory, reward hacking, linear representation hypothesis, circuits, induction heads, and activation steering.

Prerequisites: Level 7

M8.1 · Preference Learning Theory: RLHF, DPO & Beyond — (sessions: 4)

Formulate the Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) objective: maximizing expected reward while penalizing divergence from reference policy via KL divergence D_KL(pi_theta || pi_ref).
Analyze Reward Model Training: Bradley-Terry pairwise preference loss log sigma(r(x, y_w) - r(x, y_l)) and margin calibration.
Analyze Reward Hacking and Overoptimization: Goodhart's law in RLHF, where the policy exploits flaws in the learned reward model to achieve high reward without satisfying true human intent (Gao et al.).
Derive Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, Rafailov et al.) mathematically: expressing the optimal policy analytically in terms of ground-truth reward and substituting into the Bradley-Terry objective.
Prove that DPO implicitly optimizes the exact same objective as PPO-based RLHF without training a separate reward model or executing reinforcement learning loops.
Analyze generalizations and variants of DPO: Identity Preference Optimization (IPO, Azar et al.), Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO, Ethayarajh et al.), and Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO).
Investigate Online and Iterative DPO: on-policy negative sampling, self-play preference tuning, and preventing off-policy distribution shift.
Outcome: derive the closed-form relationship between reward functions and optimal policies in DPO, and implement DPO loss from scratch in PyTorch.

M8.2 · Adversarial Robustness, Jailbreaks & Safety Bounds — (sessions: 3)

Formulate adversarial attacks as constrained optimization problems: finding minimal perturbation delta under L_p norm bound that changes model output.
Analyze classical adversarial attacks on vision models: Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM, Goodfellow et al.), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD, Madry et al.), and Carlini-Wagner (CW) attacks.
Study Adversarial Training: minimax optimization min_theta E[max_{||delta||<=epsilon} L(theta, x + delta, y)] and its robustness guarantees.
Analyze Adversarial Vulnerabilities in Language Models: token-level gradient attacks (Greedy Coordinate Gradient, GCG, Zou et al.), suffix optimization, and universal transferable jailbreaks.
Examine Multimodal Jailbreaks: exploiting vision encoders with imperceptible adversarial perturbations that bypass text-based safety guardrails.
Analyze the Safety Alignment Tax: trade-offs where aggressive refusal training reduces model helpfulness, reasoning ability, or mathematical competence.
Outcome: implement Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) adversarial attack on neural representations and evaluate model accuracy drops under bounded perturbations.

M8.3 · Mechanistic Interpretability: Circuits & Induction Heads — (sessions: 4)

Understand the goal of Mechanistic Interpretability: reverse-engineering deep neural network weights and activations into human-understandable computational algorithms and circuits (Olah et al.).
Study the Linear Representation Hypothesis: high-level semantic concepts are represented as linear directions (one-dimensional subspaces) in activation space (Elhage et al.).
Analyze the Transformer as a Residual Stream: viewing layers as reading from and writing to a shared communication bus rather than transforming representations sequentially.
Decompose Self-Attention Mathematically: QK-circuit (determining where information moves) and OV-circuit (determining what information is written to the residual stream).
Master Induction Heads (Olsson et al.): two-layer attention circuit that detects and continues repeated token sequences [A][B] ... [A] -> [B], forming the fundamental mechanism of in-context learning.
Study Polysemanticity and Superposition (Anthropic): models representing more features than they have dimensions by packing non-orthogonal vectors into high-dimensional space.
Analyze Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs): training L1-regularized overcomplete autoencoders on MLP activations to disentangle superposed representations into monosemantic features.
Outcome: implement an attention circuit visualization script that identifies induction heads in a small transformer model through copy-score metrics.

M8.4 · Representation Engineering & Activation Steering — (sessions: 3)

Understand Representation Engineering (RepE, Zou et al.): directly inspecting and manipulating the latent cognitive state of models rather than relying purely on prompt instructions.
Analyze Linear Artificial Tomography (LAT): extracting contrastive activation vectors for concepts like truthfulness, toxicity, sycophancy, or utility.
Master Activation Addition / Steering Vectors: adding a scaled concept direction vector (e.g. + alpha * v_truthful) to residual stream activations during inference.
Compare Steering Vectors versus Prompting: stability across long conversations, resistance to adversarial jailbreaking, and low computational overhead.
Examine Concept Erasing: null-space projections (Iterative Nullspace Projection, INLP, Ravfogel et al.) that completely remove sensitive concept directions from representations.
Analyze the trade-offs of representation steering: unintended collateral damage to language fluency, perplexity degradation, and concept leakage across related topics.
Outcome: extract a truthfulness steering vector by contrastive activation subtraction and demonstrate steerable suppression or elevation of honesty during text generation.

M8.5 · Formal Verification & Auditing for Frontier AI Safety — (sessions: 2)

Analyze Formal Verification of Neural Networks: proving mathematical guarantees that an input within a bounded region epsilon will never produce an unsafe output.
Study Interval Bound Propagation (IBP) and Crown: computing upper and lower bounds on layer activations to certify robustness.
Understand Scalability Limits of Formal Verification: why exact verification is NP-complete and remains intractable for billion-parameter foundation models.
Examine Red Teaming Methodologies: automated red-teaming agents, reinforcement learning for diverse vulnerability discovery (Perez et al.), and human expert evaluation loops.
Study Dangerous Capability Evaluations: autonomous replication benchmarks, cyberattack exploitation capabilities, chemical/biological weapon knowledge elicitation, and power-seeking behavior metrics.
Outcome: implement Interval Bound Propagation (IBP) for a multi-layer network and compute certified robustness bounds against bounded input perturbations.

M8.6 · Capstone: Mechanistic Probing of Latent Truthfulness and Steering Vector Intervention — (sessions: 2)

Design an empirical research project in mechanistic interpretability and alignment: investigating whether an open-weight foundation model internally represents truthfulness when it outputs false statements.
Curate a contrastive dataset of paired statements (True vs False across common misconceptions, factual trivia, and sycophantic traps).
Extract intermediate residual stream activations across all layers and token positions during forward passes.
Train linear probes (Logistic Regression / Ridge) on intermediate activations to classify truthfulness; identify the layers with maximum probe accuracy.
Extract a global Truthfulness Steering Vector using Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA): v = Mean(A_true) - Mean(A_false).
Inject the extracted steering vector into the residual stream at target layers during generation on hallucination-prone questions.
Measure the quantitative impact of activation steering across: (1) truthfulness accuracy, (2) refusal rate, and (3) generation perplexity and coherence.
Visualize attention head contributions and perform attribution patching to locate which specific attention heads write to the truthfulness subspace.
Draft a rigorous academic research paper detailing the linear representation hypothesis findings, ablation of steering magnitudes alpha, and limitations of activation editing.
Final result: an open-source mechanistic interpretability notebook and paper demonstrating extraction of truthfulness representations and activation steering intervention.

Level 8 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Formulate the RLHF objective and explain the role of the KL penalty against the reference policy.
Explain reward hacking and why overoptimizing a proxy reward degrades true model alignment.
Derive the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) loss function mathematically from the Bradley-Terry model.
Explain the differences between DPO, IPO, and KTO in handling implicit reward constraints.
Implement Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) and explain adversarial perturbation bounds in L_p norm spaces.
Explain the mechanics of token-level gradient jailbreak attacks (such as GCG) on language models.
State the Linear Representation Hypothesis and describe how concepts are represented in activation space.
Decompose a Transformer layer into its QK-circuit and OV-circuit components.
Explain the function and mechanism of induction heads in in-context learning.
Explain the phenomenon of superposition and why models pack polysemantic features into activations.
Train a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) to extract monosemantic dictionary features from residual stream activations.
Extract concept steering vectors using contrastive activation analysis.
Perform inference-time activation steering to modulate model behavior without fine-tuning weights.
Apply Interval Bound Propagation (IBP) to certify neural network robustness bounds.
Conduct red teaming audits to identify safety vulnerabilities and capability limits in foundation models.
Progression: Preference Learning Theory & DPO → Adversarial Robustness & Jailbreaks → Mechanistic Interpretability & Circuits → Activation Steering & Representation Engineering → Formal Safety Bounds → Truthfulness Probing Capstone.
