Level 8 · Benchmark Creation, Data Curation & Evaluation Science

Phase: Reasoning, Benchmarks & Rigor
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 7 (Reinforcement Learning, Search & Test-Time Reasoning)
Scope: Master the scientific discipline of dataset curation, benchmark construction, and evaluation rigor: construct validity, annotation agreement statistics (Kappa, Alpha), contamination detection, LLM-as-a-judge calibration, and adversarial robustness testing.

M8.1 · Designing Novel Academic Benchmarks: Construct Validity & Hardness Curves — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Benchmark Design & Construct Validity
- Understand construct validity: ensuring that a benchmark quantitatively measures the intended cognitive or computational capability rather than superficial surface correlations.
- Avoid common benchmark failure modes: shortcut learning, dataset artifact exploitation, and uncalibrated multiple-choice guessing.
- Structure multi-task benchmark suites across distinct difficulty tiers (easy, intermediate, frontier) with clear task stratification.
Session 2 · Hardness Curves & Discriminative Power
- Measure the discriminative power of individual benchmark evaluation items using Item Response Theory (IRT) and difficulty curves.
- Detect ceiling effects (where SOTA models saturate performance) and floor effects (where all models perform at random chance).
- Design dynamic, living benchmarks that continuously evolve to prevent model overfitting to static benchmark distributions over time.
Mental model: A benchmark is a scientific instrument; if the instrument is calibrated on spurious artifacts, all subsequent scientific discoveries are illusions.
Try: Analyze a popular benchmark dataset, identify five examples where a model could predict the correct label using spurious surface heuristics, and fix them.
Outcome: Author novel, robust academic benchmarks with verified construct validity and calibrated discriminative hardness curves.

M8.2 · Data Curation Protocols: Deduplication, Quality Classifiers & Synthetic Generation — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Pre-Training & Fine-Tuning Corpus Curation
- Implement large-scale text preprocessing pipelines: language identification, rule-based heuristic filtering, and toxic content masking.
- Train high-precision domain quality classifiers (fastText, shallow Transformer classifiers) to filter noisy web crawls into textbook-quality corpora.
- Master scalable document deduplication: MinHash locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) and exact substring deduplication to prevent memorization.
Session 2 · Synthetic Data Generation & Data Contamination Risk
- Design synthetic data generation workflows using frontier LLMs: prompt inversion, seed task expansion, and tree-structured problem generation.
- Implement automated verification and filtering pipelines: discard hallucinated synthetic reasoning traces using symbolic verifiers and Python sandboxes.
- Monitor model collapse dynamics: quantify the degradation in linguistic diversity and tail-distribution collapse when models train recursively on synthetic data.
Mental model: High-quality data is the primary driver of capability; filtering out 80% of low-quality tokens yields superior models with lower compute.
Try: Build a data curation pipeline implementing MinHash LSH deduplication and a fastText quality classifier, filtering a raw dataset into a clean research corpus.
Outcome: Curate high-quality scientific datasets, execute massive-scale deduplication, and generate robust synthetic data with automated verification.

M8.3 · Human Annotation Science: Inter-Annotator Agreement (Kappa, Alpha) & Quality Control — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Annotation Guidelines & Human Reliability
- Author exhaustive, unambiguous annotation codebooks specifying label definitions, positive examples, edge cases, and tie-breaking rules.
- Compute pairwise inter-annotator agreement metrics: Cohen’s kappa for two raters and Fleiss’ kappa for multiple fixed raters.
- Apply Krippendorff’s alpha for flexible reliability estimation across arbitrary numbers of raters, missing data, and non-nominal scale types.
Session 2 · Crowdsourcing Quality Control & Resolving Disagreements
- Implement qualification screening tests, attention-check honey-pot instances, and rater velocity monitoring to detect low-effort crowd workers.
- Differentiate between genuine task ambiguity (high epistemic uncertainty) versus low rater competence when resolving annotation disagreements.
- Aggregate multi-annotator distributions using Dawid-Skene latent truth modeling rather than naive majority voting.
Mental model: Inter-annotator agreement quantifies the objective reality of your task; if expert humans cannot agree (alpha < 0.67), a machine cannot learn it.
Try: Conduct an annotation pilot with three raters on 100 complex domain examples, calculate Krippendorff's alpha, and revise the guidelines to resolve ambiguities.
Outcome: Design rigorous human annotation protocols, compute statistical agreement metrics, and resolve labeling disagreements with latent truth models.

M8.4 · Benchmark Contamination Auditing: Suffix Arrays, n-gram Overlap & Decontamination — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Mechanism of Test-Set Contamination
- Understand how test-set contamination occurs when benchmark questions, test splits, or solutions leak into web-scale pre-training or fine-tuning corpora.
- Distinguish between verbatim contamination (exact character overlap) versus paraphrased or synthetic contamination.
- Analyze the catastrophic impact of contamination on scientific progress: inflated benchmark scores, false capabilities, and invalid scaling claims.
Session 2 · Scalable Contamination Detection & Decontamination
- Implement scalable n-gram contamination detection algorithms (e.g., 13-gram exact match scanning) across massive multi-terabyte corpora.
- Build suffix arrays and inverted indices to identify verbatim substring overlaps between evaluation test sets and training token sets.
- Execute automated decontamination protocols: remove contaminated documents or generate clean, private benchmark test splits with verified zero overlap.
Mental model: Benchmark contamination is the AI equivalent of leaking the final exam before the semester starts; clean science requires strict quarantine.
Try: Build a 13-gram contamination scanner using a suffix array, index a training corpus, and measure the exact contamination rate against a benchmark test set.
Outcome: Audit research corpora for benchmark contamination, build scalable n-gram detectors, and guarantee pristine evaluation splits.

M8.5 · LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation: Calibrating Biases, Win-Rates & Bradley-Terry Elo — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Automated Evaluation via LLM Judges
- Understand the methodology of using frontier models as automated evaluators (LLM-as-a-Judge) for open-ended generation, reasoning, and alignment.
- Implement structured pairwise comparison and single-answer scoring prompts with detailed rubrics and chain-of-thought justification.
- Quantify human-to-LLM judge correlation (Spearman rank correlation, Kendall’s tau) to validate judge reliability against human ground truth.
Session 2 · Identifying & Mitigating Judge Biases
- Identify systematic failure modes in LLM judges: position bias (favoring candidate A), verbosity bias (favoring longer responses), and self-enhancement bias.
- Implement mitigation protocols: bidirectional evaluation (averaging swapped A/B orderings), length-normalized scoring, and multi-model judge ensembles.
- Compute global competitive rankings using the Bradley-Terry probabilistic model and maximum likelihood Elo rating systems (LMSYS Chatbot Arena methodology).
Mental model: An LLM judge is a biased human surrogate; swapping candidate order and controlling for token length prevents superficial vanity scoring.
Try: Implement a pairwise LLM evaluation script with bidirectional order swapping, compute candidate win-rates, and estimate Bradley-Terry Elo ratings.
Outcome: Deploy calibrated LLM-as-a-judge pipelines, eliminate position and verbosity biases, and calculate robust Bradley-Terry Elo rankings.

M8.6 · Capstone Project: Decontaminated Domain Benchmark with Human Agreement Calibration — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Benchmark Construction & Quality Annotation
- Construct a novel, multi-task domain-specific evaluation benchmark comprising 500+ curated reasoning questions with calibrated difficulty tiers.
- Formulate an exhaustive annotation codebook, collect multi-annotator judgments, and verify high inter-annotator reliability (Krippendorff’s alpha > 0.80).
- Implement a suffix-array contamination auditing pipeline, verifying that zero evaluation instances leak into standard pre-training sets.
Session 2 · Multi-Model Benchmarking & LLM-as-a-Judge Calibration
- Evaluate a diverse suite of open-source and proprietary foundation models on the decontaminated benchmark across multiple random seeds.
- Implement a bidirectional LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline, calibrating automated judgments against human expert consensus via Kendall’s tau correlation.
- Author an academic benchmark release paper in LaTeX containing dataset statistics, baseline comparison tables, and error distribution analyses.
Mental model: Releasing a pristine, decontaminated benchmark with verified human agreement provides an enduring standard that drives an entire research community forward.
Try: Release your benchmark dataset on Hugging Face Datasets with complete metadata, contamination audit reports, and a verified evaluation harness.
Outcome: Construct, validate, decontaminate, and publish an academic machine learning benchmark that establishes a new gold standard in a scientific domain.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Decontaminated Domain Benchmark with Human Agreement Calibration
BRIEF: Design, curate, and publish a novel domain-specific evaluation benchmark dataset in AI/ML, conduct human annotation with verified inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff’s alpha), execute an automated 13-gram contamination audit against standard pre-training corpora, and benchmark leading open-source models using a calibrated LLM-as-a-judge pipeline.
Build an academic benchmark that:
- Curates at least 500 novel, multi-step domain-specific problem instances spanning at least three stratified difficulty levels.
- Authors comprehensive, peer-reviewed annotation guidelines detailing edge cases, multi-step scoring criteria, and negative examples.
- Collects multi-annotator judgments across a representative pilot subset, computing Krippendorff’s alpha and Fleiss’ kappa to prove high reliability.
- Implements an automated 13-gram contamination auditing tool using suffix arrays or inverted indices to verify zero contamination in common pre-training corpora.
- Evaluates at least four open-source foundation models (e.g., Llama, Mistral, Qwen variants) under standardized prompting protocols.
- Implements a bidirectional LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation harness with position-bias correction and verbosity normalization.
- Computes human-to-judge alignment metrics (Kendall’s tau and Spearman rank correlation), confirming statistically significant judge calibration.
- Emits formatted LaTeX dataset documentation, datasheets for datasets (Gebru et al.), and automated leaderboards with bootstrap confidence intervals.
Deliverables:
- Formally documented dataset package formatted for Hugging Face Datasets with complete data loading scripts and checksum manifests.
- Benchmark evaluation harness codebase (evaluate_benchmark.py) with automated scoring and contamination verification.
- Complete 6-page academic benchmark paper PDF formatted in NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks track style.
Outcome: You can design, curate, decontaminate, and publish standardized evaluation benchmarks that provide rigorous, cheat-proof metrics for scientific AI research.
