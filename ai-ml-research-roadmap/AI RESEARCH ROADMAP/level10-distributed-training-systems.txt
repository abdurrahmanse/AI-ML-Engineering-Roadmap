Level 10 · Pre-Training Dynamics, Scaling Laws & Empirical Science

Phase: HPC, Scaling & Interpretability
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 9 (High-Performance Computing & Scaled Research Systems)
Scope: Master the empirical science of foundation model pre-training: deriving and fitting scaling laws (Kaplan vs. Chinchilla), diagnosing pre-training loss dynamics and loss spikes, data mixture optimization, the scientific debate surrounding emergent abilities, and tokenizer information theory.

M10.1 · Empirical Scaling Laws: Kaplan vs. Chinchilla Power-Law Fitting Methodologies — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Kaplan Power Laws & Model-Centric Scaling
- Formulate the empirical scaling hypothesis: cross-entropy loss scales as a smooth power-law function of compute budget C, dataset size D, and parameter count N.
- Analyze the OpenAI scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020): L(N) = (N_c / N)^{alpha_N}, predicting that allocating compute primarily to model size N is optimal.
- Understand the methodology of fitting power laws: running series of small compute-budget models and extrapolating performance across orders of magnitude.
Session 2 · Chinchilla Scaling Laws & Compute-Optimal Frontiers
- Deconstruct the DeepMind Chinchilla scaling laws (Hoffmann et al., 2022): L(N, D) = E + A / N^alpha + B / D^beta under compute constraint C \approx 6 * N * D.
- Derive the compute-optimal scaling frontier using the method of Lagrange multipliers, proving that parameters N and tokens D should scale equally (alpha \approx beta \approx 0.5).
- Analyze inference-optimal over-training: why open-source foundation models (Llama, Mistral) train on 15T+ tokens far past the Chinchilla compute-optimal point.
Mental model: Scaling laws turn speculative pre-training into predictable engineering; fitting curves on 10M models predicts the exact loss of a 70B model.
Try: Implement a non-linear least squares optimization function in SciPy, fitting the Chinchilla parametric loss equation to a synthetic matrix of (N, D, Loss) runs.
Outcome: Derive and fit empirical scaling laws, calculate compute-optimal parameter allocations, and extrapolate performance across compute budgets.

M10.2 · Pre-Training Optimization Dynamics: Loss Spikes, QK-Norm & z-loss Mitigations — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Pre-Training Loss Trajectories & Phase Transitions
- Analyze normal pre-training loss trajectories: rapid initial power-law descent, steady power-law decay, and asymptotic learning saturation.
- Understand optimization schedules: cosine decay schedules, warmup duration, linear decay, and constant learning rate with cooldown phases (MiniCPM, WSD schedule).
- Monitor gradient norms, weight norm growth, and learning rate dynamics across hundreds of thousands of pre-training optimizer steps.
Session 2 · Diagnosing & Preventing Loss Spikes
- Identify the root physical causes of catastrophic loss spikes during pre-training: attention logit growth causing extreme Softmax values and gradient explosions.
- Implement Query-Key Normalization (QK-Norm): applying LayerNorm or RMSNorm directly to Q and K tensors before computing scaled dot products.
- Master auxiliary stabilizing loss terms: the z-loss penalty (log^2 \sum e^{z_i}) that explicitly penalizes large unnormalized logits in the output vocabulary head.
Mental model: A loss spike is a numerical tsunami in the attention layers; QK-Norm acts as a breakwater, bounding logit amplitudes regardless of scale.
Try: Simulate an unstable pre-training run with exploding attention logits, observe the loss spike, apply QK-Norm, and verify smooth, spike-free convergence.
Outcome: Diagnose pre-training optimization instabilities, eliminate catastrophic loss spikes using QK-Norm, and deploy robust learning rate schedules.

M10.3 · Data Mixture Science: Domain Weighting, Synthetic Data Annealing & Curriculum Learning — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Data Mixture Optimization & Ablations
- Analyze the composition of web-scale pre-training mixtures: web text (Common Crawl), code (GitHub), academic papers (arXiv), and structured knowledge (Wikipedia).
- Conduct data mixture ablation studies: evaluate downstream reasoning, coding, and factual recall as a function of domain mixture percentages.
- Implement automated data weighting: compute gradient alignment between candidate data domains and target evaluation benchmarks (DoReMi methodology).
Session 2 · Curriculum Learning & Annealing Phases
- Implement curriculum learning in pre-training: transitioning from broad web scrapes to high-quality textbooks, code, and synthetic reasoning datasets.
- Master learning rate cooldown and data annealing: injecting specialized high-quality math and code tokens during the final 10-20% of the training run.
- Evaluate the impact of data freshness, deduplication thresholds, and quality filtering on downstream pre-training sample efficiency.
Mental model: Model weights are an empirical compression of the data mixture; changing the mixture percentage reshapes the model's fundamental cognitive abilities.
Try: Train small language models on three contrasting data mixtures (100% web, 50/50 web-code, code-dense), benchmarking downstream reasoning accuracy.
Outcome: Formulate data mixture strategies, optimize domain proportions systematically, and deploy data annealing during pre-training cooldown.

M10.4 · The Emergence Debate: Smooth Metrics vs. Step Discontinuities in Model Capabilities — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Emergent Abilities in Large Language Models
- Review the historical literature on emergent abilities (Wei et al., 2022): the claim that certain capabilities appear abruptly and discontinuously at specific scales.
- Analyze the counter-argument (Schaeffer et al., 2023, "Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?"): emergence as an artifact of non-linear metrics.
- Compare discontinuous step-function metrics (accuracy, exact match) against continuous, smooth metrics (token cross-entropy loss, Brier score).
Session 2 · Mathematical Modeling of Metric Non-Linearities
- Prove mathematically that a smooth, linear decrease in token error probability produces a sharp, non-linear step-function increase on multi-token sequence accuracy.
- Differentiate between genuine qualitative phase shifts (circuit formation, in-context learning emergence) versus measurement artifacts.
- Design rigorous evaluation protocols that report both smooth continuous metrics and discrete milestone accuracy to avoid deceptive claims.
Mental model: If you measure a student's knowledge using pass/fail on a 10-step math problem, a 10% skill increase looks like a sudden miracle; the underlying learning is continuous.
Try: Simulate a model whose per-token error rate decreases linearly, plot the multi-token sequence accuracy curve, and demonstrate the mathematical illusion of emergence.
Outcome: Critically evaluate claims of emergent capabilities in foundation models, distinguish measurement artifacts, and report calibrated metrics.

M10.5 · Tokenizer Science: Byte-Pair Encoding, Vocabulary Entropy & Cross-Lingual Compression — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Tokenization Algorithms & Vocabulary Construction
- Master the mathematics of Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), WordPiece, and Unigram Language Model tokenization algorithms.
- Train tokenizers from scratch using Byte-Level BPE (Hugging Face Tokenizers) with custom normalization rules and regex pre-tokenization splits.
- Analyze the trade-offs of vocabulary size V: larger vocabularies increase embedding parameter count but improve sequence compression and inference speed.
Session 2 · Tokenization Pathologies & Information Theory
- Measure tokenizer efficiency using bytes-per-token compression ratios and Shannon entropy across multilingual and code domains.
- Identify tokenization pathologies: arithmetic token fragmentation, capitalization sensitivity, and whitespace sensitivity in downstream reasoning.
- Evaluate the "fertility" metric: the average number of tokens required to represent a single word across low-resource languages, quantifying linguistic fairness.
Mental model: The tokenizer is the sensory organ of the language model; if numbers are chopped into arbitrary digit fragments, the model struggles with arithmetic.
Try: Train a custom 32k vocabulary BPE tokenizer, calculate bytes-per-token compression ratios across code, English, and low-resource text, and analyze fertility.
Outcome: Build and evaluate custom tokenizers, analyze cross-lingual compression efficiency, and eliminate tokenization pathologies.

M10.6 · Capstone Project: Compute-Optimal Scaling Curve Estimation & Loss Spike Diagnostics — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Multi-Scale Pre-Training Grid Execution
- Train a grid of language models across 5 parameter scales (10M to 150M) and varying token budgets on an HPC cluster.
- Track precise training loss trajectories, token FLOPs, and gradient norms across all grid experiments using real-time experiment logging.
- Fit both Kaplan and Chinchilla power-law scaling models to the empirical loss data using non-linear curve fitting.
Session 2 · Extrapolation Verification & Instability Diagnostics
- Extrapolate the fitted scaling law to predict the exact loss and downstream performance of an unseen larger model scale (300M parameters).
- Evaluate prediction accuracy: train the 300M model and compare empirical loss against the scaling law's predicted confidence interval.
- Author an empirical research paper in LaTeX containing fitted scaling plots, compute-optimal parameter frontier curves, and loss stability analyses.
Mental model: Predicting the exact loss of a large model before launching training is the hallmark of modern empirical AI science.
Try: Fit a Chinchilla power law on small training runs, predict the final loss of a larger model within 2% error margin, and document the findings.
Outcome: Master the empirical science of scaling laws, predict model performance across compute budgets, and diagnose pre-training optimization dynamics.
