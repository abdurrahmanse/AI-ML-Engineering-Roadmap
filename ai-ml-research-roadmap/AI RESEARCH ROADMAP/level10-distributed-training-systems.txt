Level 10 · Foundation Models, Fine-Tuning & Domain Specialization

Phase: HPC Clusters, Scaling & Explainability
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 9 (High-Performance Computing & Scaled Research Systems)
Scope: Investigate the empirical science of foundation models: empirical scaling laws (Kaplan, Chinchilla), pre-training loss dynamics and stability (spikes, z-loss, weight decay), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT: LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA), and Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT) for specialized sectors such as clinical medicine (BioGPT, Clinical-Camel) and cybersecurity threat intelligence (SecBERT, CyberLLaMA).

M10.1 · Empirical Scaling Laws: Kaplan vs Chinchilla Frontiers — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Power-Law Scaling & The Kaplan Paradigm
- Understand the empirical discovery of scaling laws: test cross-entropy loss scales as a power-law with compute (C), dataset size (D), and parameter count (N).
- Analyze the OpenAI Kaplan et al. formulation: predicting performance gains from small compute budgets to large-scale frontier runs.
- Identify the critical limitation of early scaling regimes: disproportionate allocation of compute toward parameter count while starving token counts.
Session 2 · Chinchilla Optimal Compute Frontiers (Hoffmann et al.)
- Deconstruct the DeepMind Chinchilla scaling law: determining that for optimal compute efficiency, parameters and tokens should scale equally (D ~ 20 * N).
- Fit parametric power-law curves to empirical loss sweeps across small model sizes using non-linear least squares optimization.
- Predict downstream task performance, perplexity thresholds, and phase transitions (e.g., emergence of arithmetic or in-context learning capabilities).
Mental model: Scaling laws transform AI pre-training from trial-and-error into a predictive engineering science, allowing researchers to predict giant-model loss from small pilot runs.
Try: Fit Chinchilla power-law parameters to empirical training runs from 5 small models, and project the optimal parameter-token split for a 10^21 FLOP compute budget.
Outcome: Derive and apply empirical scaling laws to predict loss frontiers and compute-optimal resource allocations for pre-training.

M10.2 · Pre-Training Dynamics, Loss Stability & Optimization Surgery — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Loss Spikes, Instability & Numerical Precision
- Diagnose catastrophic training loss spikes: uncovering underlying causes including outlier activation vectors, gradient explosions, and FP16 underflow.
- Master numerical stabilization strategies: adopting Bfloat16 (BF16) precision, implementing QK-Norm (normalizing queries and keys before attention), and RMSNorm.
- Apply auxiliary stabilization losses: implementing auxiliary z-loss (penalizing large log-partition functions) to prevent logits from drifting to extreme values.
Session 2 · Learning Rate Schedules & Optimization Surgery
- Compare learning rate schedules: Cosine Annealing, Linear Warmup-Decay, and modern Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedules facilitating continual pre-training.
- Tune decoupled AdamW hyperparameters: analyzing the impact of beta_2 (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.999), epsilon floor thresholds, and weight decay magnitude.
- Implement gradient norm clipping with adaptive thresholds to suppress anomalous mini-batch gradient shocks without terminating training.
Mental model: Pre-training stability requires continuous vigilance; small architectural hygiene fixes like QK-Norm and z-loss prevent multi-thousand-dollar training runs from diverging.
Try: Simulate an unstable training loop with high learning rates, diagnose the activation outlier pathology, and stabilize the loss trajectory using QK-Norm and z-loss.
Outcome: Diagnose and resolve training instabilities, prevent loss spikes, and tune advanced optimization schedules for stable pre-training.

M10.3 · Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning: LoRA, QLoRA & DoRA — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) Foundations
- Analyze the intrinsic low dimensionality of parameter updates during task adaptation (Aghajanyan et al.).
- Implement Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) (Hu et al.): freezing pre-trained weights W_0 and decomposing weight updates into low-rank matrices B * A (r << d).
- Tune LoRA hyperparameters: rank r, scaling factor alpha, target modules (attention projections vs. feedforward networks), and dropout.
Session 2 · Quantized LoRA (QLoRA) & Weight-Decomposed Adaptation (DoRA)
- Master QLoRA (Dettmers et al.): 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) quantization, Double Quantization, and Paged Optimizers to fine-tune 70B models on consumer GPUs.
- Implement Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation (DoRA) (Liu et al.): decomposing weights into magnitude and directional components to mirror full fine-tuning dynamics.
- Benchmark fine-tuning performance: comparing memory savings, training speed, and validation accuracy against full-parameter fine-tuning baselines.
Mental model: Fine-tuning does not require modifying all 70 billion parameters; learning low-rank directional update vectors preserves knowledge while adapting to new tasks.
Try: Fine-tune a pre-trained open-weight language model on a specialized classification task using QLoRA in 4-bit precision, logging peak VRAM and convergence.
Outcome: Implement, optimize, and benchmark parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques (LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA) on hardware-constrained research systems.

M10.4 · Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT) in Specialized Sectors — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Continual Pre-Training & Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting
- Understand Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT) (Gururangan et al.): continuing self-supervised pre-training on specialized domain corpora.
- Address catastrophic forgetting: the degradation of general reasoning capabilities when updating weights on specialized single-domain distributions.
- Implement experience replay and corpus mixing: blending 15-20% general pre-training data alongside specialized domain tokens to preserve core linguistic and logical abilities.
Session 2 · Domain-Specific Tokenization & Vocabulary Adaptation
- Analyze tokenization pathologies in specialized domains: how standard tokenizers excessively fragment medical terminology or cybersecurity hex code.
- Train custom domain tokenizers (Byte-Pair Encoding BPE) on medical literature (PubMed) or cybersecurity reports (CVEs, threat intelligence).
- Expand model vocabularies with new domain tokens, initializing new embedding weights using mean projections of constituent subwords.
Mental model: DAPT teaches a generalist model the specialized vocabulary, syntax, and relational knowledge of a scientific domain without erasing its foundational intellect.
Try: Perform Continual Pre-Training of a small language model on a curated corpus of clinical abstracts, tracking both clinical perplexity and general reasoning benchmarks.
Outcome: Execute Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training for specialized sectors while mitigating catastrophic forgetting through corpus blending.

M10.5 · Instruction Tuning & Multi-Turn Clinical/Cyber Alignment — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Instruction Data Curation & Format Engineering
- Structure supervised fine-tuning (SFT) datasets formatted for multi-turn instruction following: system prompts, user queries, and assistant responses.
- Implement clinical reasoning formats: structuring diagnostic cases with Patient History, Physical Examination, Differential Diagnosis, and Treatment Plan steps.
- Engineer cybersecurity response formats: formatting Incident Triage, MITRE ATT&CK Mapping, Evidence Artifact Analysis, and Remediation Strategy.
Session 2 · Loss Masking & Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Quality
- Implement completion-only loss masking: calculating cross-entropy loss exclusively on assistant response tokens while masking prompt and instruction tokens.
- Elicit structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning: training models to articulate step-by-step intermediate rationales before outputting clinical or security conclusions.
- Filter synthetic instruction datasets for contamination, factual hallucination, and stylistic repetition using automated rule-based and model-based heuristics.
Mental model: A foundation model trained on raw text predicts the next word; instruction tuning aligns its behavioral interface to act as a structured domain expert.
Try: Format a dataset of 1,000 domain-specific clinical or cybersecurity scenarios with completion-only loss masking, and instruction-tune a base model.
Outcome: Curate high-quality domain instruction datasets, apply completion-only loss masking, and align models for multi-turn scientific reasoning.

M10.6 · Capstone Project: Domain-Adapted Medical or Cybersecurity Foundation Model — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Pre-Training / DAPT Execution & Tokenizer Adaptation
- Execute Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training on a specialized domain corpus: either clinical medicine (PubMed/MIMIC notes) or cybersecurity (CVE/threat reports).
- Apply QLoRA / DoRA or full-weight continued pre-training with Warmup-Stable-Decay learning rates and general corpus replay.
- Track training loss convergence, validation perplexity, and gradient norms across millions of domain tokens on an HPC cluster.
Session 2 · Supervised Instruction Fine-Tuning & Multi-Benchmark Evaluation
- Perform supervised instruction tuning on a curated set of domain-specific multi-turn reasoning scenarios with completion-only loss masking.
- Benchmark the adapted model against the general base model and closed-source baselines on domain benchmarks (e.g., MedQA/PubMedQA or CyberSecEval).
- Measure and document the retention of general knowledge (MMLU) to demonstrate zero catastrophic forgetting in the final research report.
Mental model: Domain adaptation is the premier methodology for creating state-of-the-art scientific models that outperform massive generalist LLMs on specialized tasks.
Try: Run the full DAPT + SFT pipeline, evaluate domain task accuracy against the base model, and generate an automated comparative benchmark table.
Outcome: Deliver a fully trained, domain-specialized foundation model that achieves state-of-the-art domain accuracy while preserving general reasoning capabilities.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Domain-Adapted Medical or Cybersecurity Foundation Model
BRIEF: Perform Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT) followed by Supervised Instruction Tuning (SFT) on an open-weight foundation model using clinical healthcare or cybersecurity corpora, demonstrating statistically significant gains on domain benchmarks without catastrophic forgetting.
Build a domain-adapted foundation model that:
- Ingests a domain corpus (e.g., PubMed clinical papers / MIMIC clinical notes or CVE reports / threat intelligence).
- Implements continual pre-training via PEFT (QLoRA/DoRA) or FSDP with 15% general replay buffer integration to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- Tracks pre-training dynamics: logging validation perplexity curves, learning rate warmups, and loss stability metrics.
- Curates an instruction-tuning dataset of 1,000+ domain scenarios with structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- Trains the instruction model using completion-only loss masking and evaluated across multiple random seeds.
- Benchmarks against the unadapted base model and competitive baselines on established domain test suites (e.g., MedQA/MedMCQA or CyberSecEval).
- Evaluates general capability retention on a subset of MMLU to empirically prove the absence of catastrophic forgetting.
Deliverables:
- Complete research repository (domain_foundation_adaptation/) with modular pre-training, fine-tuning, and evaluation pipelines.
- Open-source model adapter checkpoints and Hugging Face model card documentation.
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation report formatted as a top-tier conference paper draft (ACL / NeurIPS format).
- Comparative performance figures showing domain task accuracy gains alongside general benchmark preservation.
Outcome: You can specialize large foundation models for high-stakes scientific, clinical, and cybersecurity domains with peer-reviewed empirical rigor.
