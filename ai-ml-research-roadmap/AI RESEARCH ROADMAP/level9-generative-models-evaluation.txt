Level 9 · Generative Models

Phase: Architectures & Experiments
Suggested duration: 36 sessions (~9 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 8
Scope: Master probabilistic generative models: derive and implement Variational Autoencoders, Generative Adversarial Networks, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models, Continuous Flow Matching, generative distribution metrics, and the empirical methodology of researching LLMs as generative systems.

M9.1 · Variational Autoencoders (VAE) & Latent Variable Models — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Latent variable formulations & the ELBO derivation
- Intractable Posteriors: Formulate generative latent variable models p(x, z), explain the intractability of true posterior p(z|x), and introduce amortized variational inference q_phi(z|x).
- Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO): Derive the mathematical ELBO from Jensen's inequality: log p(x) >= E[log p(x|z)] - D_KL(q(z|x) || p(z)), balancing reconstruction and prior regularization.
Session 2 · The reparameterization trick & beta-VAE
- Reparameterization Trick: Derive backpropagation through stochastic sampling nodes by decomposing latent variables as z = mu + sigma * epsilon where epsilon ~ N(0, I).
- Latent Space Disentanglement: Implement beta-VAE to scale the KL regularization penalty and evaluate factorized, disentangled latent representation dimensions.
Mental model: A VAE maps complex high-dimensional data into a continuous, smooth low-dimensional latent manifold where every point can be decoded into a realistic sample.
Try: Implement a convolutional VAE from scratch in PyTorch, derive and verify the analytical Gaussian KL divergence, and visualize 2D latent space manifold interpolations.
Outcome: You can derive the ELBO, implement the reparameterization trick, and analyze continuous latent representations.

M9.2 · Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) & Minimax Dynamics — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Two-player zero-sum games & the minimax objective
- Minimax Formulation: Formulate the two-player zero-sum game between Generator G(z) and Discriminator D(x): min_G max_D V(D, G).
- Divergence Minimization: Prove that under an optimal discriminator, the minimax game minimizes the Jensen-Shannon Divergence between true and generated distributions.
Session 2 · Training pathologies, Wasserstein distance & WGAN-GP
- Training Instabilities: Analyze mode collapse, vanishing gradients under optimal discriminators, and cyclic non-convergence limit cycles.
- Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty: Derive the Earth Mover's Distance via Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality, enforce 1-Lipschitz continuity with gradient penalty (WGAN-GP).
Mental model: A GAN is an adversarial arms race; the discriminator provides an adaptive, learnable loss function that guides the generator to match the real data manifold.
Try: Train a WGAN-GP on an image dataset, plot the continuous Wasserstein loss critic curves, and verify that it avoids the mode collapse failure of a standard vanilla GAN.
Outcome: You can formulate adversarial games, implement Wasserstein GANs with gradient penalty, and diagnose training instabilities.

M9.3 · Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) & Score-Based SDEs — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Forward diffusion process & closed-form sampling
- Forward Gaussian Markov Chain: Formulate the forward process adding scheduled Gaussian noise beta_1...beta_T; derive closed-form sampling of x_t directly from x_0 at any step t.
- Reverse Denoising Process: Parameterize the reverse mean using a neural network predicting the injected noise epsilon_theta(x_t, t) under the simplified MSE variational bound.
Session 2 · Score matching, SDEs & Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)
- Score-Based Perspective: Unify diffusion with Score-Based Generative Modeling (Song et al.) via continuous Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) and Probability Flow ODEs.
- Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG): Implement unconditional label dropout during training and scale conditional score estimates during inference to trade diversity for sample quality.
Mental model: Diffusion gradually dissolves data structure into pure Gaussian chaos, then trains a neural network to systematically reverse entropy step by step.
Try: Code a 1D or 2D toy diffusion model from scratch in PyTorch, implement the analytical closed-form forward sampling, and visualize the reverse denoising trajectory over 1,000 steps.
Outcome: You can derive forward and reverse diffusion processes, implement DDPM training and sampling loops, and apply Classifier-Free Guidance.

M9.4 · Continuous Flow Matching (CFM) & Optimal Transport Paths — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Normalizing flows & continuous-time neural ODEs
- Change of Variables: Formulate finite normalizing flows via invertible mappings and Jacobian determinants; transition to Continuous-Time Normalizing Flows (CNFs).
- Neural ODEs: Model continuous probability trajectories using parameterized time-dependent velocity vector fields: dx/dt = v_theta(x, t).
Session 2 · Flow matching & Optimal Transport probability paths
- Vector Field Regression: Formulate Flow Matching (Lipman et al.) to regress vector fields directly without expensive numerical ODE integration during training.
- Optimal Transport Flow Matching (OT-CFM): Construct straight probability trajectories between Gaussian noise and target data distributions, enabling fast ODE generation in 10-20 steps.
Mental model: Flow Matching learns straight, deterministic vector fields that push pure noise into complex data manifolds without the curved, stochastic paths of diffusion.
Try: Train an Optimal Transport Flow Matching model on a 2D multimodal distribution, solve the reverse trajectory with an explicit Euler ODE solver, and compare steps vs diffusion.
Outcome: You can formulate continuous-time neural ODEs, implement Optimal Transport Flow Matching, and sample high-quality generations with fast numerical solvers.

M9.5 · Generative Evaluation Metrics, Distribution Fidelity & Memorization Audits — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Distribution fidelity: FID & Inception Score
- Frechet Inception Distance (FID): Formulate the Wasserstein-2 distance between Gaussian-approximated Inception feature representations of real and synthetic distributions.
- Inception Score (IS) & Limitations: Calculate IS combining conditional confidence and marginal entropy; analyze sensitivity to class priors and sample counts.
Session 2 · Precision, recall & training data memorization audits
- Precision & Recall for Distributions: Disentangle sample quality (precision) from mode coverage (recall) using k-nearest neighbor hyperspheres in feature space.
- Memorization & Privacy Auditing: Audit generative models for training data memorization by computing nearest-neighbor distances in latent space to training samples.
Mental model: Generative quality cannot be judged by human visual inspection alone; rigorous evaluation requires measuring distribution overlap, diversity, and memorization.
Try: Compute the FID and Precision-Recall curves between real and generated distributions, and audit nearest-neighbor distances to detect whether the generator is copying training data.
Outcome: You can evaluate generative models using FID, Inception Score, and Precision/Recall, while conducting automated training data memorization audits.

M9.6 · Capstone: Denoising Diffusion & Continuous Flow Matching Generative Benchmark — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Model implementation & training under compute parity
- Implement a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) and an Optimal Transport Flow Matching (OT-CFM) model using a shared U-Net backbone on a complex dataset.
- Train both models to convergence under equal GPU compute budgets, logging loss curves, step latencies, and parameter checkpoint sizes.
Session 2 · Empirical distribution benchmarking & scientific manuscript
- Evaluate sample quality using FID, Precision, Recall, and perceptual similarity (LPIPS) across varying sampling step counts (5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 500 steps).
- Author a 6-page comparative research paper in LaTeX documenting theoretical foundations, generation speedup of Flow Matching over DDPM, and memorization audit findings.
Final result: A comprehensive comparative benchmark paper and code repository evaluating Denoising Diffusion and Optimal Transport Flow Matching under equal compute parity.

M9.7 · Researching Large Language Models as Generative Systems — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Studying in-context learning and emergent capabilities empirically
- In-Context Learning Experimental Protocol: Design a controlled study of in-context learning (ICL) that systematically isolates the contribution of: (1) label format (what the output label tokens look like), (2) label correctness (using random wrong labels vs correct labels), and (3) example ordering (first vs last examples) — following Min et al. (2022) "Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations" methodology — to determine what ICL examples actually teach the model.
- Emergent Capabilities Measurement: Critically evaluate claims of emergent capabilities in large language models (Wei et al., 2022) by testing whether apparent emergence is a consequence of metric discontinuity (using accuracy rather than a continuous metric like log-probability) or genuine qualitative capability jumps; replicate the analysis of Schaeffer et al. (2023) "Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?" on a small open model.
- Chain-of-Thought Research: Design a study measuring how chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) affects reasoning performance across problem difficulty levels, measuring the marginal benefit of CoT over direct prompting on GSM8K grade school math problems stratified by number of reasoning steps required.
Session 2 · Hallucination measurement and memorization auditing in LLMs
- Hallucination Rate Measurement Protocol: Define and operationalize LLM hallucination precisely for a specific domain (e.g. medical question answering, biographical facts, scientific citations); design a test set where ground truth is verifiable from authoritative sources; measure hallucination rate as the fraction of generated claims that cannot be verified in the source corpus; compute 95% confidence intervals.
- Detecting Training Data Memorization in LLMs: Apply the Carlini et al. (2021) membership inference methodology to test whether an open LLM has memorized specific training documents: prompt the model with a prefix from a suspected training document and measure whether the continuation matches the original text at above-chance rates using perplexity comparison between the memorized and non-memorized continuation.
- Distinguishing Generalization from Pattern Matching: Design an experiment using held-out perturbed test cases (e.g. GSM8K problems with numbers changed to values not seen in training) to test whether LLM mathematical performance reflects genuine arithmetic generalization or memorized answer patterns.
Mental model: Researching LLMs as generative systems requires treating every impressive capability claim as a hypothesis to be falsified, not a fact to be celebrated — the questions are always "does this capability hold under distribution shift?" and "does removing training data contamination eliminate the capability?".
Try: Design and execute a study testing whether in-context few-shot learning examples help an open LLM (e.g. Llama-3-8B) on a classification task because of their label format or their label correctness: use 4 conditions (correct labels, wrong labels, no labels, no examples), run 500 test examples per condition, and compute McNemar significance tests comparing all condition pairs.
Outcome: You can design controlled experiments to study in-context learning, measure hallucination rates with rigorous protocols, detect training data memorization in LLMs, and evaluate whether capability claims survive distribution shift testing.
