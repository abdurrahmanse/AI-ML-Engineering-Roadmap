Level 13 · Trustworthy AI & Safety

Phase: Trustworthy AI
Suggested duration: 32 sessions (~8 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 12
Scope: Deconstruct black-box models using post-hoc XAI and mechanistic interpretability (Sparse Autoencoders), audit adversarial robustness (PGD, AutoAttack), measure algorithmic fairness, and lead independent research programs with risk registers and decision gates.

M13.1 · Explainable AI (XAI): Attribution, Saliency & Sanity Checks — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Game-theoretic attribution: SHAP & LIME
- Shapley Value Foundations: Derive Shapley values from cooperative game theory; prove the axioms of Efficiency, Symmetry, Dummy Player, and Additivity.
- KernelSHAP & TreeSHAP: Implement TreeSHAP for fast exact computation on tree ensembles and KernelSHAP for black-box surrogate approximations (LIME).
Session 2 · Gradient attribution & sanity check diagnostics
- Axiomatic Gradient Attribution: Formulate Integrated Gradients across straight interpolation paths from a neutral baseline, fulfilling Completeness and Implementation Invariance.
- Saliency Map Sanity Checks: Apply Adebayo et al. sanity checks (randomizing model weights and labels) to detect when visual saliency methods act merely as edge detectors.
Mental model: An explanation is not an intuitive story; it is an axiomatic measurement of how much each input feature contributed to shifting the model output from a baseline.
Try: Compute Integrated Gradients and KernelSHAP on a deep neural network, conduct a model-weight randomization sanity check, and identify features with spurious attribution.
Outcome: You can calculate mathematically grounded feature attributions using SHAP and Integrated Gradients while verifying them with saliency sanity checks.

M13.2 · Mechanistic Interpretability & Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Circuit analysis, residual streams & induction heads
- Mechanistic Deconstruction: Trace activations through the residual stream, attention heads, and MLP blocks, treating neural networks as readable computational circuits.
- In-Context Induction Heads: Identify two-head induction circuits that implement pattern copying ([A][B]...[A] -> [B]) to explain in-context few-shot learning in transformers.
Session 2 · Polysemanticity & Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)
- Superposition Theory: Explain why individual neurons fire on unrelated concepts (polysemanticity) as models compress more features than physical neuron dimensions.
- Dictionary Learning with SAEs: Train Sparse Autoencoders on residual activations using L1 sparsity penalties to extract monosemantic, interpretable feature directions.
Mental model: Neurons are not the fundamental units of thought; Sparse Autoencoders decompress entangled superposition into true, monosemantic semantic concepts.
Try: Train a Sparse Autoencoder on the residual stream activations of a small open language model, extracting interpretable dictionary features that activate on specific semantic themes.
Outcome: You can dissect transformer circuits, locate induction heads, and train Sparse Autoencoders to extract monosemantic features from residual representations.

M13.3 · Adversarial Robustness, Evasion Attacks & Certified Defenses — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Adversarial evasion attacks: FGSM, PGD & AutoAttack
- Adversarial Optimization: Formulate adversarial evasion attacks as constrained optimization: maximize loss subject to an Lp norm bound (||x_adv - x||_p <= epsilon).
- Gradient-Based Attacks: Implement Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), multi-step Projected Gradient Descent (PGD / Madry defense), and the parameter-free AutoAttack ensemble.
Session 2 · Robust training, certified bounds & jailbreak defenses
- Adversarial Training: Train models via robust minimax optimization (Madry defense), generating PGD adversarial examples online during the forward-backward pass.
- Certified Defenses & LLM Jailbreaks: Formulate randomized smoothing for certified robust radii; evaluate LLM jailbreaking attacks (GCG gradient attacks) and refusal steering.
Mental model: A model that is 99% accurate on clean test data can be 0% accurate under a tiny, imperceptible perturbation; robustness measures worst-case generalization.
Try: Implement PGD adversarial training on an image classifier in PyTorch, evaluate robust accuracy against white-box and black-box AutoAttack, and measure the clean-accuracy trade-off.
Outcome: You can formulate adversarial attack optimization, execute PGD and AutoAttack benchmarks, and train certifiably robust models.

M13.4 · Algorithmic Fairness, Bias Auditing & Differential Privacy — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Mathematical fairness criteria & impossibility theorems
- Fairness Definitions: Formulate Demographic Parity (P(Y_hat=1|A=0) = P(Y_hat=1|A=1)), Equalized Odds (matching TPR and FPR across groups), and Predictive Parity.
- The Impossibility Theorem: Prove mathematically (Kleinberg et al., Chouldechova) that Demographic Parity, Equalized Odds, and Calibration cannot simultaneously hold when base rates differ.
Session 2 · Bias mitigation & Differential Privacy (DP-SGD)
- Mitigation Interventions: Apply pre-processing (reweighing), in-processing (adversarial debiasing), and post-processing (threshold optimization) to equalize subgroup outcomes.
- Differential Privacy: Formulate (epsilon, delta)-Differential Privacy; implement DP-SGD with per-sample gradient clipping and calibrated Gaussian noise injection.
Mental model: Fairness is not a single objective to maximize; it is a series of mutually incompatible mathematical criteria requiring explicit ethical and legal trade-offs.
Try: Audit a clinical risk model across demographic subgroups, calculate disparate impact ratios and equalized odds deltas, and implement fair threshold optimization.
Outcome: You can audit algorithmic fairness, prove mathematical trade-offs between fairness criteria, and train differentially private models with DP-SGD.

M13.5 · Independent Research Leadership, Risk Registers & Decision Gates — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Structuring multi-stage research programs
- Research Program Roadmapping: Break complex scientific hypotheses into sequential milestones with measurable empirical gates and fallback contingencies.
- Risk Registers: Maintain an active research risk register tracking data acquisition delays, compute overruns, negative pilot results, and collaboration dependencies.
Session 2 · Go/No-Go decision gates & mentorship
- Scientific Decision Gates: Establish objective quantitative criteria for continuing, pivoting, or terminating a research project when empirical evidence disproves initial hypotheses.
- Collaborative Research Culture: Lead code review sessions, establish lab documentation standards, and mentor junior researchers while maintaining intellectual honesty.
Mental model: Research leadership is the management of uncertainty; great leaders make uncertainty visible early enough for the team to pivot before wasting resources.
Try: Create a complete 12-week research roadmap and risk register for a proposed AI project, establishing explicit go/no-go empirical gates at Week 4 and Week 8.
Outcome: You can direct collaborative research programs, manage project risk registers, and make objective go/no-go decisions based on empirical evidence.

M13.6 · Capstone: Mechanistic Interpretability & Adversarial Robustness Audit — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · Multi-dimensional trust audit of an open foundation model
- Conduct an exhaustive trustworthiness audit of an open model, combining Sparse Autoencoder feature extraction, white-box PGD adversarial attacks, and demographic fairness audits.
- Isolate semantic circuits responsible for specific predictions, evaluate robust accuracy degradation under AutoAttack, and quantify subgroup calibration errors.
Session 2 · Comprehensive audit dossier & leadership presentation
- Author a formal 6-page Trustworthy AI Research Paper in LaTeX detailing mechanistic findings, adversarial vulnerability surfaces, and mitigation trade-offs.
- Deliver an executive scientific briefing presenting key audit findings, actionable governance recommendations, and residual risk disclosures to a technical committee.
Final result: A comprehensive mechanistic interpretability and adversarial robustness audit dossier on an open foundation model, accompanied by a publication-ready paper.
