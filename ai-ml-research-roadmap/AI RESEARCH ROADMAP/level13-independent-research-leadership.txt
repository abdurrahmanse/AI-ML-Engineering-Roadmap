Level 13 · Independent Research and Project Leadership

Phase: Cross-Disciplinary Research & Capstones
Suggested duration: 30 sessions (~7 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 12
Scope: Move from completing exercises to leading a defensible research program with milestones, collaborators, risks, and a clear contribution.

M13.1 · From Literature Gap to Contribution — (sessions: 5)
- Map a field's established findings, open problems, competing explanations, and available resources.
- Test whether a proposed contribution is novel, feasible, important, and distinguishable from prior work.
- Define a minimal publishable question and a sequence of risk-reducing pilot studies.
- Break an open-ended idea into milestones, decision gates, collaborator responsibilities, and a budget for data and compute.
- Run a pilot, review progress against the protocol, and make explicit go/no-go decisions when evidence changes the plan.
Mental model: A contribution is a new, reliable answer to a meaningful question, not just a new implementation.
Try: Write a novelty matrix comparing a proposed study with ten closest papers.
Outcome: You can turn a literature gap into a bounded contribution with a credible plan.

M13.2 · Research Planning and Collaboration — (sessions: 5)
- Break a project into hypotheses, work packages, milestones, dependencies, and decision gates.
- Assign roles, maintain meeting notes, manage review cycles, and resolve disagreements with evidence.
- Track compute, data, ethics, authorship, and schedule risks before they become blockers.
- Break an open-ended idea into milestones, decision gates, collaborator responsibilities, and a budget for data and compute.
- Run a pilot, review progress against the protocol, and make explicit go/no-go decisions when evidence changes the plan.
Mental model: Research leadership makes uncertainty visible early enough for a team to act.
Try: Run a weekly research meeting using a decision log, risk register, and next-evidence checklist.
Outcome: You can coordinate a research project without losing scientific ownership.

M13.3 · Scientific Presentation and Mentorship — (sessions: 5)
- Explain a contribution to a beginner, peer, domain expert, reviewer, and hiring panel.
- Design a talk around question, insight, evidence, limitations, and next experiment.
- Give constructive code, paper, and presentation feedback while preserving the author's agency.
- Break an open-ended idea into milestones, decision gates, collaborator responsibilities, and a budget for data and compute.
- Run a pilot, review progress against the protocol, and make explicit go/no-go decisions when evidence changes the plan.
Mental model: If the claim cannot survive a change in audience, it probably has not been understood deeply enough.
Try: Deliver the same five-minute result briefing to technical and non-technical listeners and revise it from questions.
Outcome: You can communicate evidence and mentor others through a research process.

M13.4 · Milestones, Risk Registers and Decision Gates — (sessions: 3)
- Convert a research question into milestones with evidence criteria, dependencies, owners, and stop conditions.
- Maintain a risk register for data, ethics, compute, validity, collaboration, and publication risks.
Mental model: Research leadership makes uncertainty visible early enough for a team to act.
Try: Hold a project review at the first decision gate and document a changed plan.
Outcome: You can manage a research project without hiding uncertainty until the deadline.

M13.5 · Collaboration, Mentorship and Research Culture — (sessions: 3)
- Define authorship expectations, contribution records, meeting notes, code review, and escalation paths.
- Give feedback on claims, evidence, and limitations while preserving psychological safety and intellectual honesty.
Mental model: Collaboration quality is part of the reproducibility of a research program.
Try: Create a contributor guide and a decision log for a small research team.
Outcome: You can lead a healthy, accountable research collaboration.

M13.6 · Capstone: Independent Study Plan — (sessions: 9)
- Produce a six-to-twelve-week study plan with literature, hypotheses, pilot, evaluation, risks, and deliverables.
- Complete a pilot that can falsify the main idea before committing to full implementation.
- Present a go/no-go decision supported by evidence and a revised project schedule.
Final result: A leadership-ready research plan that protects time, compute, collaborators, and scientific validity.
