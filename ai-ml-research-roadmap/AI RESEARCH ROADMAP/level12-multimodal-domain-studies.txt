Level 12 · Multimodal and Domain Research

Phase: Scaled Systems, Trustworthy AI & Alignment
Suggested duration: 30 sessions (~7 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 11
Scope: Translate research methods across images, text, audio, tables, and domain workflows while working with experts who understand the problem better than the model builder.

M12.1 · Multimodal Representation and Fusion — (sessions: 5)
- Align image, text, audio, tabular, and temporal representations with suitable encoders and objectives.
- Compare early fusion, late fusion, cross-attention, contrastive learning, and missing-modality strategies.
- Evaluate whether a multimodal gain comes from useful interaction or simply additional information.
- Work with domain experts to define clinically, scientifically, or operationally meaningful outcomes and unacceptable failure modes.
- Align modalities, annotations, and evaluation splits while documenting consent, domain shift, and interdisciplinary assumptions.
Mental model: Combining modalities is a measurement and alignment problem before it is an architecture problem.
Try: Build a two-modality classifier with modality ablations and inspect performance when one modality is missing.
Outcome: You can design multimodal experiments that expose the source of a claimed improvement.

M12.2 · Domain Adaptation and Expert Collaboration — (sessions: 5)
- Translate a domain workflow into labels, constraints, outcomes, and reviewable decision points.
- Use domain interviews, annotation pilots, disagreement analysis, and expert validation.
- Distinguish benchmark success from usefulness, safety, and adoption in the real workflow.
- Work with domain experts to define clinically, scientifically, or operationally meaningful outcomes and unacceptable failure modes.
- Align modalities, annotations, and evaluation splits while documenting consent, domain shift, and interdisciplinary assumptions.
Mental model: Domain experts are research partners who define validity, not merely label providers.
Try: Run an annotation pilot with a domain reviewer and revise the codebook from disagreements.
Outcome: You can conduct cross-disciplinary research without flattening domain knowledge.

M12.3 · Applied Research and Deployment Evidence — (sessions: 5)
- Design prospective, retrospective, offline, and human-in-the-loop evaluations.
- Measure workflow time, error costs, calibration, accessibility, adoption barriers, and unintended effects.
- Write a deployment decision record with monitoring, rollback, and ownership.
- Work with domain experts to define clinically, scientifically, or operationally meaningful outcomes and unacceptable failure modes.
- Align modalities, annotations, and evaluation splits while documenting consent, domain shift, and interdisciplinary assumptions.
Mental model: A research prototype becomes an applied contribution only when its context and limits are demonstrated.
Try: Create an evaluation plan for a domain partner that includes technical and human success criteria.
Outcome: You can connect a model result to a defensible applied research decision.

M12.4 · Alignment, Fusion and Missing-Modality Analysis — (sessions: 3)
- Compare early, intermediate, late, and cross-attention fusion under matched data and compute budgets.
- Test missing modalities, noisy annotations, domain shift, and modality-specific failure modes.
Mental model: More modalities add opportunities for evidence and more ways for a model to fail silently.
Try: Create a modality ablation matrix with expert-approved failure categories.
Outcome: You can evaluate whether multimodal fusion adds reliable information.

M12.5 · Expert Review and Domain Validity — (sessions: 3)
- Co-design outcomes, annotation protocols, error taxonomies, and acceptable thresholds with domain experts.
- Document what the model can support operationally and what still requires human judgment.
Mental model: Domain expertise changes the question, not just the label quality.
Try: Run an expert review session and revise the evaluation protocol from disagreement.
Outcome: You can communicate technical evidence in the language and constraints of a real domain.

M12.6 · Capstone: Cross-Disciplinary Multimodal Study — (sessions: 9)
- Partner with a domain expert or use a documented public workflow and formulate one measurable question.
- Build a multimodal baseline, run modality and stakeholder-informed ablations, and report limitations.
- Deliver a technical report, domain-facing briefing, data statement, and reproducible artifact.
Final result: A complete cross-disciplinary study showing scientific, technical, and communication maturity.
