Level 13 · Multimodal & Cross-Disciplinary Research Methodologies

Phase: Frontier Robustness & Thesis Projects
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 12 (AI Alignment, Robustness & Adversarial Defense)
Scope: Explore cross-disciplinary AI research spanning multiple modalities: joint vision-language representation learning (CLIP, SigLIP), multimodal foundation models (LLaVA, Med-PaLM M, BioMedCLIP) for radiology report generation and clinical decision support, cross-modal retrieval, fusing disparate data streams (e.g., continuous ICU physiological waveforms with physician clinical notes, or network packet captures with threat intelligence reports), and formulating interdisciplinary research hypotheses.

M13.1 · Contrastive Vision-Language Learning: CLIP & BioMedCLIP — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training (CLIP & SigLIP)
- Derive the symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss over paired visual and textual batches (Radford et al. CLIP).
- Analyze learned temperature parameters and the catastrophic memory scaling of global softmax normalization across massive distributed batch sizes.
- Master SigLIP (Zhai et al.): replacing softmax with pairwise sigmoid loss to stabilize distributed training and improve zero-shot classification accuracy.
Session 2 · Domain-Specific Vision-Language Pre-Training (BioMedCLIP)
- Train specialized contrastive models on domain-specific scientific image-text pairs (e.g., BioMedCLIP trained on PubMed Central figures and captions).
- Formulate zero-shot domain classification: generating clinical prompt templates (e.g., "a chest radiograph showing signs of [CLASS]") for pathology identification.
- Evaluate cross-modal retrieval: querying vast radiological archives using natural language queries and measuring Recall@K metrics.
Mental model: Joint contrastive pre-training embeds disparate modalities into a shared geometric space where semantically related concepts cluster together regardless of origin.
Try: Implement a miniature CLIP contrastive loss module in PyTorch, calculate similarity matrices between image embeddings and text embeddings, and compute zero-shot accuracy.
Outcome: Implement and train contrastive vision-language models, perform zero-shot domain classification, and execute cross-modal semantic retrieval.

M13.2 · Vision-Language Foundation Models: LLaVA & Medical VLMs — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Visual Token Projection & Multimodal Architecture (LLaVA)
- Deconstruct the modern Vision-Language Model (VLM) architecture: visual backbone encoder (CLIP ViT), projection adapter (MLP), and autoregressive LLM decoder.
- Implement projection strategies: linear projections, multi-layer perceptron connectors, and cross-attention resamplers (Flamingo / Perceiver).
- Master two-stage multimodal training: Stage 1 visual-language alignment (training the projection layer while freezing backbones) and Stage 2 end-to-end instruction tuning.
Session 2 · Medical Multimodal Decoders & Radiology Report Generation
- Architect medical VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-Med, Med-PaLM M) for visual question answering (VQA) on clinical imaging (CT, MRI, ultrasound).
- Formulate automated radiology report generation: generating structured clinical findings and impression sections directly from multi-view chest radiographs.
- Evaluate clinical text generation: combining natural language metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) with clinical factual precision metrics (CheXbert-based F1, Green score).
Mental model: A VLM translates visual patches into foreign language tokens, allowing the text decoder to reason over visual concepts as if they were written words.
Try: Fine-tune a Vision-Language Model on a medical VQA dataset using LoRA, evaluating clinical factual accuracy and report generation quality.
Outcome: Design and fine-tune Vision-Language foundation models for domain-specific visual reasoning and automated scientific report generation.

M13.3 · Heterogeneous Multimodal Fusion: Tabular, Time-Series & Text — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Multimodal Fusion Strategies (Early, Late & Cross-Attention)
- Compare multimodal fusion paradigms: early fusion (feature concatenation), late fusion (decision ensembling), and intermediate hybrid fusion.
- Implement cross-attention fusion: allowing query vectors from one modality (e.g., clinical text notes) to attend to key-value representations of another (e.g., ICU waveforms).
- Handle missing modalities during inference: implementing modality dropout and robust fusion layers that operate effectively when specific data streams are absent.
Session 2 · Fusing Longitudinal Records with Unstructured Clinical Notes
- Ingest heterogeneous patient streams from MIMIC-IV: aligning continuous ICU physiological waveforms (ECG, blood pressure), discrete lab events, and physician progress notes.
- Address asynchronous sampling rates: temporal alignment of high-frequency continuous signals alongside sparse, irregularly sampled clinical lab events.
- Benchmark multimodal fusion performance against unimodal baselines: demonstrating the concrete empirical benefit of cross-modal representation learning.
Mental model: Intermediate cross-attention allows dense physiological telemetry to ground the ambiguous descriptions found in human-written clinical progress notes.
Try: Build a cross-attention fusion module in PyTorch that ingests tabular lab values and unstructured text embeddings, predicting patient ICU readmission risk.
Outcome: Design and train intermediate fusion architectures that combine heterogeneous tabular, continuous time-series, and unstructured text data.

M13.4 · Multimodal Cybersecurity: Fusing Packet Telemetry & Threat Intelligence — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Aligning Low-Level Telemetry with High-Level Threat Text
- Bridge the semantic gap in cybersecurity: connecting low-level binary packet captures (PCAP) and system logs with high-level human threat intelligence reports.
- Represent security telemetry multimodally: combining structured network flow features with unstructured CVE vulnerability descriptions and MITRE ATT&CK tactics.
- Train joint embedding spaces mapping observed intrusion telemetry directly to standardized MITRE ATT&CK technique taxonomies.
Session 2 · Multimodal Incident Correlation & Threat Hunting
- Implement multimodal incident triage: correlating anomalous network flow spikes with endpoint process execution trees and firewall text logs.
- Detect coordinated multi-stage advanced persistent threats (APTs) by tracing cross-modal causality across heterogeneous enterprise data streams.
- Evaluate false positive suppression: demonstrating how cross-modal verification eliminates spurious alerts generated by single-modality intrusion sensors.
Mental model: A network anomaly in isolation is just noise; correlated with an endpoint process execution and a CVE exploit pattern, it becomes verified threat intelligence.
Try: Build a joint embedding model that maps raw network flow anomaly vectors to corresponding MITRE ATT&CK tactic descriptions in a shared latent space.
Outcome: Formulate and implement cross-modal cybersecurity fusion systems linking raw network telemetry to high-level threat intelligence corpora.

M13.5 · Interdisciplinary Research Collaboration & Domain Expert Co-Design — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Collaborating with Medical Clinicians & Security Operators
- Understand the human-in-the-loop paradigm: establishing collaborative research partnerships with medical doctors, radiologists, and enterprise security analysts.
- Conduct workflow observations and qualitative interviews to identify genuine clinical or operational bottlenecks rather than artificial academic benchmarks.
- Navigate institutional review boards (IRB) and ethical oversight committees: protocol authoring, data use agreements (DUA), and patient consent management.
Session 2 · Co-Designing Evaluation Metrics & Clinical Utility
- Co-design domain-grounded evaluation metrics with experts: moving beyond raw AUROC to actionable clinical net benefit curves and operational false discovery rates.
- Implement Decision Curve Analysis (DCA): quantifying the clinical utility of deploying an AI diagnostic model across varying decision threshold preferences.
- Prepare collaborative interdisciplinary grant applications and multi-author publications spanning computer science, medicine, and engineering.
Mental model: An algorithm with 99% accuracy is useless if it disrupts clinical workflow or creates alert fatigue; co-designing with practitioners ensures real-world impact.
Try: Conduct a Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) on a diagnostic model in Python, plotting net clinical benefit across diagnostic threshold probabilities.
Outcome: Establish interdisciplinary research collaborations, navigate IRB protocols, and co-design domain-grounded evaluation metrics with expert practitioners.

M13.6 · Capstone Project: Cross-Disciplinary Multimodal Clinical or Cyber Research System — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Multimodal Architecture & Data Ingestion Pipeline
- Build an end-to-end multimodal research system integrating at least two distinct modalities: imaging + text (radiology report generation) or telemetry + text (cyber threat fusion).
- Ingest and synchronize heterogeneous domain datasets: ensuring strict temporal alignment and entity-level (patient/host) partitioning.
- Implement contrastive alignment (CLIP/SigLIP) or intermediate cross-attention fusion across the multimodal backbones.
Session 2 · Empirical Evaluation, Unimodal Ablations & Clinical/Security Reporting
- Benchmark the multimodal system against isolated unimodal baselines: proving statistically significant performance gains achieved through cross-modal fusion.
- Conduct extensive ablation studies: evaluating the impact of cross-attention layer depth, projection connectors, and modality dropout rates.
- Package the research system into a publication-ready code release with interactive visual dashboards demonstrating cross-modal attention heatmaps.
Mental model: High-impact cross-disciplinary research proves that combining modalities solves problems that no single modality could solve alone.
Try: Run the full multimodal pipeline, log training metrics and cross-modal attention weights, and generate a 4-page interdisciplinary conference paper draft.
Outcome: Deliver a complete, publication-ready multimodal research system validated against unimodal baselines on real-world scientific datasets.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Cross-Disciplinary Multimodal Clinical or Cyber Research System
BRIEF: Design, train, and empirically evaluate a multimodal research architecture that fuses two or more heterogeneous modalities (e.g., medical images + clinical reports, or network telemetry + threat intelligence text), demonstrating superior performance over unimodal baselines.
Build a multimodal research system that:
- Ingests and synchronizes heterogeneous data streams (e.g., CheXpert images + radiology reports, or CIC-IDS network flows + CVE descriptions).
- Implements cross-modal alignment or fusion: utilizing contrastive objectives (CLIP/SigLIP) or intermediate cross-attention Transformer blocks.
- Employs domain-specific backbones (e.g., Vision Transformer + BioMed language model, or 1D CNN + Security text encoder).
- Evaluates against competitive unimodal baselines on downstream classification, generation, or retrieval benchmarks.
- Demonstrates statistically significant performance improvements (p < 0.01 via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests) resulting from multimodal fusion.
- Conducts an ablation study analyzing modality dropout resilience and cross-attention head contributions.
- Visualizes cross-modal attention maps linking specific visual/telemetry features directly to corresponding text tokens.
Deliverables:
- Modular, fully documented multimodal PyTorch repository (multimodal_research_system/) with clean configuration management.
- Trained model checkpoints and automated inference demo script.
- High-resolution multi-panel publication figures displaying cross-modal attention heatmaps and comparative performance bar plots.
- A 4-page academic research paper draft formatted in standard IEEE / ACM conference style (e.g., CVPR, MICCAI, or ACM CCS format).
Outcome: You can design, implement, and scientifically validate cross-disciplinary multimodal AI systems that bridge disparate data modalities for top-tier publication.
