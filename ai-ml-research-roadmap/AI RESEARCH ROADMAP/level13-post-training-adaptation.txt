Level 13 · Multimodal & Cross-Disciplinary AI for Science

Phase: Frontier, Alignment & Thesis
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 12 (AI Alignment, Safety & Ethical Research)
Scope: Master the architectural principles of multimodal foundation models and cross-disciplinary AI for science: joint vision-language contrastive alignment (CLIP, SigLIP), cross-modal projectors (LLaVA), structural biology models (AlphaFold/ESM), physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), and neural operators.

M13.1 · Cross-Modal Contrastive Alignment: CLIP, SigLIP & Dual-Encoder Geometry — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training (CLIP)
- Formulate the dual-encoder contrastive learning objective: parallel image and text encoders projecting disparate modalities into a shared normalized embedding space.
- Derive the symmetric cross-entropy loss over image-to-text and text-to-image cosine similarities with a learned temperature parameter.
- Implement zero-shot classification via text prompt engineering ("a photo of a {class}") and vector similarity dot products.
Session 2 · Sigmoid Loss for Language-Image Pre-Training (SigLIP)
- Analyze the communication bottleneck of standard CLIP: global multi-GPU All-Gather operations required for Softmax denominator normalization across large batch sizes.
- Formulate SigLIP (Zhai et al.): replacing multi-class Softmax with independent pairwise sigmoid losses over all image-text pairs.
- Implement SigLIP loss in PyTorch, proving linear communication scaling and improved zero-shot retrieval accuracy under identical batch budgets.
Mental model: Cross-modal contrastive learning constructs a shared semantic Rosetta Stone where visual concepts and linguistic symbols map to identical coordinates.
Try: Implement the SigLIP pairwise sigmoid loss function from scratch, and verify that it avoids global cross-GPU All-Gather operations.
Outcome: Build, train, and optimize dual-encoder multimodal contrastive models and understand the geometric alignment of cross-modal spaces.

M13.2 · Vision-Language Projection Architectures: LLaVA, Cross-Attention & Perceiver Resamplers — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Visual Tokenization & Projection Bottlenecks
- Process continuous 2D images as discrete token sequences using Vision Transformers (ViT): patch extraction, linear projection, and class token embeddings.
- Implement linear and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) projection adapters (LLaVA architecture) mapping frozen vision tokens directly into the LLM token embedding space.
- Analyze the information density mismatch: high-resolution image patches yield hundreds of tokens, necessitating spatial pooling or token pruning.
Session 2 · Cross-Attention Gating & Perceiver Resamplers
- Implement Perceiver Resamplers (Flamingo architecture) using a fixed set of learnable latent queries to cross-attend into arbitrary-length visual sequences.
- Master gated cross-attention layers inserted between frozen language layers, allowing visual signals to steer language generation without catastrophic forgetting.
- Evaluate multimodal reasoning: visual question answering (VQA), chart comprehension, and document understanding across standard multimodal benchmarks.
Mental model: The projection adapter acts as an optical cable, translating high-dimensional visual feature vectors into tokens the language model's residual stream can parse.
Try: Build a minimal LLaVA-style multimodal model in PyTorch combining a frozen ViT encoder, a 2-layer projection MLP, and a small language model.
Outcome: Architect multimodal vision-language models, implement projection adapters and Perceiver resamplers, and benchmark multimodal reasoning.

M13.3 · AI for Structural Biology & Molecular Modeling: AlphaFold & ESM Principles — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Protein Representation & Evolutionary Scale Modeling (ESM)
- Represent biological sequences (DNA, RNA, proteins) as discrete token sequences over amino acid alphabets.
- Master evolutionary scale modeling (ESM, Rives et al.): training massive masked language models on billions of natural protein sequences to learn evolutionary fitness.
- Extract learned attention contact maps from protein language models, proving that self-attention weights directly reflect 3D residue-residue physical proximity.
Session 2 · 3D Molecular Coordinates & Invariant Point Attention (AlphaFold)
- Understand the structural biology challenge: predicting 3D atomic coordinates (x, y, z) from 1D primary amino acid sequences.
- Master the invariant point attention (IPA) mechanism in AlphaFold2: operating directly on 3D Euclidean frame transformations (SE(3) equivariance).
- Interpret confidence metrics in structural biology: predicted Local Distance Difference Test (pLDDT) and Predicted Aligned Error (PAE) matrices.
Mental model: Biological evolution acts as nature's unsupervised pre-training; language models trained on sequences recover 3D biophysical structure.
Try: Load a pre-trained protein language model (ESM-2), extract the attention contact matrix for a target sequence, and compare against experimental PDB structures.
Outcome: Master the foundations of biological AI models, extract structural contact maps from protein representations, and evaluate 3D structural predictions.

M13.4 · Physics-Informed Machine Learning: PINNs, Operator Learning & Conservation Laws — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)
- Formulate partial differential equations (PDEs) as loss regularizers: Burgers' equation, Navier-Stokes, and heat diffusion.
- Implement Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs, Raissi et al.): using autograd to compute exact spatial and temporal derivatives (du/dt, d^2u/dx^2).
- Minimize the PDE residual loss L_residual = ||f(x, t; u)||^2 alongside empirical initial and boundary condition losses without requiring mesh generation.
Session 2 · Neural Operators & Fourier Neural Operators (FNO)
- Understand the limitation of standard PINNs: learning a single specific PDE solution rather than learning the general solution operator across arbitrary input fields.
- Formulate Neural Operators (Li et al.): learning mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces invariant to mesh discretization.
- Implement Fourier Neural Operators (FNO): computing convolutions in Fourier space via Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT) for hyper-fast fluid simulation.
Mental model: Standard ML fits curves to scattered data points; Physics-Informed ML constrains the curve to strictly satisfy the fundamental differential laws of the universe.
Try: Implement a Physics-Informed Neural Network in PyTorch solving the 1D wave equation, computing exact second-order derivatives via autograd.
Outcome: Implement Physics-Informed Neural Networks, enforce physical conservation laws in loss functions, and master Fourier Neural Operators.

M13.5 · Cross-Domain Transfer Learning: Adapting Foundation Models to Scientific Disciplines — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Domain-Specific Continued Pre-Training
- Adapt general foundation models to specialized scientific domains (medicine, climate modeling, chemistry, genomics) via continued domain pre-training.
- Prevent catastrophic forgetting of general reasoning: blend domain-specific scientific corpora with 10-20% general instruction and math replay data.
- Manage vocabulary adaptation: extending tokenizers with scientific nomenclature (SMILES chemical strings, genomic k-mers) without corrupting base embeddings.
Session 2 · Multi-Task Scientific Benchmarking & Validation
- Design domain-specific validation suites evaluating both statistical performance and physical plausibility (e.g., mass conservation, charge neutrality).
- Conduct blinded expert evaluations with domain specialists (chemists, biologists, clinicians) to evaluate real-world utility beyond academic benchmarks.
- Document scientific reproducibility artifacts: raw simulation data, random seeds, environmental parameters, and verified software container recipes.
Mental model: Adapting a foundation model to science is not just teaching it vocabulary; it is teaching it to respect the non-negotiable invariants of physical reality.
Try: Fine-tune a foundation model on a domain-specific scientific corpus with replay data, and verify that general reasoning benchmarks do not degrade.
Outcome: Adapt foundation models to complex scientific domains, prevent catastrophic forgetting, and enforce domain-specific physical invariants.

Session 2 · Physical Verification, Benchmarking & Publication Drafting

- Conduct an ablation study comparing specialized scientific pre-training against naive general-purpose baselines.

Mental model: The frontier of AI research is cross-disciplinary; the greatest impact emerges where deep learning principles interface with physical and biological science.

Outcome: Design, train, and validate cross-modal foundation architectures for scientific discovery that integrate rigorous domain physics and biology.
