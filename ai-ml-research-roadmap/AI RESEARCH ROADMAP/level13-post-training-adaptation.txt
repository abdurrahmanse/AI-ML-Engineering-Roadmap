Level 13 · Post-Training, Parameter-Efficient Tuning & Continual Learning

Scope: investigate scientific techniques for adapting and steering foundation models: spectral theory of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA/DoRA), representation collapse, catastrophic forgetting, continual learning dynamics, and model merging.

Prerequisites: Level 12

M13.1 · Theory of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) — (sessions: 3)

Understand the computational and storage impracticality of full parameter fine-tuning for massive foundation models across specialized downstream domains.
Analyze the Intrinsic Dimensionality of Deep Models (Aghajanyan et al.): proving that the parameter updates required for downstream tasks occupy a very low-dimensional intrinsic subspace.
Master Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA, Hu et al.): reparameterizing weight updates as Delta W = B * A * (alpha / r), where B \in R^{d x r}, A \in R^{r x k}, and rank r << min(d, k).
Analyze LoRA Initialization: initializing A with Gaussian random noise and B with zeros so that Delta W = 0 at the start of training, preserving exact base model behavior.
Master Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation (DoRA, Liu et al.): decomposing weights into magnitude and directional components W = m * (V + Delta V) / ||V + Delta V||_F to bridge the performance gap with full fine-tuning.
Explore Prefix Tuning and Prompt Tuning: prepending learnable continuous virtual tokens to key and value matrices in attention layers.
Outcome: derive LoRA mathematically, prove that rank r updates preserve parameter gradients in a low-dimensional manifold, and implement custom LoRA layers from scratch.

M13.2 · Spectral Analysis of LoRA & Subspace Geometry — (sessions: 3)

Analyze the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of fine-tuned weight differences: Delta W = U \Sigma V^T and analyze the rapid decay of singular values.
Study Subspace Similarity: computing Grassmannian distance and principal angles between the subspaces spanned by LoRA matrices and full parameter update matrices.
Investigate Rank Over-Allocation: what happens when rank r is chosen larger than the true intrinsic rank of the downstream adaptation task?
Analyze which Transformer modules benefit most from low-rank adaptation: query/key/value attention projections versus MLP feedforward expansions.
Study QLoRA (Dettmers et al.): NormalFloat4 (NF4) data type, Double Quantization (DQ), and Paged Optimizers to enable fine-tuning 70B models on single consumer GPUs.
Outcome: compute the singular value spectrum of LoRA matrices across training checkpoints to empirically observe rank saturation and subspace alignment.

M13.3 · Catastrophic Forgetting & Continual Learning Dynamics — (sessions: 4)

Define Catastrophic Forgetting (McCloskey & Cohen): the severe degradation of previously learned capabilities when a neural network is fine-tuned sequentially on new domains.
Analyze the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma: balancing the acquisition of new knowledge (plasticity) against the retention of prior knowledge (stability).
Study Continual Learning Methodologies:
Session 1 · Regularization-Based Approaches
Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC, Kirkpatrick et al.): penalizing changes to parameters critical to past tasks using the diagonal of the empirical Fisher Information Matrix.
Synaptic Intelligence and memory-aware synapses: tracking path integrals of parameter importance online during training.
Session 2 · Replay and Memory-Based Approaches
Experience Replay: storing a small buffer of exemplar data from earlier tasks and mixing them into current training batches.
Generative Replay: using a generative model to produce synthetic samples of earlier tasks to interleave during adaptation.
Session 3 · Parameter Isolation and Dynamic Expansion
Progressive Neural Networks and modular parameter allocation: freezing old task sub-networks and adding new modules for novel tasks.
Outcome: derive Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) mathematically using Laplace approximation of the posterior distribution and implement EWC loss in PyTorch.

M13.4 · Model Merging, Weight Averaging & Task Arithmetic — (sessions: 3)

Understand Model Merging: combining multiple independently fine-tuned models into a single multi-task model without access to original training data or additional gradient updates.
Study Linear Weight Averaging: Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA, Izmailov et al.) and Model Soups (Wortsman et al.) averaging checkpoints in flat loss basins.
Master Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al.): treating fine-tuned models as task vectors tau_t = W_fine-tuned - W_base, enabling task addition (multi-task combination) and task subtraction (unlearning concepts).
Analyze Interference and Parameter Clash: when task vectors have conflicting gradient directions in overlapping parameter dimensions.
Study Advanced Merging Algorithms:
TIES-Merging (Yadav et al.): Trimming redundant small values, Resolving sign conflicts, and Merging aligned parameters.
DARE (Drop And REscale, Yu et al.): extreme random sparsification of task vectors followed by rescaling to prevent representation collapse.
Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP): interpolating weights along the geodesic of high-dimensional spheres rather than linear Euclidean interpolation.
Outcome: implement Task Vector addition and TIES-Merging in Python, evaluating multi-task accuracy retention across independently fine-tuned checkpoints.

M13.5 · Representation Drift & Mechanistic Effects of Post-Training — (sessions: 3)

Investigate Representation Drift: analyzing how internal latent representations shift during fine-tuning, instruction tuning, and alignment.
Study the "Surface Form Hypothesis" (Zhou et al., LIMA): hypothesis that pre-training learns virtually all factual knowledge and reasoning, while post-training primarily learns style, formatting, and interactive behavior.
Measure Representation Drift using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) and Procrustes distance across pre-trained and fine-tuned layer activations.
Analyze Weight Disentanglement: identifying whether post-training modifies core attention mechanisms or isolated late-layer projections.
Examine Unlearning and Concept Erasure in Fine-Tuned Models: representation editing to remove copyrighted text, hazardous chemical knowledge, or personally identifiable information (PII).
Outcome: measure the layer-wise CKA similarity between base and instruction-tuned model checkpoints to empirically test the Surface Form Hypothesis.

M13.6 · Capstone: Empirical Study of Representation Drift and Subspace Conservation in LoRA vs Full Fine-Tuning — (sessions: 2)

Design an empirical research experiment comparing parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA) against full parameter fine-tuning on a specialized scientific or reasoning task.
Fine-tune identical base foundation models using: (1) Full parameter fine-tuning, (2) LoRA with low rank (r=4), (3) LoRA with high rank (r=64), and (4) DoRA.
Track training dynamics, validation loss, and task accuracy over identical training epochs.
Evaluate Catastrophic Forgetting: measure accuracy drops on standard general-domain language benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, ARC) before and after domain adaptation.
Compute Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of weight update matrices Delta W for full fine-tuning and compare principal subspace overlap against the learned LoRA matrices using Grassmannian distance metrics.
Perform Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) across all Transformer layers to map exact representation drift across the network depth.
Conduct Task Vector arithmetic: test whether subtracting the fine-tuned task vector cleanly reverses domain adaptation without damaging pre-trained general knowledge.
Draft an academic research paper presenting singular value spectra, CKA drift heatmaps, forgetting benchmarks, and theoretical analysis of low-rank subspace conservation.
Final result: a reproducible PEFT research benchmark codebase, evaluation notebook, and academic paper evaluating representation drift and forgetting dynamics.

Level 13 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Explain the Intrinsic Dimensionality hypothesis of deep neural network fine-tuning.
Derive the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) reparameterization Delta W = B * A.
Explain the initialization scheme of LoRA and why it guarantees identity mapping at step zero.
Formulate DoRA (Weight-Decomposed LoRA) and explain how magnitude-direction decoupling improves expressivity.
Analyze the singular value spectrum of weight updates using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
Explain the mechanics of QLoRA: NormalFloat4 (NF4) quantization, Double Quantization, and Paged Optimizers.
Define catastrophic forgetting and state the stability-plasticity dilemma.
Derive Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) using the Fisher Information Matrix and Laplace approximation.
Explain the mechanics of Task Vector arithmetic: task addition, task subtraction, and unlearning.
Implement TIES-Merging and DARE to resolve parameter sign conflicts when merging multi-task models.
Explain the Surface Form Hypothesis and its implications for instruction fine-tuning datasets.
Measure representation drift across fine-tuning checkpoints using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA).
Evaluate the trade-off between domain specialization and general-purpose knowledge retention.
