Level 12 · AI Alignment, Robustness & Adversarial Defense

Phase: Frontier Robustness & Thesis Projects
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 11 (Explainability, Interpretability & Trustworthy AI)
Scope: Conduct rigorous research into adversarial robustness and alignment: generating white-box and black-box adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD, Carlini-Wagner) on medical image classification and cybersecurity intrusion detection systems (evasion attacks on malware classifiers); adversarial training and certified defense bounds; preference alignment methodologies (RLHF, DPO, KTO); auditing demographic bias and algorithmic fairness across diverse patient populations.

M12.1 · Adversarial Attacks: White-Box, Black-Box & Evasion Vectors — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · First-Order Adversarial Perturbations (FGSM & PGD)
- Formulate the adversarial perturbation optimization problem: maximizing loss subject to an L_p norm constraint (L_infinity, L_2) on perturbation delta.
- Derive the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) (Goodfellow et al.) as a one-step linearization of the adversarial loss landscape.
- Implement Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) (Madry et al.) as the standard multi-step first-order white-box adversary with random restarts.
Session 2 · Targeted Attacks, Carlini-Wagner & Black-Box Evasion
- Formulate the Carlini-Wagner (C&W) optimization attack: finding minimal L_2 distortion while bypassing gradient masking and defensive distillation.
- Execute black-box adversarial attacks: leveraging transferability across substitute surrogate models and gradient-free zeroth-order optimization (NES).
- Demonstrate extreme vulnerability in medical imaging: adding imperceptible high-frequency pixel noise to chest X-rays that flips diagnostic outputs with 99% confidence.
Mental model: Deep neural networks have linear behavior in high-dimensional spaces; tiny aligned perturbations across thousands of dimensions accumulate into massive logit swings.
Try: Implement a PGD L_infinity attack in PyTorch against a pre-trained radiological classifier, plot the perturbation noise image, and record the accuracy drop.
Outcome: Implement first-order and optimization-based adversarial attacks to evaluate the adversarial vulnerability of deep learning models.

M12.2 · Adversarial Training, Robust Optimization & Certified Bounds — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Min-Max Robust Optimization & Adversarial Training
- Formalize adversarial training as a robust min-max game (Madry et al.): minimizing expected risk under worst-case inner-maximization adversarial perturbations.
- Implement PGD adversarial training: dynamically generating adversarial examples during every training mini-batch and optimizing model weights on corrupted inputs.
- Analyze the fundamental trade-off between standard accuracy and adversarial robustness (Zhang et al. TRADES formulation).
Session 2 · Certified Defenses & Randomized Smoothing
- Understand why empirical defenses frequently succumb to stronger adaptive attacks (gradient obfuscation, shattered gradients).
- Master certified robustness: providing mathematical guarantees that no perturbation within an L_2 radius R can flip the model prediction.
- Implement Randomized Smoothing (Cohen et al.): certifying robust classifications by convolving base classifiers with isotropic Gaussian noise.
Mental model: Empirical defenses patch specific attack tools; certified robustness provides a mathematical guarantee of safety within a defined perturbation sphere.
Try: Train a model using PGD adversarial training in PyTorch, evaluate robust accuracy against increasing perturbation epsilon, and compute certified radii via randomized smoothing.
Outcome: Formulate robust optimization objectives, implement adversarial training, and compute mathematically certified robustness bounds.

M12.3 · Cybersecurity Evasion & Malware Feature Manipulation — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Feature-Space vs. Problem-Space Attacks in Security
- Differentiate between feature-space attacks (perturbing feature vectors directly) and problem-space attacks (modifying actual executable binary files).
- Account for inverse mapping and domain preservation constraints: ensuring modified malicious binaries execute correctly and preserve exploit functionality.
- Implement structural evasion: injecting benign byte sequences into Portable Executable (PE) slack spaces and appending benign overlay sections.
Session 2 · Evading Machine Learning Intrusion & Malware Detectors
- Evade gradient boosted decision trees (Ember benchmark) and neural network detectors by crafting functional malware variants using genetic algorithms and RL agents.
- Analyze evasion of network intrusion detection systems (CIC-IDS): packet splitting, timing jitter, and dummy packet insertion that disrupt flow statistics.
- Implement robust malware feature extractors that resist structural manipulation and strip non-semantic byte artifacts prior to classification.
Mental model: In cybersecurity research, adversaries modify executable files, not abstract numbers; an attack is only valid if the malware payload still detonates.
Try: Modify a Windows PE executable using Python lief to alter non-functional byte headers, verify that the binary still executes, and test classifier evasion.
Outcome: Execute problem-space evasion attacks on cybersecurity malware classifiers and design robust detection systems resilient to binary manipulation.

M12.4 · Preference Alignment: RLHF, DPO & Kahneman-Tversky Optimization — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)
- Master the canonical three-stage RLHF pipeline: supervised fine-tuning (SFT), reward modeling on pairwise preferences, and PPO policy optimization.
- Derive the Bradley-Terry preference model: estimating the latent reward function r(x, y) from pairwise human comparison datasets.
- Prevent policy collapse and reward hacking: enforcing a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty between the fine-tuned policy and the reference model.
Session 2 · Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) & Direct Alignment
- Deconstruct Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al.): analytically reparameterizing the reward function in terms of the policy itself.
- Derive the closed-form DPO objective, eliminating the need to train a separate reward model or run unstable reinforcement learning loops during training.
- Explore Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO): aligning models using binary feedback (thumbs up / thumbs down) based on prospect theory loss functions.
Mental model: DPO proves that the optimal policy implicitly defines its own reward function, allowing direct preference alignment via standard cross-entropy loss.
Try: Implement the DPO loss function in PyTorch from scratch, train a small language model on a pairwise preference dataset, and track implicit reward margins.
Outcome: Formulate and implement preference alignment objectives (RLHF, DPO, KTO) to align foundation models with human values and domain criteria.

M12.5 · Algorithmic Fairness, Demographic Disparity & Bias Auditing — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Mathematical Definitions of Fairness
- Formalize disparate impact and demographic fairness criteria: Demographic Parity (P(Y_hat = 1 | A = 0) = P(Y_hat = 1 | A = 1)) across protected attributes A.
- Formulate Equalized Odds and Equality of Opportunity (Hardt et al.): conditioning false positive and true positive rates to be equal across demographic subgroups.
- Understand the impossibility theorem of algorithmic fairness (Kleinberg et al.): proving that calibration and equalized odds cannot hold simultaneously unless base rates are identical.
Session 2 · Clinical Fairness Audits & Bias Mitigation
- Audit clinical models (MIMIC-IV / CheXpert) for performance disparities across demographic subgroups (e.g., race, biological sex, age, socioeconomic insurance status).
- Identify underdiagnosis bias: models exhibiting significantly higher false negative rates in marginalized patient populations despite high aggregate accuracy.
- Apply algorithmic mitigation techniques: fair representation learning (adversarial debiasing), threshold optimization, and re-weighting strategies.
Mental model: Aggregate accuracy hides systemic failures; a medical AI that achieves 95% overall accuracy but fails consistently on minority subgroups is unsafe.
Try: Audit a clinical diagnostic model across demographic subgroups, calculate Equalized Odds ratios, and mitigate disparities using fair threshold optimization.
Outcome: Audit machine learning models for demographic bias, quantify fairness trade-offs, and implement algorithmic mitigation strategies.

M12.6 · Capstone Project: Adversarial Robustness & Fairness Stress-Testing Benchmark — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Stress-Testing Benchmark Suite Architecture
- Build a publication-ready robustness and fairness auditing framework targeting either a clinical healthcare or cybersecurity machine learning model.
- Implement a comprehensive adversarial battery: multi-step white-box attacks (PGD, C&W), black-box surrogate transfers, and domain evasion vectors.
- Integrate demographic fairness auditing tools evaluating Equalized Odds, Demographic Parity, and subgroup error disparities.
Session 2 · Empirical Defense, Mitigation & Scientific Reporting
- Implement an empirical defense: adversarial training (PGD/TRADES) or fair representation debiasing to mitigate observed vulnerabilities.
- Quantify performance trade-offs: measuring robust accuracy vs. clean accuracy, and demographic parity vs. overall predictive utility.
- Produce publication-ready comparative tables and multi-panel figures formatted for submission to top-tier security or ML venues (USENIX Security, NeurIPS).
Mental model: Publishing top-tier safety research requires proving both that an attack is empirically devastating and that your proposed defense provides genuine mitigation.
Try: Run the complete adversarial and fairness stress-testing suite, generating an automated safety audit report and mitigation trade-off curves.
Outcome: Deliver an exhaustive, peer-reviewed empirical audit evaluating adversarial robustness, evasion vulnerability, and algorithmic fairness.
