Level 12 · Multimodal & Cross-Modal Representation Research

Scope: investigate representation learning across perceptual modalities: contrastive vision-language alignment, visual tokenization, autoregressive multimodal foundation architectures, cross-attention fusion, speech representations, and video latent dynamics.

Prerequisites: Level 11

M12.1 · Contrastive Vision-Language Alignment: CLIP & SigLIP — (sessions: 3)

Understand multimodal alignment: mapping heterogeneous data modalities (images and text) into a shared semantic latent space.
Master CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training, Radford et al.): dual-encoder architecture (Vision Transformer image encoder + Transformer text encoder).
Analyze the symmetric InfoNCE loss function: maximizing cosine similarity of matched image-text pairs along the diagonal while penalizing off-diagonal pairs.
Study Zero-Shot Classification via CLIP: constructing prompt templates ("a photo of a {label}"), encoding candidate text labels, and computing cosine classification probabilities.
Master SigLIP (Sigmoid Loss for Language Image Pre-Training, Zhai et al.): replacing the global Softmax normalization with pairwise sigmoid loss.
Analyze the benefits of SigLIP: eliminating the global all-gather communication bottleneck across distributed GPUs, enabling larger batch sizes and stable scaling.
Examine Modality Gap (Liang et al.): the geometric phenomenon where image embeddings and text embeddings occupy completely disjoint cones in latent space.
Outcome: derive SigLIP loss mathematically and train a dual-encoder vision-language model with contrastive alignment on image-text pairs.

M12.2 · Visual Tokenization & Discrete Representation Learning — (sessions: 4)

Understand the difference between continuous visual representations (patches) and discrete visual tokens.
Master the Vision Transformer (ViT, Dosovitskiy et al.): image patch extraction (e.g. 16x16 pixels), linear patch projection, learnable class token [CLS], and 2D positional encodings.
Study Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAE, Van den Oord et al.): continuous encoder, discrete codebook discretization via nearest-neighbor vector quantization, and straight-through estimator (STE).
Master VQ-GAN (Esser et al.): augmenting VQ-VAE with adversarial perceptual loss and patch-based discriminator to generate crisp high-frequency textures without blurriness.
Explore Continuous Patch Tokenization vs Discrete Tokens: analyzing why modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) favor continuous patch representations over discrete codebooks.
Analyze High-Resolution Image Processing: dynamic tiling, patch cropping, and spatial aspect-ratio preserving grids in modern Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).
Outcome: implement a discrete vector quantization layer with Straight-Through Estimator (STE) gradient copying in PyTorch.

M12.3 · Vision-Language Foundation Models & Multimodal Adapters — (sessions: 4)

Analyze the architectural taxonomy of modern Vision-Language Models:
Session 1 · Projection Adapters & Architecture Design
Dual-encoder alignment (CLIP style) versus unified autoregressive multimodal generation (LLaVA, Flamingo, PaLM-E).
Linear Projections and Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLP adapters, LLaVA, Liu et al.): projecting visual patch tokens directly into the LLM text token embedding space.
Cross-Attention Architectures (Flamingo, Alayrac et al.): gated cross-attention layers interleaved within frozen language model Transformer blocks.
Resamplers and Bottlenecks (Q-Former in BLIP-2, Perceiver Resampler): using fixed-size query tokens to compress variable-length visual patches into a fixed number of tokens.
Session 2 · Training Curricula for LMMs
Stage 1: Pre-training for feature alignment on massive image-caption pairs (training only the projection adapter while keeping vision encoder and LLM frozen).
Stage 2: Visual Instruction Tuning (fine-tuning the projection adapter and LLM on detailed multi-turn visual conversations, visual reasoning, and QA).
Session 3 · Multimodal Hallucinations & Spatial Grounding
Analyze Object Hallucination in LMMs: models claiming non-existent objects exist in images due to language prior bias overpowering visual features (POPE benchmark).
Study Spatial Grounding and Coordinate Modeling: bounding box prediction ([x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] normalized coordinates), visual pointing, and region-of-interest tokens.
Outcome: implement an MLP projection adapter connecting a pre-trained Vision Transformer to an open-weight language model and perform end-to-end forward generation.

M12.4 · Audio, Speech & Continuous Representation Research — (sessions: 3)

Understand acoustic representations: audio waveforms, Fourier transforms, Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), and Mel-frequency spectrograms.
Study Self-Supervised Speech Representation: wav2vec 2.0 (Baevski et al.), masked acoustic modeling, and contrastive loss over quantized latent representations.
Master Whisper (Radford et al.): weakly supervised encoder-decoder Transformer trained on 680,000 hours of multilingual audio for robust speech-to-text transcription.
Analyze Continuous Acoustic Tokenization: SoundStream and EnCodec (Défossez et al.), Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ), and neural audio codecs.
Explore Speech-Language Foundation Models: unified tokenization of speech and text, zero-shot voice cloning, and real-time duplex speech generation.
Outcome: implement a Mel-spectrogram feature extractor and inspect phonetic representations extracted from intermediate layers of a pre-trained speech model.

M12.5 · Video Representation Learning & Spatiotemporal Dynamics — (sessions: 3)

Understand the spatiotemporal challenges of video modeling: adding the temporal dimension T expands data dimensionality and computational complexity quadratically.
Study Spatiotemporal Attention: factorized space-time attention (TimeSformer, Bertasius et al., Video Swin Transformer) separating spatial attention within frames from temporal attention across frames.
Analyze 3D VAEs and Video Diffusion: inflating 2D spatial convolutions and attention layers to 3D spatiotemporal blocks for video generation (Sora, VideoLDM).
Study Causal Video Tokenization: maintaining temporal causality so future frames cannot leak information into past frame token representations.
Analyze Long-Form Video Understanding: memory caching, temporal pooling, and sparse sampling techniques to reason over hour-long video contexts.
Outcome: implement factorized space-time self-attention in PyTorch and verify computational memory savings compared to full joint space-time attention.

M12.6 · Capstone: Train a Vision-Language Adapter (LLaVA-Style) with Visual Grounding and Hallucination Probing — (sessions: 2)

Build an end-to-end Large Multimodal Model (LMM) pipeline by training a vision-language projection adapter connecting a frozen Vision Transformer (CLIP-ViT) to a frozen decoder language model.
Implement a two-layer MLP projection adapter with GELU non-linearity to align visual patch tokens with the language model embedding dimension.
Curate an image-instruction dataset (e.g. LLaVA-Instruct subset or ChartQA/VQA subset) with conversational visual question answering examples.
Execute Stage 1 Feature Alignment: train the projection adapter on image-caption pairs using autoregressive language modeling loss with frozen vision and LLM weights.
Execute Stage 2 Visual Instruction Tuning: fine-tune the adapter and top Transformer layers of the LLM on multimodal instruction data.
Conduct an empirical study on Multimodal Object Hallucination using the POPE (Polling-based Object Probing Evaluation) benchmark: measure precision, recall, and F1 across random, popular, and adversarial negative object questions.
Implement attention rollout or cross-modal attention attribution to visualize which visual image patches the language model attends to when generating factual responses.
Draft an academic research paper documenting the architecture, multimodal alignment loss dynamics, hallucination benchmark results, and attention heatmaps.
Final result: a working vision-language foundation model checkpoint, training codebase, and research paper analyzing multimodal alignment and hallucination dynamics.

Level 12 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Formulate the contrastive vision-language alignment objective of CLIP.
Derive SigLIP loss and explain how pairwise sigmoid loss eliminates all-gather communication bottlenecks.
Explain the Modality Gap phenomenon in joint multimodal representation spaces.
Implement the Vision Transformer (ViT) patch extraction and linear projection mechanics.
Explain the Vector-Quantized VAE (VQ-VAE) codebook mechanism and the Straight-Through Estimator.
Contrast linear projection adapters (LLaVA style) with cross-attention architectures (Flamingo style).
Design a two-stage training curriculum for Large Multimodal Models (alignment followed by instruction tuning).
Explain the root causes of visual object hallucination in vision-language models.
Formulate spatial grounding using normalized coordinate tokens in language models.
Transform raw audio waveforms into Mel-spectrograms and explain the wav2vec 2.0 self-supervised objective.
Explain Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) used in modern neural audio codecs.
Implement factorized spatiotemporal attention for video understanding.
Evaluate vision-language models on visual reasoning and object hallucination benchmarks (POPE, VQA).
Progression: Contrastive Multimodal Alignment (CLIP/SigLIP) → Visual Tokenization & ViT → Large Multimodal Models (LLaVA/Flamingo) → Speech & Audio Representations → Video Spatiotemporal Dynamics → Vision-Language Adapter Capstone.
