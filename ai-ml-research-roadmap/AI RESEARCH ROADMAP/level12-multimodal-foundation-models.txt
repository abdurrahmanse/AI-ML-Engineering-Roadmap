Level 12 · AI Alignment, Safety & Ethical Research

Phase: Frontier, Alignment & Thesis
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 11 (Mechanistic Interpretability & Model Analysis)
Scope: Master the mathematical derivations, empirical algorithms, and ethical methodologies of AI alignment: preference modeling (Bradley-Terry, RLHF, DPO, KTO), reward gaming and Goodhart's law, adversarial robustness (PGD, GCG attacks), algorithmic fairness audits, and AI safety governance frameworks.

M12.1 · Preference Modeling Derivations: Bradley-Terry, RLHF & Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Reward Modeling & The Bradley-Terry Formulation
- Formalize human preference datasets D = {(x, y_w, y_l)} where y_w is the preferred completion and y_l is the dispreferred completion.
- Formulate the Bradley-Terry preference model: P(y_w \succ y_l | x) = \sigma(r(x, y_w) - r(x, y_l)).
- Train deep Reward Models (RMs) by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of pairwise preferences with margin regularizers.
Session 2 · Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Closed-Form Derivation
- Analyze the classical RLHF pipeline (PPO with frozen reference policy KL penalties) and identify its complexity and training instability.
- Derive Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, Rafailov et al.) analytically: express the optimal reward implicitly as r(x, y) = \beta \log (\pi_\theta(y|x) / \pi_{ref}(y|x)).
- Implement the DPO loss objective without training an explicit reward model or sampling actions via reinforcement learning.
Mental model: DPO proves that an optimal policy implicitly defines its own reward function, turning unstable RLHF into stable binary cross-entropy optimization.
Try: Derive the DPO loss gradient analytically with respect to model parameters, and implement the DPO loss function in PyTorch with numerical stability clamps.
Outcome: Derive and implement state-of-the-art preference optimization algorithms (Reward Modeling, RLHF, DPO, KTO) from first principles.

M12.2 · Reward Over-Optimization, Sycophancy & Goodhart’s Law in Language Models — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Reward Over-Optimization & Goodhart's Law
- Understand Goodhart’s Law in AI alignment: when a proxy reward model becomes the optimization target, it ceases to be a reliable measure of true quality.
- Quantify reward over-optimization: measure the divergence between proxy reward and true human preference as KL divergence from the base model increases.
- Analyze the "Alignment Tax": the degradation of mathematical reasoning, coding capability, or linguistic diversity resulting from aggressive preference tuning.
Session 2 · Sycophancy & Hallucination Exploitation
- Define sycophancy in aligned models: the tendency of language models to echo and validate false user assertions to maximize superficial agreeableness.
- Construct synthetic contrastive sycophancy datasets evaluating model adherence to factual truth when contradicted by authoritative user prompts.
- Mitigate sycophantic behavior using targeted preference tuning and negative activation steering in the residual stream.
Mental model: Optimizing aggressively against a proxy reward produces a model that flatters the rater rather than speaking the objective truth.
Try: Evaluate an aligned model on a sycophancy probe dataset, measure the percentage of false user statements validated by the model, and implement a corrective DPO fine-tune.
Outcome: Diagnose and quantify reward over-optimization, measure the alignment tax, and eliminate sycophancy in preference-tuned models.

M12.3 · Adversarial Robustness: Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), GCG Attacks & Red-Teaming — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Adversarial Attacks on Continuous Representations
- Formulate adversarial perturbations as constrained optimization: \max_{||\delta|| \le \epsilon} L(\theta, x + \delta, y).
- Implement the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) and multi-step Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) with projection back onto the L-infinity epsilon ball.
- Understand adversarial training (Madry et al.): formulating the min-max robust optimization game to learn robust representations.
Session 2 · Discrete Token Jailbreaks & Red-Teaming Protocols
- Master discrete adversarial optimization against aligned language models: Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG, Zou et al.) attack suffixes.
- Analyze universal adversarial triggers: transferability of adversarial suffix tokens across disparate open-source and proprietary foundation models.
- Structure systematic red-teaming methodologies: automated red-teaming with adversarial LLMs, threat modeling, and defense mechanisms (perplexity filtering).
Mental model: Adversarial attacks reveal the non-robust, brittle shortcuts learned by deep networks; robustness requires closing off unconstrained gradient directions.
Try: Implement a PGD attack on a neural classifier in PyTorch, measure accuracy degradation, and verify robustness recovery through adversarial training.
Outcome: Formulate adversarial attacks, implement PGD and discrete token jailbreaks, and design defensive adversarial training protocols.

M12.4 · Algorithmic Fairness: Demographic Parity, Equalized Odds & Auditing Disparities — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Mathematical Definitions of Algorithmic Fairness
- Formalize core fairness criteria: Demographic Parity (P(\hat{Y}=1 | A=0) = P(\hat{Y}=1 | A=1)), Equal Opportunity, and Equalized Odds.
- Prove the Impossibility Theorem of Fairness (Kleinberg et al.): Demographic Parity, Equalized Odds, and Predictive Parity cannot be satisfied simultaneously.
- Measure bias in natural language models: gender/racial bias in word embeddings (WEAT), stereotypical association tests, and disparate impact ratios.
Session 2 · Fairness Interventions & Algorithmic Auditing
- Categorize bias mitigation interventions: pre-processing (re-weighing, data re-sampling), in-processing (adversarial debiasing), and post-processing (threshold tuning).
- Conduct systematic algorithmic fairness audits on production models, quantifying performance disparities across sensitive intersectional demographic groups.
- Document algorithmic impact assessments and author comprehensive Model Cards (Mitchell et al.) detailing intended scope and fairness benchmarks.
Mental model: Fairness metrics mathematically conflict; an ethical researcher must explicitly choose and defend which fairness trade-off fits the domain.
Try: Audit a predictive model for disparate impact across demographic subgroups, calculate Equalized Odds ratios, and implement an in-processing debiasing constraint.
Outcome: Apply mathematical definitions of algorithmic fairness, audit deep learning models for demographic disparities, and implement debiasing interventions.

M12.5 · AI Governance & Research Ethics: Institutional Review Boards, Dual-Use & Safety Charters — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Research Ethics & Institutional Review
- Navigate academic research ethics and Institutional Review Board (IRB) protocols for human-subject data collection and crowdsourced annotation.
- Evaluate dual-use hazards in AI research: autonomous cyber-offensive tooling, chemical/biological weapon synthesis risk, and automated disinformation pipelines.
- Apply the Menlo Report and Belmont Report principles (beneficence, justice, respect for persons) to AI/ML research methodologies.
Session 2 · Responsible Disclosure & Safety Governance
- Establish responsible vulnerability disclosure protocols for newly discovered model jailbreaks, security bypasses, and data extraction vectors.
- Understand international AI regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act risk classifications, US Executive Orders, Frontier Model Safety Charters).
- Implement tiered model release strategies: gated access, safety evaluation milestones, and model weight watermarking.
Mental model: Cutting-edge scientific power brings severe societal responsibility; ethical researchers evaluate dual-use consequences before training begins.
Try: Author a dual-use risk assessment and mitigation framework for a hypothetical frontier foundation model capability release.
Outcome: Navigate scientific research ethics, conduct dual-use risk assessments, and establish responsible disclosure and governance policies.

M12.6 · Capstone Project: Preference Alignment & Adversarial Jailbreak Vulnerability Audit — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Preference Dataset Construction & DPO Alignment
- Construct a curated pairwise preference dataset containing nuanced safety, helpfulness, and factual truthfulness examples.
- Implement and execute a complete Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) fine-tuning pipeline on an open-source instruction model.
- Monitor implicit reward margins, KL divergence from the reference policy, and verify the absence of reward over-optimization.
Session 2 · Automated Red-Teaming & Adversarial Robustness Audit
- Deploy an automated red-teaming harness executing Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) attacks and semantic jailbreak prompts against the aligned model.
- Measure Attack Success Rate (ASR) across both standard prompting and adversarial suffixes, quantifying robustness gains from alignment.
- Author an academic safety paper in LaTeX detailing preference tuning dynamics, the alignment tax, and red-teaming vulnerability audits.
Mental model: True alignment is not superficial compliance; it is robust stability that holds even when an adversary attacks the model with optimized gradients.
Try: Align a language model using DPO, subject it to an automated GCG jailbreak attack suite, and demonstrate a 70% reduction in attack success rate.
Outcome: Design, implement, and audit state-of-the-art preference alignment and adversarial defenses with publication-grade scientific rigor.
