Level 11 · Interpretability, Robustness and Fairness

Phase: Scaled Systems, Trustworthy AI & Alignment
Suggested duration: 26 sessions (~6 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 10
Scope: Study model behavior, failure, distribution shift, security, and social impact with methods that support actionable claims.

M11.1 · Model Behavior and Interpretability — (sessions: 5)
- Compare feature attribution, counterfactuals, probing, concept methods, and mechanistic analyses.
- Test explanation faithfulness, stability, usefulness, and sensitivity to baselines.
- Treat an explanation as a hypothesis about computation that requires independent validation.
- Treat explanations, robustness results, and fairness metrics as evidence with validity limits rather than definitive proof of trust.
- Test subgroup behavior, distribution shift, attack assumptions, and mitigation trade-offs with reproducible evaluation scripts.
Mental model: An explanation is evidence about a model, not a guarantee that the model used a human-readable reason.
Try: Evaluate two explanation methods on known synthetic mechanisms before applying them to a real model.
Outcome: You can make cautious, testable interpretability claims.

M11.2 · Robustness and Security — (sessions: 5)
- Test corruption, perturbation, distribution shift, prompt injection, data poisoning, and model extraction risks.
- Define threat models, attacker capabilities, budgets, and realistic success criteria.
- Compare robust training, input validation, uncertainty, monitoring, and abstention mitigations.
- Treat explanations, robustness results, and fairness metrics as evidence with validity limits rather than definitive proof of trust.
- Test subgroup behavior, distribution shift, attack assumptions, and mitigation trade-offs with reproducible evaluation scripts.
Mental model: Security claims are conditional statements about a specified attacker and environment.
Try: Write a threat model and red-team protocol before testing a model; never test against systems you do not own.
Outcome: You can evaluate and mitigate realistic reliability and security failures.

M11.3 · Fairness, Privacy and Human Impact — (sessions: 4)
- Measure subgroup performance, calibration, allocation effects, privacy risk, and representational harms.
- Explain why fairness metrics can conflict and why context and stakeholder participation matter.
- Document intended use, excluded use, affected groups, recourse, and residual risk.
- Treat explanations, robustness results, and fairness metrics as evidence with validity limits rather than definitive proof of trust.
- Test subgroup behavior, distribution shift, attack assumptions, and mitigation trade-offs with reproducible evaluation scripts.
Mental model: Responsible AI is a design and governance process, not a single metric added after training.
Try: Produce an impact assessment with stakeholder questions and a mitigation trade-off table.
Outcome: You can connect technical evaluation to human consequences and governance decisions.

M11.4 · Explanation Validity and Causal Probes — (sessions: 3)
- Distinguish feature attribution, probing, explanation, and causal intervention claims.
- Test explanation stability, faithfulness, localization, and sensitivity to baselines or perturbations.
Mental model: An explanation method is another model whose validity must be evaluated.
Try: Compare two explanation methods on the same controlled intervention and report disagreement.
Outcome: You can avoid treating plausible explanations as causal proof.

M11.5 · Robustness, Fairness and Privacy Trade-offs — (sessions: 3)
- Evaluate distribution shift, adversarial assumptions, subgroup performance, privacy leakage, and mitigation costs.
- Report who benefits, who bears residual risk, and which fairness or privacy definition was chosen.
Mental model: Trustworthy AI is a negotiated evidence and governance problem, not a single score.
Try: Build a risk register that connects each mitigation to a measurable trade-off.
Outcome: You can make trustworthy-AI claims with explicit scope and accountability.

M11.6 · Capstone: Trustworthy-AI Evaluation — (sessions: 6)
- Audit one model across interpretability, robustness, fairness, privacy, and documentation dimensions.
- Prioritize risks by severity and likelihood, then test whether mitigations improve the stated risk.
- Present unresolved risks plainly to a technical and non-technical audience.
Final result: A trustworthy-AI audit that demonstrates measurement, threat modeling, stakeholder awareness, and responsible communication.
