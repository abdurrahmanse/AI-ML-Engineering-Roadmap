Level 11 · LLMs & Reinforcement Learning

Phase: Scaled Systems & LLMs
Suggested duration: 36 sessions (~9 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 10
Scope: Master reinforcement learning from Bellman equations to PPO, Large Language Model pretraining dynamics, empirical scaling laws, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA), preference alignment (RLHF, DPO, KTO), and the research methodology of studying AI safety and security scientifically.

M11.1 · Reinforcement Learning Foundations: MDPs, Bellman Equations & DQN — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Markov Decision Processes & dynamic programming
- MDP Mathematical Framework: Formulate Markov Decision Processes: state space S, action space A, transition probability P(s'|s, a), reward function R(s, a), discount factor gamma.
- Bellman Equations: Derive the Bellman Expectation Equation and Bellman Optimality Equation for state-value V(s) and action-value Q(s, a) functions.
Session 2 · Q-Learning & Deep Q-Networks (DQN)
- Temporal Difference Learning: Implement tabular Q-Learning and SARSA; analyze off-policy versus on-policy updates.
- Deep Q-Networks (DQN): Deconstruct DQN innovations: non-linear function approximation, Experience Replay to break temporal correlation, and Target Networks to stabilize updates.
Mental model: Reinforcement learning optimizes sequential decision-making under uncertainty by trading immediate rewards against discounted future return.
Try: Implement a Deep Q-Network from scratch in PyTorch with experience replay and a frozen target network, training an agent to solve a benchmark control environment (CartPole).
Outcome: You can derive Bellman equations, implement Deep Q-Networks, and evaluate sample efficiency in sequential decision-making.

M11.2 · Policy Gradients, Actor-Critic & Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Policy gradient theorem & REINFORCE
- Policy Optimization: Formulate parameterized stochastic policies pi_theta(a|s) and derive the Policy Gradient Theorem: grad J(theta) = E[grad log pi_theta(a|s) * Q(s, a)].
- Variance Reduction: Implement the REINFORCE algorithm with state-value baseline subtraction to reduce Monte Carlo gradient variance without introducing bias.
Session 2 · Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C) & Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
- Actor-Critic Architecture: Implement Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), simultaneously learning policy actor pi_theta and value function critic V_phi to compute advantage A(s, a).
- PPO Clipped Objective: Formulate Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), clipping probability ratios r_t(theta) to prevent destructive policy updates while avoiding TRPO constraints.
Mental model: Policy gradients directly climb the expected reward surface; PPO constrains policy updates to a trusted proximal region to guarantee stable monotonic improvement.
Try: Implement PPO from scratch in PyTorch, verify that the clipped surrogate objective prevents catastrophic policy collapse, and evaluate training reward curves.
Outcome: You can derive the Policy Gradient Theorem, implement Actor-Critic algorithms, and configure PPO for continuous and discrete action spaces.

M11.3 · Large Language Models: Pre-Training Dynamics & Scaling Laws — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · LLM architectural blueprints & KV caching
- Modern LLM Anatomy: Deconstruct Llama-3 and Mistral architectures: SwiGLU activation functions, RMSNorm, Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA).
- KV Cache Optimization: Implement Key-Value (KV) caching to eliminate redundant quadratic recomputations during autoregressive token generation.
Session 2 · Empirical scaling laws & compute-optimal training
- Power Law Scaling: Analyze Kaplan et al. empirical scaling laws connecting cross-entropy loss to parameter count N, dataset size D, and compute budget C.
- Chinchilla Scaling: Study Hoffmann et al. (Chinchilla, 2022), proving compute-optimal training requires scaling parameters and tokens equally (D approx 20 * N).
Mental model: Large language models are next-token predictors whose downstream capabilities scale predictably with compute, dataset size, and architectural efficiency.
Try: Build an autoregressive generation engine with KV caching in PyTorch, benchmark generation speedup against non-cached generation, and calculate token latency percentiles.
Outcome: You can implement modern LLM architectural components, configure KV caching, and apply Chinchilla scaling laws to budget training runs.

M11.4 · Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning: PEFT, LoRA & QLoRA — (sessions: 5)
Session 1 · Full fine-tuning versus low-rank adaptation
- Catastrophic Forgetting: Analyze the limitations of full parameter fine-tuning: memory consumption, storage overhead, and degradation of general knowledge.
- Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA): Derive weight update factorizations Delta W = B * A with rank r << d, scaling factor alpha, and target projection matrices (Q, K, V, MLP).
Session 2 · Quantized LoRA (QLoRA) & Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT)
- 4-Bit NormalFloat & QLoRA: Deconstruct QLoRA: NormalFloat4 (NF4) data type, double quantization, and paged optimizers to fine-tune 70B models on consumer GPUs.
- Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training (DAPT): Execute continued pretraining on specialized domain corpora (clinical notes, legal text, cybersecurity code) prior to instruction tuning.
Mental model: LoRA freezes the original foundational knowledge weights and trains lightweight, low-rank adapter matrices that steer model behavior along specific domain manifolds.
Try: Fine-tune an open LLM on a domain dataset using QLoRA in 4-bit precision, track GPU VRAM consumption, and evaluate domain adaptation versus full fine-tuning.
Outcome: You can derive low-rank adaptation, configure QLoRA for memory-efficient training, and execute Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training.

M11.5 · Preference Alignment: RLHF, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) & KTO — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · RLHF: Bradley-Terry reward modeling & PPO
- Human Feedback Modeling: Formalize the Bradley-Terry preference probability model over pairs of responses (y_w > y_l) and train a scalar reward model.
- PPO Alignment Loop: Align the policy using PPO against the reward model with a KL divergence penalty to prevent policy drift away from the base reference model.
Session 2 · Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) & reference-free alignment
- DPO Mathematical Derivation: Derive the closed-form implicit reward formulation (Rafailov et al., 2023), eliminating the reward model and optimizing policy weights directly.
- Alternative Alignment Objectives: Evaluate Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO, prospect theory on un-paired data), Identity Preference Optimization (IPO), and ORPO.
- Test-Time Compute & Reasoning: Analyze Chain-of-Thought (CoT), self-consistency majority voting, and test-time compute scaling for mathematical and logical reasoning.
Mental model: Preference alignment shifts language models from raw text completers into helpful, honest, and harmless assistants through reward-guided policy optimization.
Try: Align a small language model using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on an instruction-preference dataset, measuring win rate against the unaligned baseline.
Outcome: You can derive and execute Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), configure RLHF with PPO, and evaluate preference alignment outcomes.

M11.6 · Capstone: Reinforcement Learning & Open LLM Alignment Benchmark — (sessions: 6)
Session 1 · End-to-end domain adaptation & preference alignment pipeline
- Select an open foundation model, perform Domain-Adaptive Pre-Training using QLoRA on a specialized research corpus, and conduct instruction fine-tuning (SFT).
- Align the fine-tuned model using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on a curated preference dataset, logging implicit reward margins and KL divergence metrics.
Session 2 · Multi-dimensional evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge & paper report
- Benchmark the aligned model against base and SFT models across domain accuracy, instruction following, and safety, using automated LLM-as-a-judge with position debiasing.
- Author a 6-page empirical research paper in LaTeX documenting training recipes, compute requirements, reward dynamics, and comparative benchmark results.
Final result: A fully adapted and preference-aligned domain LLM, accompanied by a reproducible training pipeline and a publication-quality evaluation paper.

M11.7 · AI Safety & Security Research Methodology — (sessions: 4)
Session 1 · Studying alignment failures and jailbreaking empirically
- Alignment Tax Measurement: Design a controlled experiment measuring the "alignment tax" — the loss in benchmark task performance that results from applying safety fine-tuning (RLHF or DPO) to a base language model — by comparing the base model, SFT model, and aligned model across a comprehensive benchmark suite (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, and domain-specific tasks), measuring the performance delta attributable to alignment fine-tuning alone.
- Jailbreaking Success Rate Measurement: Design a rigorous study measuring jailbreak attack success rates on a fixed open language model using a controlled attack corpus: implement GCG (Greedy Coordinate Gradient) suffix optimization attacks, human-written jailbreak templates, and roleplay attacks; measure success rate as the fraction of harmful completions triggered per attack category with 95% confidence intervals; replicate the methodology of Zou et al. (2023) "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models".
- Refusal Mechanism Interpretability: Investigate where in the network refusal behavior originates by ablating the model's safety fine-tuning at the representation level using activation steering: add a "harmless" direction vector to the residual stream and measure how this changes refusal rates, using the methodology of Turner et al. (2023) "Activation Addition: Steering Language Models Without Optimization".
Session 2 · Scalable oversight and AI safety benchmarks
- Scalable Oversight Research Design: Design a study testing a scalable oversight protocol (e.g. debate, amplification, or process reward modeling) on a domain where the verifier (human or model) has limited domain expertise: measure whether the oversight mechanism successfully identifies correct from incorrect answers above chance when the verifier cannot directly evaluate the answer quality.
- Safety Benchmark Design Methodology: Critically evaluate existing AI safety benchmarks (TruthfulQA, BBQ Bias, WinoBias, BOLD, HarmBench) for construct validity — does the benchmark measure what it claims to measure? — by testing whether human annotators agree with the benchmark's notion of "harmful" or "biased" on a 100-question sample; identify cases where the benchmark's scoring function disagrees with human judgment.
- Red Team Research Protocol: Plan a structured red team research study as a scientific contribution: define the threat model, pre-register the attack taxonomy, pre-specify the success rate threshold that would constitute a meaningful finding, and design the disclosure protocol before running any experiments.
Mental model: AI safety research requires the same scientific discipline as any other empirical field — pre-registered hypotheses, controlled experiments, confidence intervals, and honest reporting of negative results — rather than the ad-hoc "I found a jailbreak" anecdote that currently dominates the field.
Try: Design and execute a measurement study on jailbreaking resistance of an open aligned model (e.g. Llama-3-8B-Instruct): construct a stratified attack corpus of 60 prompts covering 3 attack categories (direct harmful requests, roleplay-wrapped requests, and indirect goal completion), measure refusal rate per category with 95% confidence intervals, and test whether one defensive system prompt significantly improves refusal rate using McNemar's test.
Outcome: You can design controlled studies measuring alignment tax, jailbreak success rates, and scalable oversight effectiveness; critically evaluate AI safety benchmarks for construct validity; and plan pre-registered red team studies that meet scientific publication standards.
