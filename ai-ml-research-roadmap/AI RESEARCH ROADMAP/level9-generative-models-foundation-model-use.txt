Level 9 · Generative Models and Foundation-Model Use

Phase: Generative Models & Experimental Science
Suggested duration: 24 sessions (~6 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 8
Scope: Understand how generative models are trained, evaluated, adapted, and used responsibly for research rather than treating generated text or images as evidence.

M9.1 · Generative Modeling Principles — (sessions: 5)
- Explain autoregressive, variational, energy-based, diffusion, and flow-matching perspectives.
- Implement a small generative model and inspect likelihood, sampling, conditioning, and failure modes.
- Compare fidelity, diversity, calibration, utility, and memorization rather than relying on visual appeal.
Mental model: Generation learns a distribution; a convincing sample does not prove coverage, truth, or usefulness.
Try: Train a toy diffusion or flow model and evaluate samples with quantitative and qualitative checks.
Outcome: You can explain and evaluate the basic families of generative models.

M9.2 · Pretrained Models, Prompting and Retrieval — (sessions: 5)
- Use model cards, tokenizers, prompts, structured outputs, retrieval, citations, and abstention patterns.
- Evaluate factuality, robustness, contamination, and sensitivity to prompts and context.
- Keep retrieved evidence separate from model-generated claims in an analysis pipeline.
Mental model: A foundation model is a probabilistic component whose outputs require measurement and provenance.
Try: Build a small retrieval-augmented experiment with a citation checker and an abstention test set.
Outcome: You can use pretrained models as research instruments without confusing fluency with truth.

M9.3 · Adaptation and Efficiency — (sessions: 5)
- Compare zero-shot, few-shot, supervised fine-tuning, adapters, LoRA, and prompt-based adaptation.
- Track data provenance, trainable parameters, compute, checkpoints, and evaluation leakage.
- Decide when adaptation is justified over retrieval, better data, or a simpler baseline.
Mental model: Adaptation changes a model's behavior through a data and optimization budget that must be measured.
Try: Adapt a small open model with LoRA and compare it with retrieval and prompting under a fixed budget.
Outcome: You can choose and evaluate an adaptation strategy with transparent trade-offs.

M9.4 · Capstone: Generative System Evaluation — (sessions: 9)
- Define a real use case, threat model, permitted data, and success criteria before implementation.
- Compare a generative baseline, retrieval system, and adapted model with human or domain checks.
- Publish prompts, evaluation examples, failure cases, costs, and known unsafe or unsupported uses.
Final result: A responsible generative-AI research report grounded in evidence rather than demos.
