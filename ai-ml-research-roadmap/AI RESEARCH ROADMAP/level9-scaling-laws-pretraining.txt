Level 9 · Foundation Model Pre-Training & Scaling Laws

Scope: explore the empirical science of large-scale foundation model pre-training: Kaplan versus Chinchilla scaling laws, compute-optimal allocation, synthetic data curation, pre-training loss dynamics, and tokenization frontiers.

Prerequisites: Level 8

M9.1 · Empirical Scaling Laws: Kaplan vs Chinchilla — (sessions: 4)

Understand the concept of empirical scaling laws in deep learning: power-law relationships L = (N_c / N)^alpha_N between loss, compute, data, and parameters.
Analyze Kaplan et al. (OpenAI 2020) scaling laws: power laws for cross-entropy loss as a function of model parameter count N, dataset size D, and training compute budget C.
Examine Kaplan's conclusion favoring scaling parameters over data: allocating compute primarily to model size while training on relatively small datasets.
Master Chinchilla Scaling Laws (Hoffmann et al., DeepMind 2022): identifying the suboptimal learning rate schedule and under-training flaw in Kaplan's methodology.
Analyze Hoffmann's three parametric fitting approaches: minimum loss envelopes, varying parameter count curves, and parametric loss modeling.
Derive the Chinchilla compute-optimal scaling rule: model parameters N and training tokens D should scale in equal proportion: N proportional to C^0.5, D proportional to C^0.5.
Understand Inference-Optimal vs Compute-Optimal Training: why frontier open-source models (Llama series) train on 15T+ tokens (far beyond Chinchilla optimal) to amortize inference serving costs.
Outcome: fit Chinchilla power-law parameters using non-linear least squares optimization over empirical loss checkpoints and compute optimal N and D for a target FLOP budget.

M9.2 · Pre-Training Data Curation, Deduplication & Quality Filtering — (sessions: 4)

Analyze the composition of frontier pre-training datasets (Common Crawl, RefinedWeb, FineWeb, RedPajama, The Stack): web text, books, papers, code, and conversations.
Study the impact of data quality on downstream performance: why filtering out low-quality web text improves model capability more than scaling model size by 2x.
Master Quality Filtering Algorithms: fastText heuristic classifiers trained on curated high-quality text (Wikipedia, textbooks) versus raw web scrapes.
Analyze Line-Level and Document-Level Heuristics: filtering based on word length distribution, punctuation ratios, repetition patterns, stop-word frequency, and perplexity filtering.
Master Text Deduplication: MinHash Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH), Jaccard similarity thresholds, and suffix array exact substring deduplication (Lee et al.).
Analyze the mathematical consequences of duplicate data: memorization risks, double-counting gradient updates, and loss of model generalization.
Examine Domain Mixing and Data Curricula: balancing web text, code, mathematics, and multilingual data, and scheduling high-quality tokens toward the end of training.
Outcome: implement a MinHash LSH deduplication pipeline and train a fastText quality filtering model to process and score raw web text.

M9.3 · Synthetic Data Generation & Quality Filtering Science — (sessions: 3)

Understand the impending web data exhaustion wall (Villalobos et al.) and the strategic role of synthetic data in foundation model training.
Analyze Synthetic Data Methodologies: self-instruct, evol-instruct (WizardLM), textbook generation (Textbooks Are All You Need, Phi series), and synthetic reasoning chains.
Study Synthetic Data Failure Modes: model collapse (Shumailov et al.), where training models iteratively on recursive generations leads to loss of rare tail knowledge and degenerative entropy collapse.
Examine Quality Filtering for Synthetic Data: using execution-based verifiers (unit tests for code, formal solvers for math), model judges, and diversity clustering.
Analyze Instruction-Backtranslation: using models to generate prompts for high-quality unlabelled web documents to produce synthetic instruction-response pairs at scale.
Outcome: design a synthetic question-answer generation pipeline with execution verification that avoids synthetic repetition collapse.

M9.4 · Tokenization Frontiers & Vocabulary Science — (sessions: 3)

Understand why tokenization is a critical inductive bias affecting arithmetic, code, multilingual performance, and safety.
Analyze Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE, Sennrich et al.): greedy frequency-based subword vocabulary construction and merge rules.
Analyze WordPiece (Schuster & Nakajima) and Unigram Language Model (Kudo, SentencePiece): probabilistic vocabulary pruning via language model likelihood reduction.
Examine Byte-Level Tokenization (BBPE): fallback to raw bytes to eliminate out-of-vocabulary (OOV) tokens completely.
Study Tokenization Failure Modes: digit tokenization causing arithmetic inability (tokenizing "1234" as single tokens vs individual digits), whitespace sensitivity, and prompt injection attacks in token space.
Explore Token-Free Architectures: character-level and byte-level foundation models (Megabyte, ByT5) and their trade-offs in context length and computational FLOPs.
Outcome: train a custom BPE tokenizer from raw text, inspect its merge table, and compare compression ratios across English, non-Latin scripts, and code.

M9.5 · Pre-Training Dynamics, Loss Spikes & Numerical Stability — (sessions: 3)

Analyze the loss curve trajectory during large-scale pre-training: power-law descent, plateauing, and learning rate decay phases.
Study Loss Spikes in Large-Scale Training: sudden divergence in loss accompanied by gradient norm explosion, and methods for mitigation.
Analyze the root causes of loss spikes: batch composition anomalies, attention logit growth, outlier features in hidden states, and FP16 underflow/overflow.
Master Numerical Stability Guards: QK-Norm (applying LayerNorm to queries and keys before dot-product attention, Dehghani et al.), z-loss regularization, and gradient clipping.
Understand Checkpointing and Rollback Strategies: automated anomaly detection, checkpoint frequency, saving optimizer states, and rolling back with modified data shuffle seeds.
Study Learning Rate Warmup and Decay: why linear warmup prevents destructive updates in randomly initialized representations, and comparing cosine vs WSD (Warmup-Stable-Decay) schedules.
Outcome: implement QK-Norm and z-loss regularization in a Transformer architecture and demonstrate suppression of attention logit explosion during training.

M9.6 · Capstone: Compute-Optimal Pre-Training Experiment and Scaling Law Curve Fitting — (sessions: 2)

Design an empirical pre-training experiment to fit Kaplan and Chinchilla scaling laws across a micro-cluster.
Construct a cleaned, deduplicated, and filtered training corpus with balanced domain mixing (code, mathematics, general prose).
Train a family of 5 decoder-only Transformer models spanning parameters from 10M to 150M across varied token allocations (10M to 1B tokens).
Log training loss, validation perplexity, gradient norms, and cumulative floating point operations (FLOPs) accurately using theoretical FLOP formulas: 6 * N * D per training step.
Fit parametric scaling law equations: L(N, D) = E + A / N^alpha + B / D^beta using non-linear curve fitting (SciPy optimize curve_fit).
Extract the optimal parameter-to-token ratio and compare empirical findings against Chinchilla predictions (alpha approx 0.34, beta approx 0.28).
Evaluate the downstream zero-shot capability of the models on standard language evaluation benchmarks to test whether loss scaling correlates with task performance.
Draft an academic research paper documenting the pre-training pipeline, dataset preparation, loss spike mitigation strategies, and scaling law exponent derivations.
Final result: a reproducible pre-training codebase, fitted scaling law power-law parameters, and academic paper analyzing empirical loss scaling dynamics.

Level 9 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Derive the theoretical floating point operations (FLOPs) required for forward and backward passes of a Transformer: 6 * N * D.
Explain the mathematical difference between Kaplan and Chinchilla scaling laws.
Derive the compute-optimal parameter-to-token allocation under a fixed compute budget constraint.
Explain why modern open-weight models are intentionally trained far past the Chinchilla compute-optimal frontier.
Implement MinHash and Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) for large-scale text deduplication.
Train fastText quality classifiers to score web-scraped documents against high-quality reference corpora.
Explain the phenomenon of model collapse when recursively training on synthetic data.
Implement Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) from scratch and inspect the vocabulary merge tree.
Identify tokenization failure modes affecting arithmetic, code indentation, and multilingual representations.
Analyze the structural causes of pre-training loss spikes and attention logit growth.
Implement QK-Norm and z-loss to ensure numerical stability during multi-billion parameter pre-training.
Formulate and execute a multi-model scaling law experiment and fit power-law coefficients.
Explain the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) learning rate schedule and its advantages over standard cosine decay.
Progression: Scaling Laws & Chinchilla Optimality → Pre-Training Data Curation & Deduplication → Synthetic Data & Model Collapse → Tokenization Frontiers → Pre-Training Stability & Loss Spikes → Scaling Law Curve Fitting Capstone.
