Level 2 · Research Mathematics & Statistical Hypothesis Testing

Phase: Foundations & Methodology
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 1 (Literature Review & Scientific Problem Formulation)
Scope: Master the core mathematical tools and statistical hypothesis testing methodologies required to derive machine learning models, prove convergence properties, and rigorously validate empirical claims in academic papers.

M2.1 · Applied Linear Algebra: High-Dimensional Geometry & Spectral Methods — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · High-Dimensional Geometry & Vector Subspaces
- Analyze geometric phenomena in high-dimensional vector spaces: the curse of dimensionality, concentration of measure, and orthogonality of random vectors.
- Compute orthogonal projections onto subspaces and derive projection matrices for dimensionality reduction and subspace clustering.
- Understand Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, QR decomposition, and their applications in stable numerical computations.
Session 2 · Spectral Decompositions & Energy Concentration
- Compute Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and eigendecompositions of symmetric positive semi-definite kernel and covariance matrices.
- Apply the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem to determine optimal low-rank matrix approximations and bound reconstruction error.
- Analyze matrix condition numbers and spectral norms to predict numerical instability in deep linear layers and attention projections.
Mental model: High-dimensional representations concentrate on low-dimensional manifolds; spectral decompositions identify the principal axes of information.
Try: Implement truncated SVD from scratch in NumPy, decompose a high-dimensional feature matrix, and compute the reconstruction error against theoretical bounds.
Outcome: Manipulate high-dimensional vector representations, analyze spectral properties of neural representations, and avoid numerical ill-conditioning.

M2.2 · Matrix Calculus & Symbolic Derivations for Gradient Flow — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Matrix Differentials & Tensor Contractions
- Master numerator and denominator layout conventions in matrix calculus for vector-valued and matrix-valued functions.
- Compute analytical gradients and Jacobians of composite matrix expressions (trace, determinant, quadratic forms, matrix inverses).
- Derive the backpropagation equations for standard neural network layers (linear projections, convolutions, self-attention, layer normalization).
Session 2 · Second-Order Curvature & Hessian Analysis
- Compute the Hessian matrix of loss functions and analyze its eigenvalue spectrum to evaluate local curvature and saddle point geometry.
- Understand the Gauss-Newton approximation and Fisher Information Matrix in natural gradient descent and empirical optimization.
- Derive convergence rates for first-order gradient descent on L-smooth and mu-strongly convex objective functions.
Mental model: Backpropagation is symbolic tensor contraction along the edges of a computational directed acyclic graph.
Try: Derive the exact analytical matrix gradient of the multi-head self-attention operation with respect to the Query weight matrix on paper, then verify with autograd.
Outcome: Derive exact mathematical gradients for custom architectural layers and analytically inspect optimization loss curvature.

M2.3 · Probability Spaces, Density Estimation & Information Theory in ML — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Probability Spaces & Continuous Densities
- Formalize probability spaces, random variables, cumulative distribution functions, and multivariate continuous density functions.
- Compute conditional expectations, law of total variance, and covariance matrices for multivariate Gaussian distributions.
- Implement maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) and maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation under Gaussian and Laplace priors.
Session 2 · Information-Theoretic Metrics in Machine Learning
- Calculate Shannon entropy, differential entropy, joint entropy, and conditional entropy for discrete and continuous distributions.
- Compute Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence and Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence between empirical and model distributions.
- Apply mutual information and the Information Bottleneck principle to quantify feature compression in deep neural representations.
Mental model: Information theory measures surprise and uncertainty; learning is compressing the input while preserving mutual information with the target.
Try: Calculate the analytical KL divergence between two multivariate Gaussians, implement the formula, and verify against Monte Carlo sampling estimates.
Outcome: Apply formal probabilistic modeling to machine learning problems and compute information-theoretic metrics across learned representations.

M2.4 · Statistical Hypothesis Testing: Parametric & Non-Parametric Significance in ML — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Null Hypothesis & Parametric Testing
- Formulate null (H0) and alternative (H1) hypotheses to evaluate whether a proposed model genuinely outperforms a baseline.
- Understand Type I (false positive) and Type II (false negative) errors, significance level alpha, and statistical power (1 - beta).
- Apply two-sample Student’s t-tests and paired t-tests when assumptions of normality and equal variance are verified.
Session 2 · Non-Parametric Tests for Machine Learning Benchmarks
- Identify why deep learning benchmark metrics often violate normality assumptions, necessitating non-parametric statistical tests.
- Execute the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired performance comparisons across multiple test benchmark datasets.
- Apply the Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis test for comparing multi-model distributions without assuming Gaussian distributions.
Mental model: A higher benchmark score without a statistical significance test is an unverified anecdote, not a scientific finding.
Try: Run 10 random seeds for a proposed model and a baseline, test for normality using Shapiro-Wilk, and execute a Wilcoxon signed-rank test to compute the p-value.
Outcome: Formulate rigorous statistical hypothesis tests and select appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests for machine learning evaluations.

M2.5 · Effect Sizes, Multiple Hypothesis Corrections & Bootstrap Confidence Intervals — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Effect Size Quantification & Multiple Testing Corrections
- Differentiate between statistical significance (p < 0.05) and practical significance by computing Cohen’s d and Cliff’s delta effect sizes.
- Control the Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) across multiple benchmark comparisons using the Bonferroni correction and Holm-Bonferroni method.
- Control the False Discovery Rate (FDR) in large-scale metric screening using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure.
Session 2 · Bootstrap Resampling & Confidence Intervals
- Apply non-parametric bootstrap resampling to compute 95% confidence intervals for complex metrics (F1-score, BLEU, ROUGE, AUC-ROC).
- Understand stratified bootstrapping to preserve class balance across resampled evaluation test sets.
- Report evaluation results using mean, standard deviation, and confidence intervals across at least 5 to 10 independent random seeds.
Mental model: The p-value tells you if an effect exists; the effect size tells you if anyone should care; confidence intervals show your uncertainty.
Try: Implement a bootstrap confidence interval function in Python, resample model predictions 1,000 times, and compute the 95% percentile confidence bounds.
Outcome: Compute effect sizes, correct for multiple hypothesis testing, and report confidence intervals adhering to top-tier empirical standards.

M2.6 · Capstone Project: Statistical Significance & Experimental Power Analysis Suite — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Architecture of the Statistical Evaluation Suite
- Design a standalone scientific evaluation Python package that ingests raw model prediction logs across multiple seeds and benchmark splits.
- Implement normality testing (Shapiro-Wilk, D’Agostino-Pearson) to automatically route data between parametric and non-parametric tests.
- Integrate automated two-sample t-tests, paired t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Mann-Whitney U tests with automated p-value reporting.
Session 2 · Effect Sizes, Power Analysis & Academic Reporting
- Incorporate Cohen’s d, Cliff’s delta, and non-parametric bootstrap 95% confidence interval estimation.
- Implement a statistical power analysis module that calculates the required sample size and seed count to detect a target effect size at 80% power.
- Generate publication-ready LaTeX tables automatically annotating statistically significant improvements with bold markers and p-value asterisks.
Mental model: A rigorous statistical analysis suite transforms noisy empirical experiment logs into defensible, publication-ready scientific evidence.
Try: Run the statistical suite on multi-seed benchmark results comparing two models, producing an annotated LaTeX table with bootstrap confidence intervals.
Outcome: Possess an automated statistical verification toolkit that ensures all future empirical claims meet the highest standards of scientific rigor.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Statistical Significance & Experimental Power Analysis Suite
BRIEF: Build a modular Python library for AI/ML researchers that takes raw prediction logs and metric scores from multiple random seeds, evaluates statistical distributions, runs appropriate significance tests, computes effect sizes and bootstrap confidence intervals, and formats publication-ready LaTeX tables.
Build a statistical suite that:
- Ingests structured JSON/CSV experimental logs containing per-instance predictions and evaluation scores across multiple random seeds and models.
- Performs automated distribution diagnostics (Shapiro-Wilk normality test, Levene's test for equality of variances).
- Automatically selects and executes appropriate tests: paired/unpaired Student’s t-test, Welch’s t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, or Mann-Whitney U test.
- Computes standardized effect sizes including Cohen’s d for parametric data and Cliff’s delta for non-parametric evaluation distributions.
- Applies multiple hypothesis testing corrections including Bonferroni, Holm-Bonferroni, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustments.
- Executes non-parametric stratified bootstrap resampling (1,000+ iterations) to compute 95% percentile and BCa confidence intervals.
- Conducts statistical power analysis estimating the number of experimental runs or test samples required to achieve 80% power at alpha = 0.05.
- Emits formatted LaTeX table source code with mean, standard deviation, confidence intervals, and statistical significance indicators (* p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01).
Deliverables:
- Clean, documented Python package with automated CLI (stat_eval --baseline baseline.json --candidate model.json --alpha 0.05).
- Comprehensive test suite validating statistical calculations against standard R and SciPy reference outputs.
- Example tutorial notebook demonstrating end-to-end statistical validation of a published conference benchmark comparison.
Outcome: You can rigorously validate empirical machine learning results, prove statistical significance, and generate publication-grade experimental tables.
