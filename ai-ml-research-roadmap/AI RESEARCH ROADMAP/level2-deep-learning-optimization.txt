Level 2 · Scholarly Literature Search, Paper Reading & Reference Management

Phase: Research Foundations & RA Onboarding
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 1 (Finding an Advisor, Securing an RA Position & Academic Networking)
Scope: Master the scientific method of discovering, reading, evaluating, and managing scholarly literature in Computer Science and Data Science: navigating major bibliographic databases (ArXiv, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, PubMed, DBLP), understanding journal impact factors and conference rankings (CORE A*), Keshav's 3-pass reading method, Zotero/BibTeX workflows, and constructing Systematic Literature Reviews (SLR).

M2.1 · Scholarly Search Engines & Bibliographic Databases: ArXiv, IEEE, ACM & PubMed — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Bibliographic Databases & Search Syntax
- Query major computer science and interdisciplinary scientific databases: ArXiv, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, DBLP, and ScienceDirect.
- Navigate life science and biomedical databases (PubMed, NCBI, BioMed Central) for medical AI research, using MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) terms.
- Construct advanced boolean search strings combining title, abstract, keyword wildcards, and author institutional affiliations.
Session 2 · Citation Networks & Academic Graph Traversal
- Traverse citation graphs forward and backward using Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, and Connected Papers to uncover seminal ancestral works.
- Track influential citations and methodology evolutions using citation velocity, co-citation clustering, and paper genealogy trees.
- Set up automated research feeds via ArXiv sanity preserver, Hugging Face Daily Papers, and keyword alert webhooks.
Mental model: The academic literature is a sprawling directed graph; following incoming and outgoing citation edges reveals how paradigms form and diverge.
Try: Construct an advanced boolean search query on IEEE Xplore and PubMed for deep learning in medical image segmentation, mapping the top 10 most cited papers.
Outcome: Systematically discover scholarly literature across major computer science and domain-specific bibliographic databases.

M2.2 · Deconstructing Journals, Conferences & Venue Rankings: IEEE, ACM, Springer & Nature — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · The Conference-Driven Nature of Computer Science
- Understand why Computer Science prioritizes peer-reviewed conference proceedings (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, KDD, CVPR, ACL) over traditional academic journals.
- Evaluate conference prestige and selectivity using CORE rankings (CORE A*, A, B), acceptance rates (typically 15-25%), and Google Scholar h5-indices.
- Analyze specialized domain conferences: IEEE S&P / USENIX Security for cybersecurity, MICCAI for medical imaging, and CHIL for clinical ML.
Session 2 · Academic Journals, Impact Factors & Peer-Review Standards
- Navigate premier AI/ML journals: Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR), IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI), IEEE TIFS.
- Analyze high-impact multidisciplinary journals: Nature, Nature Machine Intelligence, Science, and The Lancet Digital Health.
- Understand journal indexing metrics: Journal Citation Reports (JCR), Clarivate Impact Factors, CiteScore, and SCImago Journal Rank (Q1 to Q4 quartiles).
Mental model: Top conferences deliver fast, cutting-edge breakthroughs; prestigious journals publish comprehensive, foundational reference works.
Try: Identify the top 3 conferences and top 3 Q1 journals in your target research domain (e.g., medical AI or cybersecurity), noting their h5-index and review cycles.
Outcome: Navigate the academic publishing ecosystem, evaluate venue prestige, and distinguish predatory publishers from reputable venues.

M2.3 · Critical Paper Reading: The Three-Pass Method & Dissecting Scientific Claims — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Efficient Multi-Pass Reading Strategy
- Apply Keshav’s Three-Pass Method to evaluate paper relevance, understand main mechanisms, and dissect deep mathematical assumptions in structured tiers.
- Pass 1 (10 minutes): analyze title, abstract, section headings, and conclusions to determine if the paper is worth reading for your research question.
- Pass 2 (1 hour): grasp the paper's contents, analyze key figures and tables, and identify the core algorithmic contribution without getting bogged down in proofs.
Session 2 · Deep Technical Dissection & Interrogating Claims
- Pass 3 (multi-hour): mentally re-implement the paper from scratch, identifying implicit assumptions, unstated tricks, and missing baselines.
- Differentiate between rigorous theoretical proofs, validated empirical findings, and unsupported speculative claims.
- Identify common research red flags: cherry-picked qualitative examples, weak or un-tuned baselines, shifting metrics, and missing ablation tables.
Mental model: Reading an academic paper is not passive consumption; it is an active peer review where you cross-examine every claim against the empirical data.
Try: Execute a 3-pass reading of a recent top-tier conference paper, writing a 1-page structured critique evaluating its core claims and baseline fairness.
Outcome: Read research papers efficiently, extract core insights rapidly, and critically evaluate the validity of scientific claims.

M2.4 · Reference Management & BibTeX Workflows: Zotero, Mendeley & Overleaf — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Systematic Reference Library Architecture
- Organize academic PDF libraries systematically using Zotero with automatic metadata extraction, collection hierarchies, and tag systems.
- Configure Better BibTeX for Zotero to generate clean, collision-free, standardized citation keys (e.g., [Author][Year][Keyword]).
- Annotate PDFs directly with structured notes, linking research methodologies, dataset benchmarks, and mathematical formulations.
Session 2 · Integrating References with Overleaf & LaTeX
- Integrate Zotero with Overleaf and local LaTeX environments, enabling real-time synchronization of shared .bib reference files across lab members.
- Master BibTeX entry types: @article for journals, @inproceedings for conferences, @book for textbooks, and @misc for preprints.
- Maintain clean citation records: verify that ArXiv preprints are updated with official conference/journal publication citations once accepted.
Mental model: A disorganized researcher forgets 80% of what they read; an automated Zotero library turns reading into a searchable intellectual database.
Try: Build a 30-paper library in Zotero with Better BibTeX keys, sync it to an Overleaf document, and cite three papers with formatted \cite{} commands.
Outcome: Manage thousands of scholarly references effortlessly, maintain pristine BibTeX registries, and streamline academic LaTeX authoring.

M2.5 · Literature Synthesis, Gap Identification & Research Taxonomies — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Constructing Comparative Literature Synthesis Matrices
- Build multi-dimensional literature synthesis matrices mapping prior papers across problem formulation, datasets, architectures, and performance.
- Develop conceptual taxonomies that categorize existing approaches into clear methodological families with contrasting trade-offs.
- Identify contradictory findings in published literature, isolating variations in dataset preprocessing or hyperparameter tuning as potential causes.
Session 2 · Isolating Genuine Research Gaps & Novelty Framing
- Identify fertile research gaps: unaddressed failure modes, untested domain transfers (e.g., applying a vision technique to medical pathology), or scaling limits.
- Formulate a clear "Delta Statement": articulating precisely how your proposed research extends, challenges, or unifies prior literature.
- Write compelling "Related Work" sections that tell a structured historical story rather than providing a disjointed laundry list of paper summaries.
Mental model: A literature survey is not a summary of what has been done; it is a map that highlights the unexplored territory waiting for your paper.
Try: Create a comparative synthesis matrix of 10 papers in a subfield, identify an unaddressed limitation, and write a 3-paragraph Related Work narrative.
Outcome: Synthesize complex literature into comparative taxonomies, isolate significant research gaps, and frame scientific novelty compellingly.

M2.6 · Capstone Project: Systematic Literature Survey (SLR) & PRISMA Flow Diagram — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Protocol Formulation & Systematic Filtering
- Formulate a formal Systematic Literature Review (SLR) protocol following the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews) guidelines.
- Define explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria, boolean search strings across 3 major databases, and screening procedures.
- Execute the search across databases, document raw results, remove duplicates, and record exclusion reasons across abstract and full-text screening.
Session 2 · Synthesis, Taxonomy & Survey Paper Manuscript
- Construct a comprehensive comparative taxonomy matrix evaluating at least 25 screened papers across domain datasets, models, metrics, and limitations.
- Generate a formal PRISMA 2020 flow diagram illustrating the identification, screening, eligibility, and inclusion stages.
- Author an academic Survey Paper manuscript in LaTeX (NeurIPS/IEEE format) detailing the state of the art, open challenges, and future research directions.
Mental model: A systematic literature review establishes you as an authoritative domain expert; it is often the first major publication of an MSc or PhD student.
Try: Compile your complete SLR manuscript into a clean LaTeX PDF, complete with a PRISMA flow diagram, comparative taxonomy table, and 30+ BibTeX citations.
Outcome: Conduct and author a publication-grade Systematic Literature Review suitable for journal or conference survey tracks.

CAPSTONE PROJECT: Systematic Literature Survey (SLR) & PRISMA Flow Diagram
BRIEF: Author a publication-grade Systematic Literature Review (SLR) paper in LaTeX following the formal PRISMA guidelines. The review must address an active research question in AI/ML (e.g., deep learning for medical diagnosis or graph neural networks for cybersecurity), evaluate at least 25 peer-reviewed papers, and construct a comprehensive taxonomy matrix.
Build a literature survey that:
- Formulates a clear research question (e.g., "What are the primary failure modes of transformer models in cyber threat detection?").
- Constructs structured boolean search queries across at least three major scholarly databases (e.g., IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, PubMed, ArXiv).
- Defines explicit inclusion criteria (e.g., peer-reviewed CORE A/A* conference or Q1 journal, empirical benchmark evaluated) and exclusion criteria.
- Implements a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram documenting the number of records identified, duplicates removed, abstracts screened, and final studies included.
- Builds a comprehensive comparative synthesis matrix evaluating all included papers across problem formulation, architecture, datasets, metrics, and limits.
- Constructs an original conceptual taxonomy diagram categorizing existing approaches into distinct algorithmic and architectural families.
- Synthesizes key open research gaps, emerging trends, and unaddressed challenges in the domain.
- Authors an 8-page academic Survey Paper in LaTeX following the standard IEEE Transactions or ACM Computing Surveys template with BibTeX citations.
Deliverables:
- Formally compiled 8-page Systematic Literature Review PDF in LaTeX.
- Vector graphics file of the PRISMA Flow Diagram and the conceptual taxonomy hierarchy.
- Complete Zotero/BibTeX library (.bib) containing cleanly formatted citations for all referenced works.
Outcome: You can systematically discover, critically analyze, and synthesize scientific literature to produce publication-grade survey papers and establish research authority.
