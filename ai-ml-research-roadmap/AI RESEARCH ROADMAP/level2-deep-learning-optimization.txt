Level 2 · Deep Learning Theory & Optimization Dynamics

Scope: examine the mathematical mechanics of deep neural networks: expressivity and universal approximation theorems, loss surface geometry, non-convex optimization dynamics, adaptive optimizers, normalization layers, and implicit regularization.

Prerequisites: Level 1

M2.1 · Expressivity & Universal Approximation Theory — (sessions: 3)

Study the Universal Approximation Theorem for single-hidden-layer networks: Cybenko's theorem for sigmoidal activations and Hornik's theorem for arbitrary continuous activations.
Analyze width versus depth trade-offs: exponential expressivity of deep architectures in separating complex topological manifolds.
Understand linear regions of deep ReLU networks: counting maximal numbers of linear regions as a function of depth, width, and input dimension.
Investigate the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime: infinite-width limits, linear dynamics in function space, and kernel convergence.
Analyze inductive bias of convolutional architectures: translation equivariance, weight sharing, locality, and sample complexity advantages on spatial data.
Examine expressivity limitations: parity problems, graph isomorphism testing limitations (Weisfeiler-Lehman hierarchy in GNNs), and depth lower bounds.
Outcome: derive proofs for single-layer universal approximation and compute exact bounds on linear regions created by deep ReLU feedforward networks.

M2.2 · Loss Landscapes & Non-Convex Optimization — (sessions: 4)

Analyze the geometry of deep learning loss surfaces: high dimensionality, non-convexity, saddle points, local minima, and plateaus.
Understand the strict saddle property and escaping saddle points via perturbed stochastic gradient descent (Ge et al.).
Study the landscape geometry of overparameterized networks: mode connectivity, linear mode connectivity, and loss valleys.
Analyze Hessian spectrum dynamics: dominant eigenvalues, bulk distribution, ill-conditioning, and edge of stability phenomenon.
Understand flat versus sharp minima: trace of the Hessian, spectral radius, Fisher information, and their empirical correlation with test generalization (Keskar et al., Dinh et al.).
Examine Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM): mathematical formulation, minimax perturbation objective, and regularizing loss curvature.
Investigate loss surface visualization techniques: filter-normalized random directions, 1D and 2D contour projections (Li et al.).
Outcome: compute the top eigenvalues of the empirical Hessian along training trajectories and measure loss landscape curvature across different architectural choices.

M2.3 · Stochastic Optimization & Adaptive Learning Dynamics — (sessions: 4)

Analyze Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD): unbiased gradient estimates, gradient variance, mini-batching dynamics, and convergence rates on non-convex objectives.
Study Polyak momentum and Nesterov accelerated gradient in stochastic regimes: dampening oscillations and traversing ravines.
Master adaptive learning rate algorithms: AdaGrad, RMSprop, Adam, and AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter decoupled weight decay proof).
Analyze Adam's failure modes: non-convergence on simple convex objectives (Reddi et al. AMSGrad), hyperparameter sensitivity (beta1, beta2, epsilon), and heavy-tailed noise.
Investigate learning rate schedules: linear warmup, step decay, exponential decay, cosine annealing with warm restarts, and 1cycle policy.
Analyze the stochastic differential equation (SDE) view of SGD: Langevin dynamics, diffusion approximations, and escape from sharp local basins.
Examine gradient clipping: norm clipping vs value clipping, preserving gradient direction, and preventing catastrophic loss spikes during deep training.
Outcome: implement AdamW from scratch with decoupled L2 weight decay and prove that standard L2 regularization in Adam is mathematically non-equivalent to weight decay.

M2.4 · Normalization & Signal Propagation Dynamics — (sessions: 3)

Analyze the internal covariate shift hypothesis and alternative theoretical explanations of normalization effectiveness (Santurkar et al., loss smoothing).
Master Batch Normalization: mathematical formulation, training versus inference running statistics, scale-invariance, and gradient scaling properties.
Understand Layer Normalization: channel-wise normalization, invariance to shift and scaling, and suitability for sequential and autoregressive models.
Analyze RMSNorm (Root Mean Square Normalization): computational efficiency, removing mean centering, and mathematical properties in modern LLMs.
Study weight initialization theory: Xavier/Glorot initialization for symmetric activations, He/Kaiming initialization for ReLU/GELU, and preserving variance across layers.
Analyze dynamical isometry and signal propagation in deep networks: preventing exploding/vanishing gradients at initialization (Pennington et al.).
Examine alternatives to normalization: Fixup initialization, ReZero, and DeepNorm for extremely deep residual architectures.
Outcome: prove gradient variance preservation under Kaiming initialization and implement LayerNorm and RMSNorm with analytical backward pass derivations.

M2.5 · Implicit Regularization & Double Descent — (sessions: 3)

Understand the classical bias-variance trade-off versus modern deep learning observations: overparameterization without explicit regularization.
Analyze implicit regularization: how SGD selects minimum L2-norm solutions in underdetermined linear regression and maximum margin solutions in separable classification.
Study the Double Descent phenomenon (Belkin et al.): classical U-curve, interpolation threshold peak, and the overparameterized modern regime.
Differentiate model-wise double descent, sample-wise double descent, and epoch-wise double descent across training horizons.
Analyze explicit regularization techniques: L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge/weight decay), Dropout (approximate variational inference interpretation), and data augmentation.
Examine label noise and memorization: early stopping, Arpit et al. study on networks learning patterns before memorizing noise, and clean-up phases.
Outcome: reproduce epoch-wise and model-size double descent empirically on controlled synthetic and benchmark datasets.

M2.6 · Capstone: Empirical Investigation of Double Descent and Loss Landscape Curvature — (sessions: 2)

Design an empirical research experiment investigating the relationship between model parameter capacity, loss curvature, and generalization error.
Train a family of deep neural networks varying systematically from underparameterized to heavily overparameterized regimes across controlled label noise levels.
Implement an efficient Power Iteration algorithm to compute the spectral radius (top eigenvalue lambda_max) and trace of the empirical Hessian without forming full N x N Hessian matrices.
Measure loss sharpness around converged solutions using filter-normalized random direction perturbations.
Track training loss, test loss, generalization error, and Hessian sharpness across training epochs to empirically observe double descent peaks.
Evaluate the mitigating effects of explicit regularization (weight decay, dropout) and optimization curvature smoothing (Sharpness-Aware Minimization) on double descent severity.
Generate publication-quality research figures: test error vs parameter count curves, Hessian eigenvalue trajectories, and 2D loss surface contour maps.
Draft an academic research paper reporting findings with rigorous ablation tables and theoretical discussion linking empirical observations to NTK theory.
Final result: a reproducible research experiment and empirical paper dissecting double descent dynamics and loss landscape geometry in deep networks.

Level 2 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

State and prove the Universal Approximation Theorem for single-hidden-layer networks.
Quantify the maximum number of linear regions in deep ReLU architectures.
Explain the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime and its assumptions regarding network width.
Analyze non-convex loss surfaces and characterize saddle points, local minima, and plateaus.
Compute the dominant eigenvalues of the empirical Hessian using power iteration and Hessian-vector products.
Explain the difference between flat and sharp minima and their relationship to generalization.
Derive the AdamW update rules and explain why standard L2 penalty fails in adaptive optimizers.
Analyze the stochastic differential equation (SDE) approximation of stochastic gradient descent.
Derive the variance preservation condition for Kaiming (He) weight initialization.
Implement Layer Normalization and RMSNorm with full analytical backward gradients.
Explain how residual connections and normalization maintain dynamical isometry in deep networks.
Define implicit regularization and explain minimum-norm selection by gradient descent.
Formulate and empirically reproduce the double descent curve across model capacity and training epochs.
Implement Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and analyze its effect on Hessian spectra.
Visualize 2D loss surfaces using filter-normalized random perturbation directions.
Conduct controlled experiments measuring the effects of label noise and overparameterization on test risk.
Progression: Expressivity & Universal Approximation → Loss Landscapes & Hessian Geometry → Adaptive Optimization & AdamW → Normalization & Signal Propagation → Implicit Regularization & Double Descent → Landscape Curvature Capstone.
