Level 6 · Generative Modeling & Probabilistic Machine Learning

Phase: Paradigms & Generative Models
Suggested duration: 13 sessions (~3 weeks)
Prerequisites: Level 5 (Core Modeling Paradigms: Architectures & Representations)
Scope: Master the mathematical formulations and empirical execution of modern generative modeling: Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), continuous-time score-based diffusion models (DDPM, SDEs), Flow Matching on probability manifolds, and rigorous distribution evaluation metrics.

M6.1 · Variational Inference, Latent Variable Models & The ELBO Objective — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Intractable Posteriors & Variational Inference
- Formulate directed latent variable models p(x) = \int p(x|z) p(z) dz and understand the intractability of the true marginal likelihood and posterior.
- Introduce the variational distribution q_phi(z|x) and derive the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) mathematically using Jensen's inequality and KL divergence.
- Deconstruct the ELBO into the reconstruction likelihood term and the latent prior regularization term.
Session 2 · The Reparameterization Trick & Variational Autoencoders
- Analyze why the naive gradient estimator has high variance and derive the reparameterization trick z = mu + sigma * epsilon for backpropagation.
- Implement the standard Variational Autoencoder (VAE) from scratch in PyTorch with Gaussian encoder and decoder networks.
- Address posterior collapse in VAEs using KL annealing schedules, free bits, and vector quantization (VQ-VAE with straight-through estimators).
Mental model: The ELBO trades off accurate reconstruction of empirical data against keeping the latent distribution close to a tractable Gaussian prior.
Try: Implement a VAE on image data, apply the reparameterization trick, and inspect the latent space by generating samples along a 2D latent grid traversal.
Outcome: Derive and implement Variational Autoencoders from first principles and resolve posterior collapse in deep latent variable models.

M6.2 · Score-Based Generative Modeling & Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Forward Diffusion Process & Analytical Transitions
- Define the forward Markovian noising chain q(x_t | x_{t-1}) corrupting data into isotropic Gaussian noise according to variance schedule beta_t.
- Derive the closed-form transition distribution q(x_t | x_0) = N(x_t; sqrt(alpha_bar_t) x_0, (1 - alpha_bar_t) I) for arbitrary timestep t.
- Implement the forward noising loop in PyTorch, generating noisy tensors at arbitrary timesteps without recursive iteration.
Session 2 · The Reverse Denoising Process & The Simplified Loss
- Formulate the reverse denoising process p_theta(x_{t-1} | x_t) parameterized by a deep neural network (U-Net or Diffusion Transformer DiT).
- Derive the variational bound on negative log-likelihood for diffusion models and reduce it to the simplified epsilon-prediction objective L_simple.
- Implement the DDPM training loop, sampling random timesteps, injecting Gaussian noise, and computing the MSE loss between true and predicted noise.
Mental model: Forward diffusion systematically destroys information; reverse diffusion trains a neural network to invert thermodynamic entropy.
Try: Implement the forward noising formula in PyTorch, verify that clean samples degenerate into isotropic Gaussian noise at t=1000, and write the training loss.
Outcome: Derive the forward and reverse DDPM equations analytically and implement end-to-end diffusion training loops.

M6.3 · Continuous-Time Diffusion: Itô Stochastic Differential Equations & ODE Sampling — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Continuous-Time Diffusion Formulations (Song et al.)
- Generalize discrete diffusion timesteps into continuous-time Itô Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs): dx = f(x, t) dt + g(t) dw.
- Classify variance preserving (VP), variance exploding (VE), and sub-VP continuous diffusion processes.
- Formulate the Anderson reverse-time SDE theorem, showing that reversing an SDE requires estimating the score function \nabla_x log p_t(x).
Session 2 · Probability Flow ODEs & Accelerated Sampling
- Derive the deterministic Probability Flow ODE that shares identical marginal probability densities p_t(x) with the continuous SDE.
- Leverage numerical ODE solvers (Euler, Runge-Kutta, DPM-Solver) to achieve high-quality generation in 10-25 steps instead of 1,000 steps.
- Implement Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG): training conditionally with randomly dropped labels and extrapolating along the conditional score vector.
Mental model: The Probability Flow ODE defines a deterministic vector field that smoothly maps random Gaussian noise into the data distribution manifold.
Try: Implement a predictor-corrector sampler and an accelerated ODE solver in PyTorch, comparing sample generation quality and step counts.
Outcome: Master continuous-time SDE/ODE diffusion formulations, implement Classifier-Free Guidance, and accelerate reverse sampling.

M6.4 · Flow Matching & Optimal Transport on Probability Manifolds — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Continuous Normalizing Flows & Flow Matching
- Formulate Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) where a time-dependent vector field v_t(x) generates a continuous probability path from p_0 to p_1.
- Understand the intractability of standard CNF maximum likelihood training and introduce the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective (Lipman et al.).
- Derive the regression loss ||v_theta(x_t, t) - u_t(x | x_1)||^2, proving that marginal vector fields can be learned via conditional flow targets.
Session 2 · Optimal Transport Displacement Interpolation
- Compare standard Brownian diffusion paths with Optimal Transport (OT) displacement paths: straight lines connecting noise p_0 to data p_1.
- Implement Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM): linear trajectories x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1 with constant velocity v_t = x_1 - x_0.
- Analyze the advantages of straight flow trajectories: faster numerical integration, lower curvature, and exceptional sample quality in few steps.
Mental model: Diffusion takes a stochastic random walk through noise; Optimal Transport Flow Matching travels along straight, minimal-energy geodesic lines.
Try: Train a 2D toy Flow Matching model to transform a Gaussian distribution into a two-moons distribution using straight-line OT trajectories.
Outcome: Derive the Conditional Flow Matching objective, implement Optimal Transport probability paths, and train vector field models.

M6.5 · Empirical Evaluation of Generative Distributions: FID, Precision, Recall & Density — (sessions: 2)
Session 1 · Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) & Inception Score (IS)
- Understand the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) metric: extracting deep feature representations and computing the Wasserstein-2 distance between Gaussians.
- Implement the analytical Fréchet distance formula: ||mu_1 - mu_2||^2 + Tr(Sigma_1 + Sigma_2 - 2 (Sigma_1 Sigma_2)^{1/2}).
- Identify FID failure modes: sensitivity to sample count (bias at low N), image resolution, JPEG compression artifacts, and feature extractor biases.
Session 2 · Disentangling Quality vs Diversity: Precision, Recall, Coverage & Density
- Disentangle generative sample fidelity from distribution coverage using Precision and Recall metrics for generative models (Sajjadi et al.).
- Implement Density and Coverage metrics to detect mode dropping, mode collapse, and out-of-distribution hallucinations.
- Evaluate perceptual metrics (LPIPS, CLIP-Score) and compute distribution distance across scientific feature spaces.
Mental model: FID provides a single composite distance score; precision measures how realistic samples are, while recall measures how many modes were covered.
Try: Compute the exact FID between a generated sample set and a validation set using clean-fid, verifying sensitivity to sample size and image resizing.
Outcome: Evaluate generative models with statistical rigor, compute FID/IS accurately, and diagnose mode collapse using precision-recall metrics.

M6.6 · Capstone Project: Continuous Flow Matching Model with Rigorous Manifold Evals — (sessions: 3)
Session 1 · Flow Matching Architecture & Optimal Transport Paths
- Implement a complete continuous Optimal Transport Flow Matching (OT-CFM) model in PyTorch using a modern U-Net or DiT backbone.
- Construct the conditional vector field target generator with straight-line trajectories connecting standard Gaussian noise to data distributions.
- Integrate Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) conditioning to steer the generative vector field toward specified class labels or text embeddings.
Session 2 · Accelerated Integration & Empirical Evaluation
- Implement an adaptive numerical ODE integrator (Runge-Kutta 4th order and Euler) for deterministic sample generation in 20 steps.
- Build an automated evaluation pipeline computing Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Precision, and Recall across 10,000 generated samples.
- Conduct an ablation study comparing straight-line Optimal Transport paths against standard VP diffusion paths, plotting trajectory curvature.
Mental model: Flow Matching represents the modern paradigm of generative modeling, replacing stochastic noise simulation with deterministic vector field regression.
Try: Generate high-fidelity samples in 20 ODE steps using your Flow Matching model, and compute the empirical FID score against a held-out test distribution.
Outcome: Build, train, and scientifically evaluate state-of-the-art continuous generative models using Flow Matching and rigorous distribution metrics.
