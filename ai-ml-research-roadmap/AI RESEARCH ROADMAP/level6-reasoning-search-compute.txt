Level 6 · Reasoning, Planning & Test-Time Compute

Scope: investigate the frontiers of machine reasoning in language and multimodal systems: chain-of-thought mechanics, search over thought spaces, outcome versus process reward models, test-time compute scaling laws, and self-correction dynamics.

Prerequisites: Level 5

M6.1 · Foundations of Machine Reasoning & Chain-of-Thought — (sessions: 3)

Define reasoning in computational systems: deductive, inductive, abductive reasoning, and multi-step symbolic manipulation.
Analyze Chain-of-Thought (CoT, Wei et al.): prompting models to produce intermediate computational tokens before emitting final answers.
Theoretical analysis of CoT expressivity: why allowing models intermediate tokens increases the computational class of constant-depth Transformers (Merrill & Sabharwal).
Examine reasoning capacity versus model scale: emergent reasoning abilities, phase changes, and non-linear accuracy jumps on complex benchmarks.
Study Least-to-Most Prompting and Problem Decomposition: recursive reduction of hard reasoning problems into simpler sub-goals.
Analyze Failure Modes of CoT: unfaithful reasoning (reasoning traces not matching actual underlying model computation), post-hoc rationalization, and error accumulation.
Outcome: derive theoretical circuit complexity proofs demonstrating why chain-of-thought expands the computational capability of fixed-depth transformers.

M6.2 · Search Over Thought Spaces & Tree Search Algorithms — (sessions: 4)

Move beyond linear left-to-right generation: conceptualizing problem-solving as search over combinatorial thought spaces.
Analyze Tree of Thoughts (ToT, Yao et al.): state evaluation, thought generation, and systematic tree exploration via Breadth-First Search (BFS) and Depth-First Search (DFS).
Study Graph of Thoughts (GoT, Besta et al.): modeling reasoning as arbitrary directed acyclic graphs allowing thought combination, branching, and feedback loops.
Integrate Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with language models: thought state evaluation, exploration-exploitation trade-offs (PUCT), and lookahead simulations.
Analyze Search Heuristics: self-evaluation prompts, perplexity scoring, and external symbolic verifiers (Python interpreters, SMT solvers, Lean/Isabelle theorem provers).
Study A* and Best-First Search on reasoning trees: designing admissible heuristic cost functions for language model search navigation.
Outcome: implement a Tree of Thoughts search engine with backtracking and heuristic thought evaluation on complex algorithmic or mathematical puzzles.

M6.3 · Verifiers, Reward Models & Process Supervision — (sessions: 4)

Analyze the fundamental verification versus generation asymmetry: why verifying a proposed solution is often computationally easier than generating it (NP-completeness analogy).
Master Outcome-based Reward Models (ORMs, Cobbe et al.): scoring complete reasoning trajectories based solely on final answer correctness.
Analyze the credit assignment failure in ORMs: penalizing valid intermediate reasoning when a final minor arithmetic mistake occurs, and rewarding false reasoning that stumbles upon correct answers.
Master Process-Supervised Reward Models (PRMs, Lightman et al., Let's Verify Step by Step): scoring every single intermediate reasoning step individually.
Compare PRM versus ORM data collection: human step-level annotation costs vs automated synthetic step-level labeling (e.g. Monte Carlo rollouts from intermediate states).
Formulate Best-of-N (Rejection Sampling) decoding with PRMs: generating N independent reasoning paths and selecting the trajectory that maximizes the cumulative or minimum PRM step score.
Study Majority Voting (Self-Consistency, Wang et al.): sampling multiple temperature-varied reasoning paths and taking the modal final answer.
Outcome: train a Process Reward Model on step-annotated mathematical reasoning data and compare Best-of-N selection accuracy against an Outcome Reward Model.

M6.4 · Test-Time Compute & Inference Scaling Laws — (sessions: 4)

Formulate the Test-Time Compute frontier: trading off training compute, pre-training parameter scale, and test-time evaluation compute.
Analyze Inference-Time Scaling Laws (Snell et al., Brown et al.): empirical relationships between floating point operations expended at inference and final task accuracy.
Study strategies for allocating test-time compute: (1) sampling-based search (Best-of-N, Beam Search), (2) iterative refinement and revision loops, and (3) adaptive compute allocation based on problem difficulty.
Examine Test-Time Compute vs Pre-Training Compute efficiency: determining when expending 10x more test-time tokens outperforms scaling model parameters by 10x.
Analyze System 1 (fast, intuitive autoregressive generation) versus System 2 (slow, deliberative search and verification) cognitive architectures in foundation models.
Examine reasoning models trained with large-scale reinforcement learning over search chains (e.g. DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1 style): self-generated reflection tokens, backtrack tokens, and long-horizon exploration.
Outcome: formulate mathematical scaling curves measuring accuracy improvements as a function of test-time sample budgets and fit Pareto frontiers.

M6.5 · Self-Correction, Reflection & Reasoning Distillation — (sessions: 3)

Analyze Intrinsic Self-Correction: can language models accurately identify and correct their own reasoning errors without external ground-truth feedback? (Huang et al., Kamoi et al.).
Study the limitations of unassisted self-correction: model sycophancy, degradation of initially correct answers under self-critique, and over-confidence in hallucinations.
Examine External-Feedback Self-Correction: incorporating compiler errors, unit test executions, and verifiable formal logic checks into iterative reflection loops (Reflexion, Shinn et al.).
Study Reasoning Distillation: fine-tuning smaller student models on synthetic CoT and MCTS search traces generated by large frontier reasoning models.
Analyze the risk of superficial pattern imitation during distillation: student models mimicking the linguistic style of reasoning without internalizing the underlying search and verification logic.
Outcome: implement an automated reflection loop paired with a Python code execution verifier and measure the empirical rate of successful error correction versus degradation.

M6.6 · Capstone: Train a Process Reward Model and Build a Tree-Search Reasoning Engine — (sessions: 2)

Design an end-to-end research experiment developing a verified reasoning engine for challenging mathematical or competitive programming problems (e.g. GSM8K / MATH / Olympiad problems).
Construct a step-by-step reasoning dataset containing both valid steps and synthetically introduced reasoning errors (divergence points).
Train a Process Reward Model (PRM) by fine-tuning an open-weight foundation model to predict binary validity labels for each intermediate step delimiter.
Implement Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) decoding and Best-of-N rejection sampling guided by the trained PRM step scores.
Conduct an empirical study measuring accuracy against inference compute budgets across: (1) Greedy decoding, (2) Self-Consistency with majority voting (N=1 to N=64), (3) Best-of-N with ORM, and (4) MCTS guided by PRM.
Plot test-time compute scaling curves: accuracy vs total generation tokens and FLOPs expended per problem.
Perform qualitative error analysis categorizing remaining failure modes: deceptive reasoning traces that fool the PRM, early tree search pruning errors, and calculation mistakes.
Draft an academic research paper documenting the methodology, PRM training dynamics, empirical scaling laws, and reproducibility artifacts.
Final result: a verified PRM model checkpoint, MCTS reasoning search engine codebase, and academic paper analyzing test-time compute scaling dynamics.

Level 6 completion outcome

By the end of this level, you should be able to:

Explain how chain-of-thought increases the computational expressive power of constant-depth transformers.
Analyze the difference between faithful reasoning traces and post-hoc rationalizations in LLMs.
Implement the Tree of Thoughts (ToT) search algorithm with state evaluation and backtracking.
Formulate Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) over intermediate reasoning token sequences.
Explain why solution verification exhibits favorable computational complexity compared to solution generation.
Derive the credit assignment problem in Outcome-based Reward Models (ORMs).
Train a Process-Supervised Reward Model (PRM) to score individual intermediate reasoning steps.
Implement Best-of-N rejection sampling and evaluate the accuracy boost over greedy decoding.
Explain the mathematical mechanics of Self-Consistency and modal answer aggregation.
Formulate and fit empirical scaling laws relating test-time compute budget to reasoning accuracy.
Contrast System 1 intuitive autoregressive generation with System 2 deliberative search architectures.
Analyze the empirical limits of intrinsic self-correction without external environmental feedback.
Implement iterative reflection loops coupled with external symbolic verifiers and interpreters.
Distill high-compute reasoning traces into smaller, low-latency student models safely.
Conduct rigorous experiments evaluating reasoning capability across verified benchmark suites.
Progression: Chain-of-Thought Foundations → Search Over Thought Trees & MCTS → Outcome vs Process Reward Models → Test-Time Compute Scaling Laws → Self-Correction & Distillation → PRM & Tree Search Capstone.
